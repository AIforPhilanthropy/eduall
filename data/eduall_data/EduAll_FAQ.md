# EduAll Foundation - Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

---

## General Questions about EduAll Foundation

### Q1: What is EduAll Foundation?
**A:** EduAll Foundation is a Swiss non-profit organization dedicated to providing financial support to international students pursuing higher education in Switzerland. We believe education is a fundamental right and that financial constraints should not prevent talented students from achieving their academic goals.

### Q2: How long has EduAll been operating?
**A:** EduAll Foundation was founded in 2015 and has been supporting international students for over a decade. We have supported more than 1,700 students at Swiss universities.

### Q3: How is EduAll funded?
**A:** We receive funding from:
- Our endowment fund earnings (45%)
- Corporate donations (35%)
- Individual donations (15%)
- Government subsidies (5%)

All funds are used transparently and efficiently, with less than 9% going to administrative costs.

### Q4: Can I contact EduAll before applying?
**A:** Yes. You can send questions through the contact form in the application portal. We reply within five working days, Monday to Friday.

## Eligibility Questions

### Q5: I'm a Swiss citizen. Am I eligible?
**A:** Unfortunately, no. Our grants are specifically for non-Swiss citizens. Swiss citizens have access to different educational support programs through the Swiss government and cantons.

### Q6: I have a permanent residency permit (Permit C). Can I apply?
**A:** Yes, if you meet all other criteria. However, as stated in the Grant Call, Permit C holders who have significant family financial resources are not eligible. Please describe your family's situation clearly in the financial section.

### Q7: I have a Permit F (provisional admission) or a Permit S (protection status). Can I apply?
**A:** Yes, you're eligible if you meet the other criteria. Most international students hold a Permit B for study purposes; holders of Permits C, F, S, L and G may also apply.

### Q8: I'm an asylum seeker. Am I eligible?
**A:** If your asylum procedure is still ongoing (Permit N), you are not yet eligible, because the Grant Call requires a valid residence permit. If you have been granted protection (Permit B as a recognised refugee, Permit F or Permit S), you can apply.

### Q8b: My residence permit application (or renewal) is still pending. Can I apply?
**A:** Yes. Migration offices usually grant a student permit only on proof of sufficient financial means (around CHF 2,600 per month), so some applicants are waiting for a decision precisely because of their financial situation. Explain your situation in the Statement of Declared Circumstances and attach proof of the pending application. Eligibility is assessed case by case, and any award is conditional on the permit being granted.

### Q9: Do I need to speak fluent English to apply?
**A:** No. We accept applications in English, French, German, Italian, Spanish, or Portuguese. You don't need to provide TOEFL or IELTS scores, even if English is not your first language.

### Q10: I'm changing programs/institutions. Does that affect my eligibility?
**A:** Program changes require approval from EduAll. If you're currently receiving a grant and want to change programs, contact your grant coordinator. Some changes may not be funded.

### Q11: I graduated with honors/high GPA. Do I have better chances?
**A:** Academic excellence is not a primary criterion for EduAll grants. We focus on financial need and equal opportunity rather than academic competition. A GPA of 2.5 or more is normally expected. If yours is lower, you can still apply: explain the reasons in your application and what you are doing to improve.

### Q12: What if I have failed courses in my academic history?
**A:** Past academic struggles don't automatically disqualify you. Explain the circumstances in your personal statement. We look for evidence that you're committed to your current studies, not perfection in your past.

## Application Questions

### Q13: When should I apply?
**A:** Applications open June 20, 2026, and close September 1, 2026. Decisions are communicated by June 30, 2027, and funding starts on September 1, 2027. We recommend applying as early as possible—don't wait until the last day in case you encounter technical issues.

### Q14: What if I haven't been accepted to university yet?
**A:** You can apply! Simply submit your acceptance letter as soon as you receive it. You don't need to be enrolled to apply.

### Q15: What's the maximum size of my application?
**A:** Your total application (all files combined) cannot exceed 25 MB. Individual files should not exceed 5 MB each.

### Q16: Can I apply multiple times?
**A:** No, you can submit only one application per grant year. If you make a mistake, contact support to clarify whether you can update your submission.

### Q17: Do I need to notarize or certify my documents?
**A:** Not necessarily. Official documents from institutions (transcripts, acceptance letters) are preferred. Bank statements and personal documents can be unnotarized copies. Contact us if you're unsure.

### Q18: What if I don't have documents in my name?
**A:** If documents are in a different name (for example, due to marriage), provide an explanation and any legal documentation (marriage certificate, legal name change) showing the connection.

### Q19: Can I submit documents in languages other than English/French/German?
**A:** Yes, but certified translations should accompany documents in other languages. The translation should be certified by a qualified translator.

### Q20: Do I need to have my references submit official letters?
**A:** They submit their references through our online portal. The format is flexible—they can write a formal letter or complete our reference form.

## Financial Questions

### Q21: I have family support but it's not enough. Can I still apply?
**A:** Yes! We support students with partial financial need. Explain in your application how much support you have and why it's insufficient.

### Q22: What if my family won't provide financial information?
**A:** Explain the situation. If you have no access to family financial information or support, you can still apply based on your own financial circumstances.

### Q23: Do I need to declare assets like real estate or investments?
**A:** Yes, we ask for disclosure of significant assets. These affect your assessment of financial need.

### Q24: What if my family currency isn't CHF?
**A:** Convert amounts to CHF using the current exchange rate. Indicate what rate you used and the date.

### Q25: How do you define "financial need"?
**A:** We look at the ratio between:
- Your total available resources (personal income, family support, savings)
- Your essential expenses (tuition, rent, food, insurance)

If your resources fall short of reasonable expenses, you have demonstrable need. The application form calculates your current monthly financial need as monthly expenses minus family support and personal income. This is a monthly figure at the time you apply: it is not a yearly amount, and it is not the total you need for the rest of your studies. Other scholarships and savings are listed separately. When setting the award, the committee considers how long you still need to complete your degree, within the grant period, and takes your other scholarships and savings into account.

### Q26: Do part-time job earnings count as income?
**A:** Yes. Students whose residence permit was issued for study purposes, whether EU/EFTA or non-EU citizens, may work up to 15 hours a week during semesters (full-time during holidays). Holders of Permits C, F and S, and holders of a Permit B issued for family or work reasons, have no such limit. Declare the income you actually receive.

### Q26b: I study part-time. Can I apply?
**A:** Yes. Full-time and part-time students are eligible on the same terms, and the maximum award is the same. Please note that a residence permit issued for study purposes (EU/EFTA or non-EU) requires full-time study; part-time study is possible for holders of Permits C, F or S, or of a Permit B issued for family reunification or employment. The form asks for your current study mode and your planned study mode for 2027/28. Many part-time students work alongside their studies; declare this income. Some plan to reduce their working hours or to change to full-time study in September 2027 in order to finish sooner. If this applies to you, enter your expected personal income for 2027/28 and explain your plan in the Financial Need Statement.

### Q27: Can I include hoped-for income or potential job earnings?
**A:** Only include income you actually receive or have a confirmed contract for. Don't estimate "potential" earnings that aren't guaranteed. The one exception is a planned reduction: if you already know that your income will fall in 2027/28 (for example because you will cut your hours for a placement or change to full-time study), state the expected amount in the form and explain it.

### Q27b: My degree ends before or during the grant period. Can I still apply?
**A:** Awards from the 2026 call cover the 2027/28 academic year (September 2027 - August 2028). If you finish before September 2027, you cannot receive an award from this call, because only study time from September 2027 onwards is funded. If you finish during the grant period (for example in January 2028), your award is pro-rated to the months of study remaining from September 2027.

### Q28: What if my circumstances change after I apply?
**A:** Contact us immediately. If your situation significantly worsens (job loss, family emergency), we may consider updates. If it improves (new scholarship), we need to know.

### Q29: What happens if I receive another scholarship?
**A:** That's good news, but you must tell us. We may reduce our grant to avoid providing more total aid than is appropriate. Don't hide other funding.

### Q30: Can the grant cover my children's or dependents' expenses?
**A:** No, grants are for the student's own expenses only. If you have dependents, you may indicate this as a financial hardship factor, but we have a separate program specifically for students with childcare needs.

## Disbursement & Payment Questions

### Q31: When will I receive my grant money?
**A:** Grants from the 2026 call are disbursed on September 1, 2027, after you sign the grant agreement. Renewed grants for later years are also disbursed in September.

### Q32: How is the money paid to me?
**A:** Typically via bank transfer to your Swiss bank account. If you don't have a bank account, we can arrange alternatives.

### Q33: Can I ask for the money to be paid to my university instead?
**A:** Possibly. Request this in writing, and we'll work with your institution on direct payment to tuition accounts.

### Q34: What if I need money urgently before September 2027?
**A:** Grants from this call cannot be paid before September 2027. In an acute emergency, contact us: our separate emergency support fund can sometimes help with one-off payments.

### Q35: Can I receive the full annual amount as a lump sum?
**A:** Yes. Grants are normally paid in one transfer at the start of the grant period. If you prefer two payments (September and February), request this when you sign the grant agreement.

### Q36: What if I drop out after receiving funds?
**A:** If you withdraw from your program after receiving funds, you may be required to repay unused portions. The specific amount depends on when you withdraw.

### Q37: Do I need to declare the grant in my tax returns?
**A:** This depends on your home country's tax laws and your immigration status in Switzerland. Consult a tax advisor. EduAll will report the grant as required by Swiss law.

## Grant Usage Questions

### Q38: Can I use grant money to visit my family?
**A:** No, international travel for personal reasons is not covered. However, in genuine family emergencies (death, serious illness), you may request exception approval.

### Q39: Can I use grant money for an internship or study abroad semester?
**A:** Not usually, unless it's a required part of your degree program. Request approval in writing if your program includes a mandatory international component.

### Q40: I want to take a language course. Can grant funds cover it?
**A:** If it's part of your degree program requirements, yes. If it's optional or extra, you'd need written approval. Contact your grant coordinator.

### Q41: Can I buy a laptop for my studies?
**A:** Laptops may be covered if essential for your program and if the cost is reasonable. Provide justification and quote. Expensive gaming laptops or unnecessary upgrades typically aren't approved.

### Q42: Can I use the grant to buy a car?
**A:** Generally no. Cars are considered luxury items unless you have documented disability that requires transportation. Public transportation costs are covered.

### Q43: What if I misuse grant funds?
**A:** This is taken seriously. Misuse can result in:
- Immediate grant termination
- Requirement to repay funds
- Ineligibility for future EduAll grants
- Reporting to your university

### Q44: Do I need to keep receipts for my expenses?
**A:** Yes. Keep receipts and documentation for 2 years. We may request evidence of fund usage.

## Renewal & Continuation Questions

### Q45: Do I get automatic renewal if I was a recipient this year?
**A:** No. You must apply for renewal each year. However, your process is simpler than the initial application.

### Q46: What's the deadline to apply for renewal?
**A:** Renewal applications are due May 31 each year for the following academic year.

### Q47: What if I don't meet the GPA requirement one year?
**A:** We understand students sometimes struggle. Contact your grant coordinator. One semester below 2.5 may not result in termination if you have an improvement plan.

### Q48: Can my grant continue if I extend my program?
**A:** Possibly, but program extensions must be approved. Extensions beyond the maximum duration (4 years for bachelor's, 2 for master's, 3 for doctoral studies) typically aren't funded.

### Q49: I'm repeating a year due to health issues. Can I still get a grant?
**A:** Yes, if your situation is legitimate. Provide documentation explaining the delay and your plan to complete your program.

## Termination & Appeal Questions

### Q50: What happens if my grant is terminated?
**A:** You'll receive written notice explaining the reason. You have the right to:
- Request explanation and clarification
- Provide a response
- Appeal the decision
- Request review by independent panel

### Q51: Can I appeal a rejection?
**A:** Yes. You may formally appeal within 30 days of notification. Submit a written appeal with supporting documentation through the application portal.

### Q52: How long does the appeal process take?
**A:** The independent review panel typically makes a decision within 30 days of receiving your appeal.

### Q53: What if I'm required to repay grant funds?
**A:** You'll receive a written notice specifying:
- Amount to be repaid
- Reason for repayment
- Timeline for repayment (typically 30 days)
- Payment instructions

You may arrange a payment plan if full payment is impossible.

### Q54: What happens if I can't repay?
**A:** Contact us immediately. We may offer a payment plan. However, non-payment can result in:
- Legal action
- Collection proceedings
- Ineligibility for future support

## Special Circumstances Questions

### Q55: I have a disability. Is there extra support?
**A:** EduAll provides reasonable accommodations for disabilities. Additionally, we have a separate Disability Support Fund for adaptive equipment and services. Contact us to learn more.

### Q56: I'm a parent/have dependents. Can I still apply?
**A:** Yes! Student parents are welcome. However, childcare expenses aren't covered through the main grant. We have a Student Parent Support Program (separate from main grants) with up to CHF 3,000/year for childcare.

### Q57: I'm experiencing homelessness or housing insecurity. Can you help?
**A:** Contact us urgently. While our grant covers housing costs, if you're currently homeless, we may be able to:
- Refer you to our emergency support fund for a one-off payment
- Connect you with emergency housing resources

### Q58: I have mental health or medical issues. Does this affect my application?
**A:** No. Having health challenges doesn't disqualify you. Our evaluation focuses on financial need and commitment to studies, not health status. Your application is kept confidential.

### Q59: I've experienced trauma or difficult circumstances. Should I mention it?
**A:** Only if you feel comfortable and it's relevant to your financial need or educational commitment. We evaluate resilience as a factor, but you're not required to share personal trauma.

### Q60: I'm LGBTQ+. Will this affect my application?
**A:** Absolutely not. We're committed to non-discrimination. EduAll welcomes and actively supports LGBTQ+ students.

## Technical & Administrative Questions

### Q61: I can't upload my documents. What should I do?
**A:** First, try:
- Different browser (Chrome, Firefox, Safari)
- Checking file size (max 5 MB)
- Using PDF format
- Checking internet connection

If you still have problems, use the help form in the application portal and describe the issue.

### Q62: I forgot my password. How do I reset it?
**A:** Click "Forgot Password?" on the login page. Enter your email address, and you'll receive a reset link.

### Q63: How do I save my application progress?
**A:** The application auto-saves as you fill it out. You can log back in anytime to continue where you left off.

### Q64: Can I edit my application after submitting?
**A:** No, once submitted, applications cannot be edited. If you notice errors, contact us immediately through the application portal.

### Q65: What if I don't receive a confirmation email?
**A:** Check your spam folder. If you still have no email after 24 hours, use the help form in the application portal and give your reference number (if available).

### Q66: My reference didn't submit their letter. What do I do?
**A:** Follow up with your reference directly. They should submit through the portal by the deadline. You can send them a reminder email with the submission link.

## Data & Privacy Questions

### Q67: Is my application information kept confidential?
**A:** Yes. Your application is reviewed only by authorized staff and selection committee members. Information is not shared publicly without your consent.

### Q68: What happens to my data after I'm not a grant recipient?
**A:** We retain essential data for 5 years (for records and auditing purposes), then securely destroy it. Contact information may be kept for alumni purposes if you opt-in.

### Q69: Can I request my personal data?
**A:** Yes. Send a written request to our data protection officer through the application portal, and we'll provide all personal data we hold about you within 30 days.

### Q70: Can I delete my data?
**A:** After your grant period ends, you may request data deletion (except records we're legally required to retain). Submit a written request to our data protection officer.

## Post-Grant Questions

### Q71: What happens after I graduate?
**A:** Congratulations! Once you graduate, you are no longer eligible for grants, but we invite you to:
- Join our alumni network
- Mentor future applicants
- Share your story
- Give back if possible

### Q72: Do I have any obligations after receiving a grant?
**A:** No ongoing obligations. Grants are not loans and don't require you to work for EduAll or donate back. Your only obligation is to use the grant for approved purposes during your studies.

### Q73: Can I recommend EduAll to friends?
**A:** Absolutely! We're glad when recipients share information with other eligible students. However, referrals don't give anyone special advantages in selection.

### Q74: Can I donate to EduAll in the future?
**A:** Of course! Alumni donations are deeply appreciated and help us support future students. Information on how to donate is included in our annual report to alumni.
