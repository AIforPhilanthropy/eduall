## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

My grandmother could not read, but she could predict the rain. She would stand in the yard of our compound in Jos, study the sky and the shape of the clouds over the plateau, and tell us to bring in the washing an hour before the first drop fell. As a child, I thought it was magic. Later I understood it was observation, patient and precise, and I think that is where my love of physics began.

Neither of my parents finished secondary school. My father was a tin miner and my mother traded cloth. I completed a diploma in science laboratory technology and worked for eight years in a university laboratory in Jos, preparing experiments for physics students I secretly envied. In 2011, after violence in our city destroyed our neighbourhood, I came to Switzerland to join my husband. We separated a few years later, and I rebuilt my life alone, learning German and working as a care worker.

For six years I served as chair of an association of African women in Zurich, organising language courses, legal information evenings and a mentoring programme for young women considering vocational training or university. Encouraging others eventually gave me the courage to follow my own advice.

At forty-two I passed the university entrance examination and began physics in February 2025. I have completed seven courses with a GPA of 3.54, results I did not dare to hope for.

I hope to work in atmospheric physics, on weather prediction for regions where farmers still rely on grandmothers who read the sky. Education, for me, is my grandmother's gift, made precise.
