## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

I started the Bachelor in Physics at the University of Bern in February 2026, part-time.

I used to work full-time as an interpreter for Dari and Pashto at the cantonal migration services; now I work about 19 hours a week and earn CHF 2,114. From September 2027 I want to study full-time. Part-time, my bachelor would last until July 2031; full-time, it ends in January 2030. At forty-three, this difference matters. I will keep only occasional assignments, about CHF 230, and CHF 1,834 will be missing every month.

To be honest, I do not have savings, and I have no scholarship yet. The cantonal study grant rejected my application because of my age. My room is also a problem: it is a temporary room through a social housing association, and I must leave it at the end of the year.

A grant from EduAll would make the full-time year possible and let me look for a stable room without panic.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

The first time I saw the night sky in the Swiss mountains, on a trip with my German class near Grindelwald in 2014, I stood in the snow for an hour. In Kabul I had loved the stars, but the city lights and the dust hid most of them. Here I could see the Milky Way. I decided then that one day I would study physics, even if it took me many years. It took me twelve.

I grew up in Kabul. My father was a driver and my mother did not go to school. I finished school in 2001, but in those years there was no possibility for me to go to university. I worked as an interpreter for international organisations for ten years, and I led a small team of translators for a health programme.

In 2013 I had to leave Afghanistan because of threats connected to my work. I applied for asylum in Switzerland and was recognised as a refugee. The first years I lived in asylum centres and then in shared rooms, and I moved seven times. I still do not have a stable home.

I learned German and worked as an interpreter again, for the migration services and hospitals. For eight years I also volunteered as a mentor for newly arrived refugees, and I helped to build an interpreters' network in Bern.

At forty-two I passed the university entrance exam. Now I sit in lectures with students who are twenty. Sometimes it is hard, but I am happy.

For me education is finally the chance to look at the stars not only with wonder, but with understanding.
