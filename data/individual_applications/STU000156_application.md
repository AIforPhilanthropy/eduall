## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

For two years before university, I worked full-time in a warehouse in Crissier, packing orders for an online retailer. I saved almost everything, and I still have CHF 6,278 of those savings today.

When I started my Bachelor's in Biology at the University of Lausanne in September 2025, I chose to study part-time and to keep working at the same warehouse, now about 22 hours a week, mostly early mornings. I earn CHF 2,474. My father, who works as a cook in Lausanne and earns around CHF 4,200, gives me CHF 360 a month. My monthly expenses are CHF 2,279, including CHF 1,266 for a studio and CHF 140 for utilities. I took the studio because I could not find a shared flat quickly and did not want to lose my university place; it was a costly decision.

I will be candid about my studies too. My first year was difficult academically. My GPA is 2.16, and I am at risk. French academic language, especially in chemistry and physics, has been much harder than I expected, and I failed two exams in the first semester. I retook one successfully in the summer.

From September 2027 the laboratory practicals take three afternoons a week, and I will have to cut my shifts to about CHF 1,350 a month. The gap will then be CHF 569 a month. I am applying so that I can keep going, move to cheaper housing, and focus on bringing my results up rather than working more.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

I came to Lausanne at sixteen to join my father, who had been working here for ten years. In Casablanca I had been a good student, but here I was placed in a class for newcomers, and for the first time in my life, I could not follow the lessons. My father had left school at twelve; my mother never went to school. There was nobody at home who could help me with homework in French.

I finished school with an apprenticeship certificate, then worked two years in the warehouse, and prepared the university entrance exam in the evenings. When I was admitted to biology, my father cried.

I want to study biology because of my mother, who has diabetes and lives in Casablanca without regular access to treatment. I want to understand the disease and one day work in research or in health services that reach people like her.

I know my first year has not shown what I can do. I am asking for the chance to prove it.
