A grant from EduAll would allow me to finish my degree without running out of money in my final year.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

In November 2022, I woke up in the Inselspital in Bern, and the doctor told me that I might not walk properly again. A shelf had collapsed on me at the warehouse where I worked. I was twenty-four. I lay there for weeks and I thought: if I cannot work with my body, I must work with my head.

I grew up in Aleppo. My father was an electrical engineer and my mother a teacher. I had just finished school and wanted to study mechanical engineering at the University of Aleppo, but the war made it impossible. I came to Switzerland in 2016, and I have lived here with provisional admission since then. My asylum application was rejected, but a return to Syria is not possible. I learned German, and for five years I worked in warehouses and on delivery vans.

After the accident, I had two operations and a year of rehabilitation. I walk with a stick now, and I have chronic pain in my back and my left leg. Long periods of sitting are difficult, so in lectures I stand at the back sometimes. But in 2025 I finally started my engineering studies, in German. I have passed nine courses so far, and I am proud of that.

On Sundays I volunteer with a group that helps young Syrians in Bern with their German and maths homework. I tell them my story, so they know it is never too late.

I want to become an engineer who designs safer workplaces, so that what happened to me happens to fewer people.
