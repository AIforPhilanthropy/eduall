## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

I will set out my finances in full, because some elements work in my favour and others do not.

My monthly expenses are CHF 2,024. My parents, a school principal and a mathematics teacher in Asyut, send me CHF 180 out of a household income of about CHF 1,030. That is about a sixth of what they earn, and it is only possible because my younger brothers have finished their studies. I earn CHF 303 a month as a physics tutor for gymnasium students. I also have CHF 3,612 in savings, which I brought from my years of work in Cairo. The monthly gap is CHF 1,541, and my savings cover it for now, but they will be exhausted around the end of my first year.

The more pressing problem is housing. My room in Wipkingen, at CHF 1,003, is a sublet that ends in December, and the tenant has told me it will not be renewed. In Zurich, finding a room without Swiss references or a guarantor is extremely difficult, and every option requires a deposit I would have to take from my savings.

I could tutor more hours, and I will try. But ETH's master's courses in physics leave little room, and I do not want to repeat the mistake I made during my bachelor's, when I worked too much and my grades suffered.

An EduAll grant would give me a stable room and the time to finish my master's by July 2028.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

For four years, every Friday, I taught physics to girls in a small community centre in Imbaba, one of Cairo's poorest neighbourhoods. None of them expected to study science. Most of their families expected them to marry at eighteen. On Fridays, we built circuits, dropped eggs from the roof, and measured the speed of falling things. One of those girls is now studying engineering at Cairo University.

I understand those girls because I was one of them. I grew up in Asyut, in Upper Egypt. My parents are teachers, and they believed in education, but our extended family did not believe in it for girls. When I was eighteen, relatives arranged an engagement for me. I refused, and I left for Cairo to study physics with almost no support. For two years, my family barely spoke to me. I paid for my studies by working in a call centre at night.

Today my parents are my strongest supporters. My mother keeps a photo of my ETH admission letter on her phone.

After my bachelor's, I worked for four years as a research assistant at the Egyptian National Research Centre, on solar materials, and taught at the community centre on Fridays. In 2026 I came to ETH for the Master's in Physics. My GPA is 3.42.

I want to work on renewable energy materials, and to return one day to Upper Egypt to teach, so that girls there know that physics is a door, not a wall.
