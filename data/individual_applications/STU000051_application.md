## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

Every Tuesday and Thursday afternoon I tutor mathematics to secondary school students in Neuchâtel, and on Saturdays I work in a bakery. Together this gives me CHF 825 a month. My two older brothers work as seafarers on cruise ships. Their incomes together are around CHF 3,270, and they send me CHF 800 every month, because I am the first in our family who has the chance to study at this level, and they want me to succeed.

My costs are CHF 2,432 a month. The largest part is rent: CHF 1,017 for a studio, plus CHF 140 utilities. My health insurance is CHF 395, because I am older than the age for student insurance plans and I have to take the normal one.

My brothers' contracts are for nine months at a time. Between contracts, they are at home in Aceh without salary, and during those months they cannot send me anything. Last year this happened for almost four months, and I had to borrow from friends to pay rent. It was a very frightening time. I was afraid every day that I would have to go home.

My master's in mathematics runs until July 2028. I am asking EduAll to help me build a small foundation for these periods without income, so that I do not have to leave my studies because of the rhythm of the sea.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

In Banda Aceh, before I came here, I taught mathematics for five years at an Islamic boarding school for girls. Many of my students were orphans. Some of them had lost their parents, like I did, in the tsunami of 2004. I was six. I remember the water, the sound of it, and then the silence. My father and my little sister were never found.

My mother raised my brothers and me alone, selling cakes in front of our house. None of us went to university, except me, many years later, with a scholarship from a local foundation. I studied mathematics at Syiah Kuala University. Mathematics was my safe place. Numbers do not change, they do not disappear. When everything around me was uncertain, a proof was always true.

At the boarding school, I also started, as a volunteer, a small afternoon club where girls could do mathematics puzzles and games. Some of them had never been told that they were good at mathematics. By the end, three of them went to university to study science.

I came to Neuchâtel in February 2026. French is still very difficult for me, and I study every evening with a dictionary. But my first semester went well, GPA 3.46. I discovered statistics, and I want to specialise in it.

My dream is to go back to Aceh and train mathematics teachers, especially for girls' schools. I want the girls of Aceh to know that numbers belong to them too.
