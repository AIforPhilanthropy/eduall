## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

Cassava feeds more than 500 million people in Africa. In my village near Nsukka, it was what we ate almost every day. When I was twelve, a disease went through the fields. The leaves turned yellow and twisted, and the roots were small and bitter. That year my family ate once a day. My father, a farmer, did not know what had happened, and no one came to explain it.

That disease was cassava mosaic virus. Today it is the subject of my master's thesis, in a plant virology group at the University of Zurich. I am studying how resistance genes behave in cassava varieties grown by smallholder farmers.

I am the first in my family to go to university. I studied biology at the University of Nigeria, Nsukka, and worked five years as a research assistant at an agricultural research institute. I co-authored a paper on virus-resistant cassava lines. I also taught farmers in extension programmes how to recognise infected plants.

When a political conflict in our region turned violent in 2021, our family home was burned. We lost everything. I left Nigeria in 2022. My asylum application was rejected, because the violence was not directed at me personally, but I was provisionally admitted, since a return to my region is not considered safe. After a difficult year I was admitted to UZH.

In Zurich I volunteer with a food-sharing association, collecting leftover food from bakeries, and I help coordinate the Nigerian community's welcome group for new students.

After my master's I want to do a PhD on plant disease resistance and then return to Nigeria to work on crop protection for small farmers.

For me, biology is not abstract. It is the difference between eating once a day and three times.
