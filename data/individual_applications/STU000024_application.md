## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

Right now I have CHF 130 a month and I need CHF 2,354. That is a gap of CHF 2,214. I have no savings.

Until June I worked as a care assistant in a nursing home in Köniz, about 12 hours a week. I had to stop, because my clinical placement started in August. It is full-time with rotating shifts on a surgical ward at the Inselspital, the University Hospital of Bern, and the nursing home could not give me shifts that fit. I did not choose this lightly, but I could not do both.

The CHF 130 comes from my uncle in Gaziantep, Turkey. He has a small tailoring shop and his family lives on around CHF 550 a month. He has helped me since I left Syria. My parents cannot send anything. After the earthquake in February 2023 our family house in Aleppo was badly damaged, and my father was injured and has not been able to work properly since. Since then I have also been sending money home when I could, which is why I have nothing saved.

My rent is CHF 1,195 plus CHF 130 for utilities for a small studio. I know this is expensive. I took it in 2023 when I urgently needed a place, and I have been looking for something cheaper since, so far without success.

I will be honest: I have been paying the last two months' rent with a loan from a friend. My placement ends in December and I graduate in July 2028. After that I can work as a nurse, and I already have a job offer from the hospital if I pass. I only need to get through these last four semesters.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

I was thirteen when my school in Aleppo was hit. I was not inside, but my best friend was. I spent the next day in a field hospital, trying to find him. He survived, but I will never forget the nurses in that hospital. They had nothing, no electricity, few medicines, and still they were calm, and they held people's hands.

My parents run a small grocery shop, and neither of them went to university. When the war made school impossible, I went to live with my uncle in Gaziantep and finished my secondary school in Turkey. I volunteered with a Syrian community clinic there, translating for patients and helping with registration.

In 2023, at nineteen, I came to Bern to study nursing. I was lucky to have an admission and a student permit. The first year was very hard. I studied German every evening and worked in a nursing home on weekends, which helped my language more than any course. I have completed 25 courses now, with a GPA of 3.02.

Since 2023 I also volunteer with the Red Cross in Bern as an interpreter for Arabic-speaking patients.

The earthquake in 2023 was the worst time. For three days I did not know if my family was alive. I still went to my exams that week. I passed them. I don't really know how.

After I graduate, I want to work in emergency and surgical nursing. Later, I hope to work with humanitarian organisations in the Middle East. I want to be, for someone else, what those nurses in Aleppo were for me.
