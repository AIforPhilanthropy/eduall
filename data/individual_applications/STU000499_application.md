## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. I am in the second year of my Bachelor's in Materials Science at EPFL, which I will complete in July 2028.

My monthly expenses are CHF 1,526. Rent is CHF 488 for a small flat in a cooperative in Renens, which I share with my daughter, followed by food at CHF 299, health insurance at CHF 295 and EPFL fees at CHF 243. The remainder covers transport, phone and study materials.

My income comes from three sources. I work as a part-time cleaner in offices in the early mornings and earn CHF 822. My mother in Faisalabad sends CHF 150 a month from a household income of about CHF 590. I also receive a scholarship of CHF 6,057 a year from a charitable foundation in Lausanne. Before this scholarship, my monthly shortfall is CHF 554.

My residence permit had to be renewed this summer, now as a student permit, and the renewal is pending. I understand that it can only be approved if I show resources of around CHF 2,600 a month. An EduAll grant would therefore not only close my monthly gap, but also strengthen my permit application, and with it the stability of my daughter's life in Switzerland.

In my last semester I will write my bachelor project in a polymer laboratory. The support would allow me to reduce my cleaning hours and give the project the time it requires.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

My daughter is nine, and she wants to be an astronaut. When she was six, she asked me why I go to university when other mothers go only to work. I told her: because one day I want to make materials for rockets. She has held me to that promise ever since.

I grew up in Faisalabad, the textile city of Pakistan. My father worked a loom in a mill and my mother embroidered at home. Neither completed school. I was married at nineteen and had no chance to study. When my marriage ended, I came to Switzerland in 2019 with my daughter, to live near my cousin, and I began from nothing: no French, no qualifications recognised, no income.

For the first three years I cleaned offices and hotel rooms and studied French in the evenings, with my daughter doing her homework beside me. In 2025 I passed the entrance examination for EPFL. I remember reading the result on my phone in a hotel corridor, holding a bucket, and crying.

The past year and a half has been demanding. I study at night, after my daughter is asleep, and I have completed twelve courses while working and raising her alone. I have learned to use every hour well.

My goal is to work on sustainable textile materials, fibres that do not pollute rivers the way the mills of my childhood did. For me, education is the promise I made to my daughter, and I intend to keep it.
