## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

My mother used to say that a family is like a pot: everyone puts in what they can, and everyone eats. Our pot has always been small, and today it is stretched further than ever.

I am in the second year of my Master's in Computer Science at ETH Zurich. My costs are CHF 2,518 a month. The biggest part is rent: CHF 1,411 for a studio in Altstetten. When I arrived in Zurich I could not find a shared flat that would accept me, and I have been on waiting lists for student housing ever since.

I earn CHF 630 a month as a teaching assistant for first-year programming courses. My uncle in Dubai, a taxi driver, contributes CHF 380 every month, although the whole household lives on roughly CHF 1,560. He does this because my father cannot, and because he promised my mother, years ago, that he would make sure I studied.

Every month I am missing CHF 1,508. My master's thesis, on privacy-preserving machine learning for medical data, needs my full attention in the next months, and I cannot take on more hours without risking it.

I am so close. Four semesters. A grant from EduAll would carry me across the finish line.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

My mother could not go to school. She was born in Afghanistan, fled to Iran as a child, and grew up in Mashhad as a refugee, cleaning other people's houses from the age of ten. When I was born, she decided one thing: her children would go to school, whatever it cost.

It cost a lot. As Afghans in Iran, we were not allowed in regular schools for many years. My mother paid for an informal school run by volunteers in a basement. I learned to read there, on donated books, with fifty other Afghan children. When the rules changed, I entered a regular school, and I was top of my class in mathematics.

I taught myself programming on a second-hand laptop my uncle sent from Dubai. At eighteen I was admitted to ETH Zurich. It felt impossible. It still does, some mornings.

I finished my bachelor at ETH in 2025 and started the master directly. In Zurich I help organise a free coding workshop for young refugees at a community centre, because I know how much one working laptop and one patient person can change.

My mother died in 2023, of an illness that could have been treated if she had had access to proper care. I work on medical data because of her.

I want to build technology that makes healthcare fairer, for people with no papers, no records and no voice. My mother put everything she had into the pot. I want to show her it was worth it.
