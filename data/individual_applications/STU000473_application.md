## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

For three years I was the elected head of the residents' committee of a large housing complex in Alexandria, with around 200 families, and I managed the budget for maintenance and repairs. So I know how to plan money, and I want to explain my budget openly.

My costs are CHF 2,004 per month. Rent is CHF 994, health insurance CHF 260, food CHF 358, fees CHF 142, and the rest transport, phone and materials. My husband works as a freelance interpreter in Basel. He earns about CHF 1,540, because the orders are irregular, and gives CHF 250 for my costs.

Until last year we covered my costs from what we saved when we sold our flat in Alexandria. This money is now finished. So I changed to part-time study and took a job as an administrative assistant in a property management company, about 24 hours a week. I earn CHF 2,687. Now my costs are covered, but I have no reserve and no scholarship.

I also have to be honest about my study results. My GPA is 2.3 and I am on academic probation. Economics in German was much more difficult for me than I expected. In the last semester my results were better, and I have six semesters left. I want to finish, not stop now, after so much effort. A grant from EduAll would let me work fewer hours before my exams and my bachelor thesis, and finish in July 2029.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

I am from Alexandria. I studied English literature there, and after that I stayed at home for many years to care for my parents, who were both ill. In our housing complex I was elected head of the residents' committee. We repaired the water tanks, organised the lifts, and argued with the building company. I discovered that I like organising, numbers, and to make decisions.

In 2020 we came to Switzerland, because my husband's family is in Basel. I decided to study economics, something completely new. I was 35.

The biggest problem was the language. I learned German for two years before university, but the lectures are fast, and the exams are with many texts. Sometimes I understood the economics but I could not write it correct in the time. My grades show this.

Still, I am proud. I am in the fifth semester, I passed 14 courses, and in the student council of the faculty I am the representative of mature students. We organised a quiet study room and a mentoring evening for older students.

After my degree I want to work in the finance of a non-profit organisation.

For me education means that it is never too late, even when it is difficult.
