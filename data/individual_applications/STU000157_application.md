## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

In my first year in Fribourg, in a seminar about reading difficulties, the professor showed a slide with a text that was written "the way a dyslexic child sees it". The other students laughed, surprised. I did not laugh. For me, this was not a simulation. It was my whole school life.

I have dyslexia. I came to Switzerland from Kumasi at eight with my mother; my father, a secondary school teacher, stayed in Ghana. My teachers here thought my problems were only because of the language. Nobody tested me until I was fourteen. Before that, I believed I was stupid. A special education teacher finally recognised it, and she changed my life. She showed me strategies, and she told me I could go to university.

Since I was sixteen I volunteer on Saturdays at a reading club for children in Bulle, and I work in a daycare centre. I love working with children.

I want to become a primary school teacher specialised in children with learning difficulties. I want no child to believe they are stupid, like I did.
