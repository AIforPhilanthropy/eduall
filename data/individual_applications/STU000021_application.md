## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

My parents own two pharmacies in Ruse, and together they earn around CHF 3,270 a month. Since February, when I began my Master's in Biochemistry in Bern, they have sent me CHF 940 every month, which covers my rent of CHF 856, my tuition and everything else I need.

But in April, everything in our family changed. My father was diagnosed with pancreatic cancer. He is in treatment now in Sofia, and the prognosis is not good. My mother is running both pharmacies alone while driving to Sofia three times a week, and we have already talked, as a family, about selling one of them. My father's treatment is only partly covered by the Bulgarian health system, and the private clinic costs are growing every month.

My parents have told me not to worry and to stay in Bern. But I am not a child, and I can see the numbers. I know that the support I receive now takes money that my father's treatment may soon need, and that it may not continue for the whole of my programme, which runs until July 2028.

I am applying to EduAll so that my studies do not become one more weight on my family during the hardest time of our lives. Before starting my master's, I worked for three years as a laboratory technician in Sofia. I am ready to take on work again here, and I am applying for research assistant positions. A grant would give me the stability to look after my studies while my family looks after my father.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

Ruse is a city on the Danube, on the border with Romania. It has beautiful old buildings from the time when it was the most modern city in Bulgaria, and it has emptied out in the last thirty years. Half of my school class now lives abroad. The chemical plant where my grandfather worked is closed. Every summer, when I go home, there are more windows without lights.

I grew up in the back room of my parents' pharmacy, doing my homework between shelves of medicines. I learned the names of drugs before I learned my multiplication tables. My father would explain to me how each medicine worked, what it did inside the body. I think that is where it all started: the feeling that there is a hidden world of molecules deciding whether we are healthy or sick.

I studied molecular biology in Sofia and then worked three years in a research laboratory on antibiotic resistance, where I became the lab coordinator for a team of six technicians. We published one article on resistant Klebsiella strains in Bulgarian hospitals, and I was proud to see my name on it.

In Bern I study biochemistry with a focus on drug development. Now, of course, my interest has a new and painful meaning. Pancreatic cancer is one of the diseases where medicine has advanced the least. When I sit with my father in the hospital, he asks me about my courses. He still explains things to me. He still wants to know how everything works.

I want to do a PhD in cancer biochemistry after my master's. Maybe it will not help my father. But it may help somebody else's father.

For me, education is the most valuable thing my family gave me, more than any money. I want to honour it.
