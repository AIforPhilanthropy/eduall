## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

Right now, on paper, I am not in deficit. I want to say that first, because it is true, and because the reason I am writing is that this will not stay true for long.

I study Biology part-time and work about 15 hours a week, childminding for two families and cleaning a small office. I earn CHF 1,700 a month. For the last six years I have lived with my partner in his flat in Bern-Bethlehem. He earns around CHF 5,000 a month and pays CHF 230 towards my share of the rent. My expenses are CHF 1,598, and I have CHF 2,024 saved.

In June, we decided to separate. It is peaceful, but it means that at the end of December I must move out, and his support will end. I have been looking for a room since July. Everything I can afford is far from the university, and every landlord asks for a deposit of two or three months' rent, which is more than my savings.

In 2027/28 I also have to retake one exam and do the laboratory courses of the third year, and I will have to reduce my work to about CHF 930 a month. My costs will not become smaller; probably bigger. My bachelor ends in January 2030.

I am forty-one. I have started over before. I am not afraid of work. But I cannot work enough hours to pay for a room in Bern alone and still pass my exams. Please help me cross this part.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

I came to Switzerland in 2008 to work as a nanny for a family in Geneva. I was twenty-three. I sent almost all my salary home to my parents in Leyte, who are rice farmers and never finished school, and to pay for my younger brothers' education. Both of them are now engineers. I am very proud of that.

For nine years, I looked after other people's children. I loved them, but I always had a dream of my own. When I was a child, I collected insects in jars and drew them in a notebook. My teacher said I should become a biologist. We laughed, because in our village nobody became anything like that.

In 2013, when typhoon Haiyan destroyed our village, I lost my grandmother and our house. I felt helpless in Geneva. I organised a collection among the Filipino workers in Switzerland, and we sent more than two tons of goods and money. That was the first time I led something big. Afterwards I became coordinator of the Filipino domestic workers' association in Bern, where I still help women who have problems with employers, pay or permits.

In 2025, after years of evening school, I started biology at the University of Bern. It is very hard. My grades are low, GPA 2.54, and I need to retake one exam. I work slowly, and many students are half my age. But I have never missed a class.

I want to work in entomology, maybe in a museum or in a laboratory on pollinators. I still have the notebook with the insect drawings. I keep it on my desk.
