## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

I am a doctoral student in Mathematics at ETH Zurich, now in my third semester. I came here in February 2025 with a full scholarship from the Higher Education Commission of Pakistan, which paid my living costs. In March 2026 the Commission suspended payments to all its overseas scholars because of budget cuts. I have received nothing since. My supervisor has confirmed in writing that there is no position in the group I could move to before 2028, because the group's funding is already committed.

My monthly costs are CHF 2,068. I earn CHF 293 by tutoring bachelor students. My older brother and sister, who are teachers in Rawalpindi and together earn about CHF 800 a month, send me CHF 230. The gap is CHF 1,545 each month, and my savings are gone.

When the scholarship stopped, I could not pay the rent for my studio anymore and had to leave it. Since May I live in a sublet room for CHF 1,167, which ends in December. I don't know yet where I will live after that.

I will be honest: my research has slowed down since March. I spend a lot of time looking for money and housing instead of doing mathematics. The Commission says payments may start again next year, but there is no date. I have also applied for ETH's doctoral hardship support and am waiting for a decision.

EduAll support would bridge this period so I can stay in my programme and continue my research. I will inform you as soon as the Commission restarts payments.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

For six years, every Saturday morning, I taught mathematics for free in a small community centre in Rawalpindi. Girls and boys from families who could not pay for tuition academies came with old notebooks and a lot of fear of numbers. My favourite moment was always when someone understood a proof for the first time and looked up with surprise. That surprise is the reason I do mathematics.

I grew up in Muzaffarabad, in Kashmir. In October 2005, when I was twelve, the earthquake destroyed our town. Our house collapsed. We survived, but my school was gone, and I spent almost two years in a tent camp before I could go back to a real classroom. My parents are both teachers, and even in the camp they organised classes for the children.

I finished my bachelor's and master's in mathematics in Islamabad, and then worked as a lecturer at a public college for seven years. At thirty-one, I got the chance to do a PhD at ETH, in algebraic geometry. I have already published two papers with my supervisor.

I am also volunteering here, in a mathematics circle for refugee teenagers in Zurich, one afternoon every two weeks.

My plan is to finish my doctorate by 2029 and return to Pakistan to teach and build research groups in mathematics at a public university. There are very few in Kashmir.

For me education was always the thing that survived when everything else fell down.
