I am in the second year of my Master's in Mathematics at the University of Zurich, with my thesis due in January 2028.

My monthly expenses are CHF 2,415, including CHF 1,318 for a studio in Wiedikon and CHF 285 for health insurance. My parents, retired teachers in Arequipa with a combined pension of around CHF 1,100, send me CHF 300 a month.

Until this spring, I tutored mathematics part-time. I stopped in March when my father was diagnosed with advanced kidney disease. He now needs dialysis three times a week, and I have travelled to Peru twice this year to help my mother organise his care. My thesis supervisor has been understanding, but I have lost months of work that I must now make up.

My parents have told me they will keep sending what they can. I know that his treatment will soon require everything they have.

The monthly gap is CHF 2,115. A grant from EduAll would allow me to complete my thesis without returning to tutoring in the final months, and without drawing on the money my parents need.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

When I was seven, my father, a mathematics teacher, gave me a puzzle: how many ways can you climb a staircase of ten steps if you can take one or two steps at a time? I spent three days on it. When I finally found the answer, eighty-nine, he told me I had discovered the Fibonacci numbers. I felt as if I had found a hidden door in the world.

I studied mathematics in Arequipa. In 2016, when I was a student, an earthquake destroyed many schools in the region, and for two years children learned in tents and borrowed rooms. With other students I organised a weekend mathematics programme for pupils whose schools had closed, and it continued for five years in community halls. After my degree I taught for eight years, first in public secondary schools and later in a teacher training college.

In 2025, at thirty-one, I came to Zurich to pursue the master's degree I had always postponed. My thesis is in combinatorics, and my first paper, on partition identities, was accepted this year.

After my degree, I want to return to Peru to train mathematics teachers. The hidden door my father showed me should be open to every child.
