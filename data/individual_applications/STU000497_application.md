## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

I have seven semesters left in the Bachelor in Microbiology at University of Basel, which I study part-time. I finish in January 2030.

My costs are CHF 1,760 each month. Rent is CHF 731, food CHF 389, health insurance CHF 260, fees CHF 142, and the rest transport, phone and books.

Since this year I work as an intercultural interpreter for Arabic, for hospitals and schools, about 12 hours a week, and I earn CHF 1,363. My brother in Damascus sends me CHF 60 when he can. His family lives from about CHF 300 a month, so I asked him many times to stop, but he says it is for our mother. Each month CHF 337 is missing.

I want to be honest about my savings of CHF 8,787. This money is from my late mother. Before she died in 2023, she sold her gold and sent it to me for my studies. I use it for the gap. I cannot work more hours: the laboratory courses in microbiology are every afternoon, and I need the evenings to study, because everything takes me double time in German.

A grant from EduAll would help me to finish and to have a small start after my degree.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

My mother was a nurse in Homs for thirty years. She told me always: "The most dangerous things are the ones you cannot see." She meant bacteria. When I was a child she showed me pictures from her old microbiology book, and I thought the bacteria looked like small animals.

My mother died in 2023, from an infection that was not treated in time, because the hospital had no antibiotics. I was already in Basel, preparing for university. I could not travel to the funeral. I continued to study, because this was what she wanted.

I came to Switzerland in 2015 with my husband's family. I studied pharmacy in Syria, but my diploma was not recognised. I learned German for many years, and it is still difficult for me. In exams I often know the answer but I cannot write it fast enough. My GPA is 2.75, and I know it could be higher in my own language.

Since 2019 I help at a Syrian women's association in Basel. We organise an Arabic reading afternoon for children and information evenings about health for women. I translate the medical words.

I want to work in a hospital laboratory, in infection diagnostics. For me education means to see the dangerous things that nobody can see, and to stop them.
