## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

The first time I held a real fossil was in the geology museum of Neuchâtel, in my first month here. It was an ammonite, 180 million years old, from the Jura. I held it in my hand and thought of my father's fields near Kolda, and wondered what stories were under them too. I was nineteen, alone, cold, and very far from home, and I suddenly felt that I was in the right place.

My father studied agronomy in Dakar and went back to farm our family land, and I grew up in the village helping in the fields. When I was twelve, a drought killed almost all our harvest. We ate one meal a day for months. I understood then that everything depends on water and soil. My teacher in Kolda told me about geology. I did not know it was a job.

After school I came to Neuchâtel to study, with the savings of my whole family. I have worked since my first semester, and I have volunteered every summer back home with a project that helps villages find and protect groundwater wells.

My studies have not been easy. I study in French, which I speak, but the sciences at this level were new and difficult for me, and I have had to fight for every exam. But I never stopped.

After my bachelor, I want to work in hydrogeology, finding water for villages in the Sahel. I want no child in Kolda to eat one meal a day because of a drought.
