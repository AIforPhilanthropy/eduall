## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

I am entering the seventh semester of my Bachelor's in Architecture at ETH Zurich, and I am applying to EduAll because my housing, which has made my studies affordable until now, is about to disappear.

For three years I have lived in a temporary-use building in Zurich-West, an old office block rented to students and artists at a low rent while it waits for demolition. My room costs CHF 482 a month. The demolition has now been scheduled for December 2026. On the open market, a room within reach of the ETH campus costs at least twice as much, and I will need a deposit.

My monthly expenses are CHF 1,538. I earn CHF 656 a month as a part-time model-builder for an architecture office in Kreis 4. My cousin in Lahore, who runs a construction supply business with a household income of around CHF 850, sends me CHF 250 each month.

The last semester is also the most expensive. My bachelor's thesis project requires physical models, printing and material samples, and the studio schedule makes it impossible to work more hours.

A grant from EduAll would allow me to keep stable accommodation and finish my degree without compromising my final project.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

Architecture matters because buildings outlive the people who design them, and they shape the lives of people who never chose them. I grew up in a one-room house in the old city of Lahore, with nine family members, one window and a courtyard we shared with four other families. That courtyard was everything: our kitchen, playground, meeting room. I have been thinking about courtyards ever since.

My father was a tailor and my mother never learned to read. I left school at fourteen to work in a construction crew, carrying bricks. At nineteen I became a site foreman, and over the next years I built houses, schools and a small health centre in Punjab. I learned how buildings are made, but I always had to follow drawings made by others, often badly adapted to the heat, the families and the way people really live.

I finished secondary school in evening classes at twenty-one. In 2023, at twenty-three, I was admitted to ETH Zurich. The first year was the hardest of my life: new language, new mathematics, and classmates who had never held a brick. I have completed 21 courses with a GPA of 3.26.

My thesis project is a design for affordable courtyard housing for low-income families in Lahore, using passive cooling and local brick. It is, in a way, a design for the house I grew up in.

After graduation, I want to complete a master's and then work on climate-adapted social housing in South Asia.
