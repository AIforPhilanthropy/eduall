## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

My doctoral research at EPFL concerns low-cost water treatment membranes, and my long-term goal is to see them manufactured in West Africa. The financial position I describe here is what stands between that goal and the next three years of work.

Unlike most doctoral candidates at EPFL, I am not employed as a doctoral assistant. I was admitted in 2025 on the basis of a project I proposed myself, with the agreement that my supervisor would seek dedicated funding for it. A first application to a national funding agency was not successful, and a resubmission is under review, with a decision expected in 2028. Until then, I am enrolled as a self-funded candidate.

My monthly expenses are CHF 2,472. Rent is CHF 1,255 for a studio in Renens, health insurance CHF 455, food CHF 334 and utilities CHF 140; the remainder covers transport, phone, materials and fees. Against this, I receive CHF 310 a month from my sibling in Lagos, whose household lives on about CHF 1,130, and I earn CHF 213 a month through occasional technical translation. I also hold a scholarship of CHF 1,739 a year from a professional engineering association. Before that scholarship, my monthly shortfall is CHF 1,949.

The conditions of my provisional admission, together with the demands of full-time laboratory work, limit how much additional employment I can undertake. I have applied to three further foundations and continue to pursue other options.

EduAll support during this bridging period would allow me to keep my research on schedule until project funding is secured, and to complete my doctorate in July 2029 as planned.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

By 2040, I would like to see a factory in Nigeria producing water filtration membranes that cost a tenth of today's imports. It is an ambitious goal, but it rests on something simple: every year, diarrhoeal disease from contaminated water kills tens of thousands of children in my country, and the technology to prevent this already exists. It is simply too expensive.

I was born in Kaduna and trained as a chemical engineer at Ahmadu Bello University. For four years I worked in water treatment, first for a state water board and later for an engineering firm, designing small treatment units for towns and schools. I saw how often imported equipment failed because spare parts were unavailable or unaffordable. That experience shaped the question I now pursue.

My path to Switzerland was not straightforward. In 2019 I had to leave Nigeria at short notice, for reasons connected to my personal safety, and I have lived in Switzerland since then under provisional admission. My asylum application was not accepted, but the authorities recognised that I could not safely be returned. I spent my first years learning French and working in a warehouse, while preparing a research proposal in my evenings. Completing a master's-level bridging programme and being accepted into a doctoral programme at EPFL in my early thirties is the achievement of which I am proudest.

In my first year I completed my first three doctoral courses and built the first laboratory prototype of a ceramic-polymer membrane using locally available clays.

Education, for me, is not a step on a career ladder. It is a way to return, with something useful, to the places that formed me.
