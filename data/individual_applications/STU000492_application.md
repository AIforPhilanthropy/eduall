I have four semesters left in my Bachelor in Geology at the University of Bern, and I finish in July 2028.

My monthly costs are CHF 1,845: rent CHF 676 for a room in a shared flat, food CHF 350, fees CHF 158, health insurance CHF 425, and the rest for transport, phone, field equipment and other small costs.

I have savings of CHF 14,667, and I want to be honest about them. I saved this money over seven years of work, first in a gold mine in western Kenya and later in construction in Switzerland. Since starting university I have been using it for my gap, and it is what is left. I plan to use it for the master in geology, which I want to start in September 2028 and which will take two more years. If I use it now for my last semesters, I will start the master with almost nothing.

I understand that my savings mean I am not in the most urgent situation. But support for these semesters would let me continue into the master with some security.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

I am 29 years old, from Kisumu, on Lake Victoria in Kenya. I study geology.

My mother sells fish at the lake shore and my father, who died when I was twelve, was a fisherman. Nobody in my family went to university. After secondary school I went to work in a small-scale gold mine near Migori, because that was the work that paid. I started as a digger. After two years I was responsible for a shift of twenty miners. I saw accidents that should never happen: tunnels without supports, mercury in the rivers. I wanted to understand the rocks we were digging in, and to make mining safer.

In 2017 I came to Switzerland through my uncle. I worked in construction and learned German. It took me three years to pass the university entrance exam. My German is good in daily life, but scientific German is still difficult, and my written exams take me longer than my classmates. My grades are average, but I have not failed a semester.

In 2024 I started geology in Bern. In the field courses in the Alps, I am the one who knows how to move safely on unstable ground.

I want to work in mining safety and environmental geology, and one day in East Africa, where artisanal mining employs millions of people without protection.

Education for me means that the next boy who goes to work in a mine at nineteen goes into a safe one.
