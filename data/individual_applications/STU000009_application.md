## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

I will be candid about my choices, because I think they matter for understanding my application. From February to May 2026, in my first semester, I worked twelve hours a week as a research assistant in a polymer lab at the Adolphe Merkle Institute. In June I decided to stop, because my laboratory courses require full days, and I was falling behind. That decision was mine, and it means I currently have no personal income.

My parents, both civil servants in Yogyakarta, have a combined monthly income of about CHF 1,320. They send me CHF 280, which is about a fifth of what they earn, and they also support my younger brother at university in Indonesia. I have no savings: what I earned from my assistant job went directly into my living costs.

My monthly expenses are CHF 1,892, of which CHF 957 is rent for a room in a shared flat in Fribourg's Pérolles district and CHF 372 is food. I have tried to keep everything else modest. The result is a shortfall of CHF 1,612 each month.

I have applied to the university's social fund and am waiting for an answer, although I was told support there is usually limited to one-off payments. I have not received any other scholarship.

My thesis is due in July 2028. An EduAll grant would allow me to complete it in 2027/28 without returning to paid work in the most intensive phase, and to graduate on time. I would rather tell you plainly that this is, in part, a situation I chose, than pretend otherwise: I chose it because finishing well matters more to me than finishing comfortably.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

The job that taught me the most was not in a laboratory. For two years after my bachelor's degree, I worked for a waste bank cooperative in Yogyakarta, where households bring sorted plastic and receive small credits in return. I weighed bags, kept records and talked with the women who ran the collection points. Every week I saw the same thing: a mountain of thin plastic films that nobody would buy, because nobody could recycle them.

I grew up in a family of civil servants. My mother works at the regional education office and my father at the land registry. They gave me a steady, unremarkable childhood, and the belief that public service is a worthy life. I studied chemistry at Gadjah Mada University and expected to become a teacher. The waste bank changed my plans.

I came to Fribourg in February 2026 for a Master's in Materials Science because of the university's strength in polymer research. It has been demanding, but also the most rewarding time of my life. I have a GPA of 3.65 and an average of 90.2, and my thesis supervisor has invited me to present my first results at the institute's internal symposium in November.

My research focuses on films made from cassava starch and agricultural waste, materials that are abundant in Indonesia and could replace some of the packaging that ends up in waste banks with nowhere to go. After graduating I would like to work in applied research, ideally in a partnership between a European institute and Indonesian manufacturers.

Outside the lab, I coordinate a monthly clean-up of the Sarine riverbanks with other students and residents, and I help organise the Indonesian community's Independence Day gathering in Fribourg.

For me, education is not an escape from where I come from. It is a way of bringing better tools back to it.
