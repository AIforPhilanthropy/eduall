## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

I am in the first year of the Master's in Economics at Università della Svizzera Italiana (USI). My monthly costs are CHF 2,632 and my income is CHF 770, so I am short CHF 1,862 every month.

My largest cost is rent: CHF 1,198, plus CHF 75 for utilities. I know it is high. I use crutches because of a spinal injury, and I need a ground-floor or lift-accessible flat close to public transport. In Lugano, accessible studios are rare and expensive. I have looked at dozens of cheaper rooms, but they were on the third or fourth floor without a lift.

I earn CHF 490 a month as a research assistant at the economics department, doing data cleaning from home, which I can manage physically. My cousin in Amman, who works in an insurance company and whose household lives on about CHF 1,250, sends me CHF 280 every month.

I have applied for support from the university's hardship fund and from the disability insurance office for mobility costs, but the procedures take months. My thesis must be submitted in July 2028. A grant from EduAll would allow me to finish on time and move straight into work.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

My goal is to work on the economics of disability and employment in the Middle East. In Palestine, and in the region more broadly, people with disabilities are among the poorest, and most of them never find stable work. I want to produce research that helps governments and organisations change this.

I grew up in Hebron. My father drove a delivery truck and my mother raised seven children. None of them had been to university before me. When I was nineteen, I was injured in a fall at a construction site where I worked during the summer. I spent a year in rehabilitation and was told I would probably never walk again. I learned to walk with crutches after two years of physiotherapy.

After the injury, I went back to school, completed a bachelor's in economics at Birzeit University and worked for three years for a vocational training organisation, helping people with disabilities find jobs. I also volunteered with a sports club for wheelchair basketball, and I helped organise a campaign to make public buildings in Hebron accessible.

I came to Lugano in 2026. My GPA is 3.03. Here I volunteer with an association that tests the accessibility of restaurants and shops and publishes the results online.

Education changed my life after the injury. I want to use it to change the lives of others.
