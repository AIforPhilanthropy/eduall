## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

Before starting my doctorate, I worked for seven years as a physician in public hospitals in Lima, including my residency, the last two as head of an emergency department shift. That job paid little by Swiss standards, but it paid regularly. My situation now is different, and I want to describe it as it is.

My doctoral position at ETH Zurich is only partly funded. I am employed for about 7 hours a week as a research assistant on my supervisor's project and earn CHF 815 a month. The rest of my costs are covered by my partner, who works as a nurse in Zurich and earns about CHF 5,900. My partner contributes CHF 1,856 a month to my costs.

My monthly costs are CHF 2,384. Rent is CHF 1,417, health insurance CHF 285 and food CHF 401. The rest is utilities, transport, phone, materials and fees.

So on paper there is no gap. But my partner pays almost a third of their salary for me, and cannot keep this up for four years. We have no savings, because my move to Switzerland and the recognition of my medical diploma cost us everything we had.

My supervisor is applying for a larger grant, which would increase my position to 60% from 2028. If it is not approved, I will need other funding. I have applied to one other foundation.

I am applying now to reduce the pressure on my partner in the first two years. If EduAll decides that my need is not urgent enough, I understand.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

I grew up in Cusco. My father was an engineer at the regional water company and my mother taught secondary school biology.

I studied medicine at the Universidad Peruana Cayetano Heredia in Lima. During my residency in emergency medicine, I saw many patients from the Andes arrive too late with complications of diabetes and high blood pressure. Most had never been diagnosed.

For seven years I worked in emergency departments in Lima. In the last two I led a shift team of twelve doctors and nurses. I also set up a monthly screening day for high blood pressure in a market in San Juan de Lurigancho, with colleagues from our hospital. In two years we screened over three thousand people.

I came to Zurich in 2025 because ETH has a strong group in digital health. My doctoral project develops a simple screening tool for rural health posts, using a smartphone and machine learning. I have completed one doctoral courses.

I miss clinical work. But I believe that a good tool can reach more people than I can in one shift.

Education, for me, is how I can multiply what I did in one hospital.
