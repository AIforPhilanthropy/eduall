## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

I am in the second year of my Bachelor's in Sociology at the University of Lucerne, and I am applying to EduAll to secure the continuity of my studies in a period of uncertainty for my family.

My parents run a grain farm near Vinnytsia, with a household income of around CHF 2,590, and contribute CHF 670 a month towards my costs. I earn CHF 255 a month as a part-time assistant in a Ukrainian community centre. My monthly expenses are CHF 2,039.

My family's farm lies in a region that has been repeatedly hit by missile strikes, and export routes for grain have been disrupted since 2022. The farm's income can change dramatically from one season to the next. My parents have told me they will do everything to keep supporting me, but I know how fragile that promise is.

An EduAll grant would give me a stable basis to complete my degree by January 2028, independently of the next harvest or the next attack.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

When I was eight, I spent a whole summer on our farm watching my grandmother run the village cooperative meeting. She could not read well, but everyone listened when she spoke. She decided who got which tractor, which field, which loan. I realised then that a community is not just people living near each other. It is a set of decisions.

My parents are farmers who never went to university. When the full-scale war began in 2022, I was twenty. For several months I volunteered in Vinnytsia, helping to organise shelter and food for families displaced from the east. I worked in a logistics hub coordinating deliveries, and I saw how quickly ordinary people built systems of solidarity out of nothing.

In 2024 I came to Switzerland to study sociology. Studying in German, a language I learned only after 2022, has been very hard, and my first semesters were a struggle. But I have found my subject: how communities organise themselves in crisis.

I want to research civil society and reconstruction in Ukraine, and later work with organisations that support communities rebuilding after war.
