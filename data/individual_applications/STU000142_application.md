## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

I am a doctoral student in Mathematics at the University of Bern, and I am applying to EduAll to secure the continuation of research that has, until now, been funded by a scholarship for scholars at risk.

That scholarship covered my first year and ended in February 2026. My supervisor has confirmed in writing that the institute cannot offer me a salaried position before a new grant application is decided in 2028. Since then, I have lived on CHF 150 a month from my cousin in Sylhet, a bank officer with a household income of around CHF 910, and on what I had set aside from my scholarship.

My monthly costs are CHF 2,053, of which CHF 1,162 is rent for a studio in Bern. I chose a studio because, as a refugee who has faced targeted threats, I value a private and secure place to live.

The monthly gap is CHF 1,903. An EduAll grant would allow me to continue my research without interruption until my position is funded, and to complete my doctorate by January 2029.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

In fifteen years, I hope to lead a research group in algebraic topology and to be an openly queer mathematician whom young people in South Asia can look to. Neither of those futures seemed possible when I was growing up in Sylhet.

My parents are a rickshaw mechanic and a housewife; neither finished school. I found mathematics through a free olympiad training programme and won a place at the University of Dhaka. There, I co-founded a discreet support network for LGBTQ students and led it for three years. In 2016, two activists I knew were murdered in Dhaka. In 2020, after my name appeared on a list online, I left the country. I was recognised as a refugee in Switzerland in 2021.

In Bern I completed a master's in mathematics while working as a tutor, and began my doctorate in 2025. I volunteer with an organisation supporting LGBTQ asylum seekers, accompanying them to appointments and interviews.

Mathematics has always been the place where I am judged only by the correctness of my reasoning. I want to open that place to others.
