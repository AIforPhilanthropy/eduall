## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

Computer science is, for me, the most practical way to change how people live, and I have four semesters left in the bachelor at University of St. Gallen. I finish in July 2028.

My costs are CHF 1,683 each month. Rent is CHF 776, food CHF 302, fees CHF 205, and health insurance CHF 175. The rest is transport, phone and study materials.

My uncle and aunt in St. Gallen support me with CHF 1,469 each month. They run a small nail salon and their household income is about CHF 5,200. I have no income at the moment, and each month CHF 214 is missing.

I want to be honest that I have savings of CHF 6,457. I earned them in the last three years with weekend work in a restaurant and as a tutor. I stopped work in summer, because my bachelor thesis is with a company and takes all my time. The savings are what I plan to use for the months after graduation, when I look for a first job or start a master, because my uncle and aunt told me they cannot support me after my graduation.

The gap is not large, and I understand if others need more. A grant from EduAll would let me keep my savings for the transition after my degree.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

I was born in Cần Thơ, in the Mekong Delta. My parents had a small boat and sold fruit at the floating market. When I was nine, my father drowned in a storm. My mother could not keep the boat alone. We moved to her sister's house, and she worked in a fish processing factory.

In 2018, when I was fourteen, I came to Switzerland to live with my uncle and aunt in St. Gallen. I did not speak German. I was put in an integration class, and after two years I was in the normal secondary school. I passed the Matura in 2022.

At university I found something I love: programming. My GPA is 3.69 and I have completed 27 courses. I worked the whole time, in a restaurant on weekends and as a tutor in mathematics for secondary students.

In my second year I became head of the tutoring programme of the computer science student association. I organise 20 tutors for first-year students. Many of them come from families without academic background, like me.

After my degree I want to work on software for small businesses, like my aunt's salon, which still makes appointments on paper.

For me education means that my father's daughter is not on a boat, but also not forgetting where the boat was.
