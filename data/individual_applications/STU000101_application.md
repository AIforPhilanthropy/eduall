## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

Let me start with the figures. My monthly expenses in Zurich are CHF 2,530. My income is CHF 1,179 from part-time work as a research assistant at an environmental consultancy, plus CHF 120 a month from my father in Saint-Louis, a retired customs officer whose pension is around CHF 600. I also receive a scholarship of CHF 6,491 a year from a Senegalese diaspora foundation, which I want to declare clearly: it reduces the monthly gap shown in the form, but even with it, I am short by several hundred francs every month. I have no savings.

The largest item is rent: CHF 1,397 for a short-term furnished studio in Oerlikon. I know this is expensive. It is also temporary. I began my Master's in Environmental Science at ETH in February 2026 and could not find anything else in time. The contract ends in March 2027, and since then I have applied for more than forty rooms without success. Several landlords told me openly they prefer Swiss tenants.

I will be honest that I have made one decision that makes my situation harder: I chose ETH, one of the most expensive places in Switzerland to study and live, over a cheaper option elsewhere. I did so because ETH's programme in hydrology is unique for my research.

An EduAll grant would allow me to pay a deposit on a stable, cheaper room and reduce my working hours during my demanding first year.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

I grew up in Saint-Louis, at the mouth of the Senegal River. In 2003, to prevent flooding, authorities cut an emergency breach through the thin strip of land that protects the city from the Atlantic. Within a few years, the breach had widened to kilometres, and the sea swallowed the fishing village of Doun Baba Dièye, where my grandmother lived. I was eleven when we moved her into our house. She never stopped talking about her garden under the water.

I studied geography at Gaston Berger University in Saint-Louis and worked for six years for a regional water agency, mapping flood risk and coastal erosion. I also co-founded a youth association that replants mangroves along the river, which now involves more than 200 young people every rainy season.

At ETH I study hydrology and coastal processes. My goal is to design nature-based protection for West African coastal cities, where millions of people live below the next storm.

My grandmother died in 2021. Her village is now a sandbank. I carry her story into every model I build.
