## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

For many years I worked as a garment worker and then as a union organiser in Dhaka. I learned what a contract means, and what it means when nobody respects it. This is why I study law.

I study Law at University of Zurich, part-time, and I have seven semesters left; I finish in January 2030. My costs are CHF 1,956 per month. My room is CHF 659. I work about 18 hours a week as an interpreter for Bengali at the courts and migration office and earn CHF 2,045. My partner, a nurse in Zurich, contributes CHF 210 from a household income of around CHF 5,900.

My costs are covered. But the interpreting comes at night and at weekends, when the police or the court call, and it takes my study time. In the last year I have my bachelor thesis and exams, and I want to reduce my interpreting work. The grant would help me to finish well.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

My parents were rickshaw pullers and domestic workers in Dhaka. I started to work in a garment factory at fifteen. After the Rana Plaza collapse in 2013, I began organising workers in my factory, and later I worked for a union. We won better safety inspections in twelve factories. I received threats, and in 2016 I left Bangladesh. In Switzerland my asylum application was rejected, but I was provisionally admitted because of the danger I would face if I were sent back.

In Zurich I learned German, did the passerelle, and started law. I also help organise a group of Bengali-speaking workers in Zurich, explaining their rights at work.

I want to become a lawyer in labour law.
