## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

I am entering the second year of my Master's in Environmental Science at the University of Geneva, which I have so far studied part-time. I am applying to EduAll to complete it full-time from September 2027, with my thesis due in July 2028.

My monthly expenses are CHF 2,287, including CHF 985 for a small studio in Carouge and CHF 560 for health insurance. I work about 19 hours a week as an accessibility tester for a digital services company, checking websites and applications with screen readers, and earn CHF 2,128 a month. My parents, who live in Meyrin, contribute CHF 240. My father retired from his work as a driver at a diplomatic mission, and my mother works part-time in a school canteen; together they earn around CHF 3,900, and they also care for my grandmother.

I have a degenerative eye condition, retinitis pigmentosa, which has significantly narrowed my field of vision. Reading with assistive technology takes me much longer than my classmates, and combining this with my job has meant studying at half pace. My thesis includes fieldwork on urban heat islands in Geneva's suburbs, which I will carry out with the help of a mobility assistant, and it requires my full attention.

From September 2027 I will keep only a few hours of testing work, about CHF 230 a month, which leaves a gap of CHF 1,817 a month. A grant would allow me to finish my degree full-time without placing further strain on my parents' retirement.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

My ambition is to work on climate adaptation in cities, making urban spaces cooler, greener and more accessible for everyone, including people who, like me, navigate them with a disability.

I was born in Lahore and came to Geneva at the age of eight, when my father took up a position as a driver for the Pakistani mission. He had studied commerce at university in Lahore, but in Geneva he drove diplomats for twenty years. He never complained. He simply insisted that we study.

I was diagnosed with retinitis pigmentosa at sixteen. It is a slow narrowing of vision, and it will continue. I was told I should perhaps avoid science. I chose environmental science anyway, with a bachelor's at the University of Geneva and now the master's programme. My thesis examines heat exposure in Geneva's suburbs, where housing is dense and green spaces are rare, and I published a first article on this topic from my bachelor's research.

For four years I chaired the student commission on accessibility at the University of Geneva, where we worked with the administration to improve accessible materials and exam accommodations for students with disabilities. I also volunteer with an association that trains guide dogs, helping at weekend training sessions in parks.

After graduating, I would like to work for a cantonal environment office or an international organisation on urban climate adaptation. I intend to make the case, from the inside, that inclusive cities and climate-resilient cities are the same project.
