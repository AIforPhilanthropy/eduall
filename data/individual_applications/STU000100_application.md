## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

I am forty-four years old, an interpreter, a former midwife's assistant, and soon, I hope, a public health professional.

I grew up in Kabul in the 1990s. During the first Taliban period, I was a teenager, and my schooling happened in secret, in a neighbour's living room, with curtains closed. My mother, a doctor who was forbidden to practise, taught us biology from her old textbooks. I still remember the fear of every knock at the door.

After 2001, I trained and worked for many years alongside midwives in a maternity hospital in Kabul. I saw women die from causes that basic prevention and access could have avoided: bleeding, infection, eclampsia. I understood that medicine alone is not enough. Health is decided long before a woman reaches the hospital.

In 2018 I came to Geneva to work for an international health NGO, and later settled in Lausanne. In 2024 I began my bachelor's in public health. Studying in French, while working as an interpreter, has been demanding, but deeply meaningful. Every week I interpret for Afghan women in Swiss hospitals, and I see the same barriers to care that I saw in Kabul, in a different form.

Outside my studies and work, I co-founded an Afghan women's circle in Lausanne, which meets monthly to share information about health, rights and services.

After graduating, I want to work in migrant health, helping public health services reach women who are too often invisible to them.
