## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

I am a third-year Bachelor's student in Physics at the University of Fribourg, and I am applying to EduAll because my housing situation has become precarious at the most important stage of my degree.

My monthly expenses total CHF 2,174. I rent a studio in Fribourg's Beauregard district for CHF 1,000 plus CHF 140 in utilities. In May, the building's owner informed all tenants that the building will be fully renovated from July 2028, and our leases will not be renewed. I have been searching since then, but affordable rooms near the university are scarce, and most landlords ask for a Swiss guarantor, which I do not have.

I cover part of my costs through two sources. I earn CHF 1,002 a month as a weekend receptionist at a hotel near the station. My father, a petroleum engineer in Basra, supports me with CHF 360 a month from a household income of around CHF 1,720; he is also paying for my two younger sisters' education in Iraq. The remaining CHF 812 a month I have covered until now with loans from friends and with extra shifts in the holidays.

My final semester includes an experimental bachelor project that requires regular evening and weekend work in the laboratory, which will conflict with my reception shifts. Moving house in the middle of it, with a deposit of two or three months' rent to find, is the risk I am trying to guard against.

An EduAll grant would allow me to secure new accommodation, reduce my working hours during my project, and graduate on time in July 2028.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

The summer I turned eleven, the electricity in Basra failed almost every afternoon. At fifty degrees, the city stopped. My father brought home a car battery and a small solar panel from his work, and together we built a fan that ran for three hours a day. It was the first thing I ever built, and I have never forgotten the feeling when the blades started turning.

I grew up asking why things work. My father answered what he could and then bought me books when he could not. At school I was the student who stayed after class to argue with the physics teacher. At nineteen, I was accepted to study physics in Fribourg, and I arrived in February 2025 with two suitcases and a French phrasebook.

Studying in French and German, as a teenager alone in a new country, was harder than I expected. There were weeks in my first year when I did not speak to anyone outside class. But I found my place in the laboratory. I have completed 18 courses, and my work on thin-film solar cells in a second-year project was selected for presentation at the department's student day.

Outside my studies, I volunteer with a homework-help programme run by Caritas in Fribourg, working with Arabic-speaking secondary school students on mathematics and science. Several of them arrived in Switzerland as I did, with more ambition than language.

My goal is to complete a master's in physics with a specialisation in photovoltaics and to work on solar energy for hot, grid-poor regions like southern Iraq, where the sun is the one resource that never fails.

Education, for me, began with a fan in a hot room. I would like it to lead to something that keeps the lights on for millions.
