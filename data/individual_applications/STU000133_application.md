## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

My monthly budget is almost balanced on paper, and I should say so clearly. My expenses are CHF 1,818, including CHF 750 for my share of our family's flat in Renens. I earn CHF 929 as a part-time laboratory technician at a materials testing company, and my brother in Erbil, a school administrator whose household earns around CHF 1,670, sends me CHF 300. The gap is CHF 589.

What the numbers do not show is what is happening around them. I am the parent of two children, aged seven and ten, whom I raise with my spouse. Their costs are not in my budget. In April my father in Erbil suffered a stroke, and my brother now carries the cost of his care, which will inevitably reduce what he can send me. My spouse's income as a delivery driver is irregular.

I must also be honest about my studies. My GPA is 2.58, lower than I hoped. Balancing a master's at EPFL, a job, two children and a family crisis has been very difficult, and it has shown in my results.

My thesis is due in July 2028. A grant from EduAll would allow me to reduce my working hours in my final semester and finish my degree.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

I grew up in Mosul in a family of tailors. My father sewed suits, my mother wedding dresses. Neither went to university. I studied materials engineering in Mosul, where I learned about metals and polymers in a laboratory with broken equipment.

In 2014, when the city fell, we had to leave for Erbil, in the Kurdistan Region of Iraq, with nothing. For two years we lived there in a single rented room, displaced inside our own country. We never applied for asylum: in 2016 we came to Switzerland through family reunification, because my spouse's family had lived in Lausanne for many years, and I received a C permit in 2024.

For ten years I worked in Switzerland in whatever I could find: cleaning, a factory, then as a laboratory technician. At thirty-eight I decided to complete a master's degree. My research at EPFL is on recyclable polymer composites.

I want to work in sustainable materials, and to show my children that it is never too late to learn.
