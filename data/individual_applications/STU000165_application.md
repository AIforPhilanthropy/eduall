## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

When I was nine, in Raqqa, my father, a tailor who had never been to school, would sit with me in the evenings and ask me questions I could not answer: why good people suffer, whether a promise binds you forever, what we owe strangers. He did not expect answers. He simply liked the questions.

I studied philosophy in Beirut, the first in my family to go to university, while working as a translator of Arabic and English texts. I volunteered for three years in a school for Syrian refugee children in the Bekaa valley, teaching reading and critical thinking.

My doctoral thesis is on the ethics of hospitality: what societies owe to strangers who arrive at their doors. It is a question my father asked me in Raqqa, and one I have lived in Beirut and in Lausanne. We came to Switzerland from Lebanon in 2024; our asylum application was not accepted, but we were provisionally admitted, because a return to Syria is not possible.

In Lausanne I help run a reading group for Arabic-speaking families at a community library. It brings together people who have lost their countries and found each other.

I want to teach and research moral and political philosophy, in Europe or in the Arab world.
