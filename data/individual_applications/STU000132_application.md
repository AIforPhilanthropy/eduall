## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

I want to give a complete picture of my finances, including my own choices.

I study Data Science part-time at the University of Neuchâtel. My monthly expenses are CHF 1,993, including CHF 1,006 for my share of our flat and CHF 110 for utilities. I work about 22 hours a week, in the evenings and on Saturdays, sorting parcels at a logistics centre, and earn CHF 2,453. My partner, a cook in a restaurant in Neuchâtel, works mostly daytime shifts so that one of us is always with our daughter. She earns around CHF 4,100 and contributes CHF 240 to my share of costs. I have no savings.

On paper, this covers my costs. In practice, it works only because we never see each other: I study in the mornings, care for our four-year-old in the afternoons, and leave for the logistics centre when my partner comes home. I chose a reduced course load for this reason, which also explains why my GPA of 2.58 is lower than I would like.

This autumn the restaurant was sold, and my partner's contract is only guaranteed until the end of the year. If she loses her job, our arrangement collapses. The grant would allow our small family to hold together while I build a degree that will, in the long run, double our household's earning capacity. I graduate in January 2031.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

What happens to a young person's plans when they become a parent at twenty? In my neighbourhood of Villa El Salvador, on the edge of Lima, the answer was usually: they end. I decided mine would not.

My parents sell vegetables in a market stall. Neither finished secondary school. I was the first in my family to be admitted to university, in statistics, in Lima. Then, at twenty, I became a father. I left my studies to work and help care for my daughter, and my partner, who had family in Switzerland, found work here. In 2024 we joined her.

The first year here was hard: new language, no friends, a small child, and the feeling that my chance had passed. What saved me was a parents' group at a community centre in Neuchâtel, where I began helping other Spanish-speaking parents with school registrations and administrative letters. I realised I was good at organising information, at making numbers and forms make sense for people.

That is why I chose data science. I want to work with data from public services, schools, health, social support, to make them easier to use for families like mine.

My daughter now goes to kindergarten. Every morning she asks if I have "school" too. Yes, I tell her. We both do.
