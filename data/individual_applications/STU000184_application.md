## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

I want to write all numbers honestly, also the ones that are not in my favour.

My costs are CHF 2,108 per month. I earn CHF 837 per month working as a cleaner in a hospital, evenings. My brother, who is a shopkeeper in Mashhad and whose household has about CHF 720, sends me CHF 200. I receive a scholarship of CHF 6,949 per year from a foundation for Afghan students, which is paid until December. And I have CHF 11,928 savings, from my work in Iran and eight years of evening work in Switzerland.

So yes, on paper my situation is better than for many others. But the scholarship ends in December, and my bachelor ends in July 2028. After, I want to start a master in materials science, and for this I have no scholarship. My savings will have to pay two years of the master.

I apply so that I can finish the bachelor without touching these savings, which are for the master.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

Bamyan is a valley of red cliffs and snow, and for all of my childhood it was also a valley of empty holes: the niches where the great Buddha statues stood until 2001, the year I was born. My father, a farmer, told me he cried that day. He said something that belongs to everybody had died.

My parents never went to school. In 2010 our family moved to Iran as workers, like many Hazaras. From twelve I worked in brick factories near Mashhad instead of going to school. As an Afghan, I had no future in education there.

In 2015, at fourteen, I came to Switzerland alone. I went to school, learned German and French, did the passerelle, and at twenty-two I started materials science in Fribourg. My GPA is 2.95; it is not high, but I work many evenings besides my studies.

In Fribourg I help to organise the Afghan community association, especially the language courses for newly arrived women.

I want to work on building materials, especially bricks, which are made in very bad conditions in Iran and Afghanistan. I want to make them better, and the work safer.
