## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

I want to lay everything out honestly, because I think it is the only way to ask for help.

My costs are CHF 2,183 a month. My only regular support is CHF 190 from my sister, a teacher in Jos whose household lives on around CHF 660, and a small scholarship of CHF 1,004 a year from a Nigerian sociological association. I have no job.

I do have CHF 11,869 in savings. They come from the sale of my late father's land, which my siblings agreed to use for my studies. I use them to pay the gap of CHF 1,993 every month. They will be gone before the end of my master's in January 2028, probably in the spring.

I should also be honest that I have never had a paid job. For six years I worked without pay as a volunteer researcher with a peace organisation in Jos, while my family supported me. I know this makes my situation look unusual, but that work gave me my two publications and the reason for my studies.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

Jos was once called the "home of peace and tourism." When I was fourteen, in 2010, it became again a city of riots. My school was burned. My neighbours, Christians and Muslims who had shared meals for decades, killed each other in the streets. My father, a secondary school headmaster, hid a Muslim family in our house for a week.

That week shaped my life. After university, I spent six years volunteering with an interfaith peace organisation, documenting violence and organising dialogue groups between young people from both communities. We published two studies on the causes of violence in the Middle Belt. I also led a community radio programme on reconciliation.

In Zurich I study sociology to understand why neighbours turn against each other, and how communities rebuild trust.

I want to return to Jos and help build lasting peace.
