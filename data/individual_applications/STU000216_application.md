## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

I would like to be fully transparent: at present, my resources exceed my expenses.

I work eleven hours a week as a sales assistant in an electronics store in Lausanne, earning CHF 1,185 a month. My parents, who run a small furniture workshop in Alexandria and earn around CHF 1,110, send me CHF 210. I have savings of CHF 8,461, which I earned working full-time for two years before starting university. My monthly costs are CHF 2,427, including CHF 1,261 in rent plus CHF 106 in utilities.

So why apply? First, EPFL's first year is notoriously selective, and I am working more hours than is wise. My first-semester GPA of 2.75 reflects that. I want to reduce my hours to pass the propaedeutic year. Second, my parents' workshop has been hit by the collapse of the Egyptian pound, and what they send me now costs them far more than it did.

If the committee decides that others need the support more, I will understand completely.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

What makes a star burn? I asked my father this at seven, on the roof of our building in Alexandria during a power cut, and he, who never finished school, said he did not know, but that someone surely did. "Go and find that person," he said. "Or become them."

My parents build furniture. Nobody in our family went to university. I spent my teenage years in the workshop after school, learning to measure twice and cut once. Physics felt like the same thing, only bigger.

After school, I worked two years full-time as a technician in an electronics repair company to save money for studying abroad. I also helped organise a Saturday science club for children in our neighbourhood, with borrowed telescopes and cardboard models.

I came to Lausanne in 2025, took the preparatory course, and started physics at EPFL in February 2026.

I want to study astrophysics.
