## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

I will list everything, because I want the committee to see the full picture.

My monthly costs are CHF 2,410. Rent is CHF 1,328 for a small ground-floor studio in Zurich-Wollishofen. I chose it because I have limited use of my right hand and arm, and I cannot manage stairs with shopping and laboratory bags easily. Health insurance is CHF 470.

I do not have a salary. My doctoral position in the microbiology group at UZH was designed to be financed by a grant for which my supervisor applied; the grant was rejected in 2025, and the group has only material costs covered for my project. My supervisor has written a letter confirming this. I receive CHF 4,089 a year from a church foundation that supports refugee scientists, and my cousin in Manila, a pharmacist, gives me CHF 390 a month, a large share of the roughly CHF 1,560 the household has.

I also have savings of CHF 12,371. This is what I saved in eight years of work as a hospital laboratory technician in Zurich before starting my doctorate. I am using these savings to cover the gap of CHF 2,020 per month, but at this rate they will last less than a year, and my doctorate runs until July 2029.

I have applied for a Swiss National Science Foundation fellowship for next year. If I receive it, I will inform EduAll immediately. Until then, a grant would slow down the loss of my savings and give me time.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

In ten years I want to lead a research group on antimicrobial resistance in South-East Asia, and train young Filipino scientists in modern microbiology.

I studied microbiology in Manila and worked as a public health laboratory scientist. I was also active in a community health organisation that documented the health effects of mining on indigenous communities in Mindanao. In 2013 several of our members were "red-tagged", accused of being communist rebels. In 2014 I was attacked on my way home. The attack damaged the nerves of my right arm. I fled to Switzerland and was recognised as a refugee.

I started again from nothing. I learned German, had my degree recognised, and worked for eight years as a laboratory technician in a hospital in Zurich, learning to work with my left hand and with adapted equipment. I also helped found a support group for Filipino workers in Zurich, many of them domestic workers without anyone to turn to.

In 2025 I started my doctorate on resistance genes in hospital wastewater. My first two semesters have gone very well, with top marks in the doctoral courses.

I know I am older than most doctoral students. But I believe my experience, as a scientist, as a refugee and as a person with a disability, makes me a better researcher and teacher.
