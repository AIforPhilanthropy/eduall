## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

I am a second-year nursing student at HES-SO La Source, and I have organised my life around a single goal: to become a registered nurse in Switzerland and, one day, to strengthen nursing education in Palestine. My financial situation is carefully managed, but it is fragile, and the most demanding part of my programme is still ahead of me.

My monthly costs are CHF 2,091, of which CHF 1,167 is rent for a small flat in Prilly and CHF 240 is health insurance. I earn CHF 1,164 a month as a nursing assistant in a care home in Lausanne, working weekend and evening shifts. My parents in Ramallah, whose combined income is around CHF 1,120 a month, send me CHF 190. They have done this every month since I arrived, even in periods when my father's work as a stonemason dried up entirely.

From the next semester, my clinical placements become full-time, with rotating shifts in hospital wards. This makes it very difficult to keep my current working hours, and my supervisors at the care home have already told me they cannot schedule around a placement timetable. The CHF 737 I lack each month today is likely to grow considerably when my income falls.

EduAll's support would allow me to complete my placements with the attention patients deserve, rather than arriving on the ward after an overnight shift elsewhere. It would also allow me to finish my degree on time, in July 2028, and begin working as a qualified nurse.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

By 2035, I want to be teaching clinical skills at a nursing school in Palestine, with the experience of working in one of the best health systems in the world behind me. Every step I have taken since I was fourteen has been towards that goal.

I grew up in Ramallah, the second of five children. My father is a stonemason and my mother raised us at home; neither of them had the chance to study beyond secondary school. When my grandmother was diagnosed with diabetes, it was a young nurse at the local clinic, not a doctor, who taught our family how to manage her insulin and diet. I was fourteen, and I followed that nurse around the clinic every afternoon for a month. I have wanted to do her job ever since.

After school I trained as a practical nurse and worked for two years at a clinic run by a women's health association. I learned how much a nurse can achieve with very little, and also how much more could be achieved with proper training and resources.

Coming to Lausanne at twenty-one meant learning French properly and starting a bachelor's degree far from my family. I have now completed two semesters and 14 courses, with a GPA of 2.85, while working part-time throughout. At the care home where I work, residents' families often ask for me by name, and I was recently asked to help train new assistants in safe mobility techniques.

I am also an active member of an Arabic-speaking women's group in Renens, where I help newcomers understand the Swiss health system and accompany them to appointments when language is a barrier.

Education, to me, is the difference between caring for people and being able to change the conditions they live in. I intend to use every part of this degree.
