## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

My mother sold vegetables in Gikomba market in Nairobi for twenty-five years. She left home before dawn and came back after dark, and she still found time every evening to check my homework, although she herself had left school at twelve. When I told her I had been admitted to a university in Switzerland, she asked me only one question: "Will you learn how to make things that last?"

That question led me to materials science. In Gikomba, nothing lasts: plastic sheeting cracks in the sun, roofs rust through, water containers split. I want to understand why materials fail, and how to make durable ones cheaply.

Before coming to Basel, I worked for three years in a plastics recycling cooperative in Nairobi, first sorting waste and later managing quality control. That experience gave me practical knowledge that I use every day in the laboratory, and a clear sense of why this field matters.

I began my studies in Basel in 2024. Earlier this year my mother was diagnosed with diabetes and developed complications that stopped her from working. As the only child, I have coordinated her care from here, by telephone with doctors in Nairobi, while continuing my studies without interruption.

In Basel I co-organise a monthly repair and recycling workshop with a student sustainability group, where students learn to fix rather than throw away. It connects my studies with the people around me.

After graduating, I intend to pursue a master's degree in polymer science and, ultimately, to work on affordable, durable materials for East African markets. Education, for me, is my mother's question, and I am still working on the answer.
