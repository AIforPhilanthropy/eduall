## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

I am entering the fourth semester of my Bachelor's in Business Administration at the University of Zurich, with graduation expected in July 2028. I am applying to EduAll because my studies involve costs that my regular budget was never designed to absorb, and because the support I receive from home rests on foundations that are less stable than they appear.

I have a severe hearing impairment. In lectures I rely on hearing aids connected to a wireless microphone system, and for seminars and exams I need real-time captioning or a transcript. The university covers part of these accommodations, but the equipment is mine. My current hearing aids are eight years old, and my audiologist has advised replacing them before my final exams. The replacement will cost several thousand francs, of which my insurance will only cover a fraction.

My monthly costs of CHF 1,987 include rent of CHF 746 for a room in a shared flat in Altstetten and CHF 274 for health insurance. I earn CHF 1,151 a month as a part-time assistant in the finance department of a logistics company, a position I have held for two years. My brothers, who run a building-supplies business in Erbil and together earn around CHF 1,920 a month, send me CHF 530. Their business depends on construction contracts that have slowed considerably over the past year, and they have warned me that they may need to reduce their help.

I have not received any other scholarships. A grant from EduAll would allow me to replace my hearing aids before my final semester and to complete my degree without increasing my working hours at the most demanding point of my studies.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

In ten years, I want to be running the finance function of an organisation that hires people like me, people whom most employers quietly screen out. I have seen what happens when finance teams are built only by those who have never had to fight for a seat at the table: they design processes that exclude without meaning to.

I grew up in Mosul, the eldest daughter of a carpenter and a mother who managed our household and the accounts of my father's workshop by hand. Neither of my parents went to university. I lost most of my hearing to meningitis when I was six, and for years teachers assumed I was simply slow. My mother taught me to read lips at the kitchen table. She also taught me bookkeeping, and I was keeping the workshop's records by the age of fourteen.

When our city fell in 2014, my family relocated to Erbil. I was eleven, and I lost a school year. I finished school in Erbil in 2021 and worked for three years, first as an administrative assistant for an NGO, then as the finance assistant of a small women's cooperative, where I helped build their first budgeting system and trained eleven women to manage their own accounts. That role convinced me that I wanted a formal education in business.

Coming to Zurich at twenty-one was a risk. My German was basic, and my hearing makes language learning particularly hard. Today I hold a GPA of 3.29 and an average grade of 86.8, and I have completed 16 courses. At work, I have been given responsibility for monthly reporting in my team, and last year I led a project to digitise our supplier invoices, which reduced processing time by a third.

My goal after graduation is to work in corporate finance for a year or two and then pursue a master's degree focused on inclusive organisations. Ultimately, I want to bring these skills back to Iraq, where businesses owned or run by women and people with disabilities are rare and rarely financed.

EduAll's commitment to equal opportunity is exactly what I hope to practise in my own career.
