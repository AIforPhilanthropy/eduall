## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

Every Tuesday evening for four years in Lyon, I served soup at a night shelter, and I learned how quickly a life can slide from stable to fragile. I think about this often now, because I am honest enough to see that my own situation is not stable.

I started my Master's in Sociology at the University of St. Gallen in February 2026. My monthly costs are CHF 2,488. Tuition at HSG is high, CHF 522 a month, and my studio costs CHF 1,035 plus CHF 91 for heating. I work as a student assistant in the university library, earning CHF 811. My aunt in Paris, a nurse, gives me CHF 652 every month, a large share of the roughly CHF 2,580 the household has. She pays my share because my parents in Dakar cannot.

That leaves CHF 1,025 missing every month. I have no savings: everything I earned during my bachelor went to rent in Lyon. I also have to be honest about my first semester: my GPA is 2.87. I struggled with German in the seminars that are not in English, and with the very different style of teaching at HSG.

A grant would allow me to stop worrying about the end of each month and focus on catching up. I do not want to lose this chance.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

When I was fourteen, my father lost his job at the port of Dakar, and our family fell apart in a few months. My parents separated, the house was sold, and I was sent to live with my aunt in Lyon. I arrived in France in winter with one suitcase and a lot of anger.

It took me a long time to feel at home anywhere. Books helped me. So did the night shelter, where I started volunteering at seventeen. I met people there who had had jobs, families and homes, and lost them. I wanted to understand how societies decide who falls and who is caught.

I finished my bachelor's in sociology at Lyon at twenty, working in a supermarket all the way through. My research on young people in precarious housing was published in two short articles, with my professor.

St. Gallen was a leap. I wanted a master's that combined sociology with economics and organisations. I am the youngest in my programme, and the only one from Senegal. Some days I feel very far from everything.

I want to work on social policy, especially housing and youth. I know from my own life that a stable roof changes everything.
