## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

I would like to be transparent from the start: my monthly shortfall is CHF 1,170, and I cover it from my savings. My parents, who own a small construction company in Dnipro, earn around CHF 2,710 and send me CHF 740. I earn CHF 473 as a museum guide. My costs are CHF 2,383, and I have CHF 4,372 in savings, which will last about four months.

My reason for applying is the uncertainty of my family's business. Their company depends on reconstruction contracts in a region still under regular missile attack, and twice this year their building sites were hit. My parents have said they will keep supporting me, but I know how quickly that could change.

I would also like to fund a research trip to Ukrainian archives for my thesis, and to reduce my dependence on my parents at a time when they need their resources for their own safety. I recognise that other applicants may need support more urgently.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

In twenty years, I want to be a historian who helps Ukraine remember the war honestly: not only its heroes, but also its ordinary people, its losses and its failures.

I was nineteen when the full-scale war began. I was in my first year of history at university in Dnipro. I spent the spring helping at a humanitarian centre that received refugees from the east, and I recorded their stories. Those recordings became the beginning of my research.

In 2022 I came to Lausanne. I learned French in a year, and I still write more slowly than my classmates. I work as a guide at a history museum and help organise a Ukrainian community archive in Lausanne.

My master's thesis is on the memories of displaced people from Donbas.
