## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

When I was a child in a village near Sylhet, my grandfather, who could not read, used to sit under the mango tree in the evening and ask us questions. "If a man steals rice to feed his children, is he a thief?" We argued for hours. I think this was my first philosophy class.

Nobody in my family went to university. I was the first. I studied Bengali literature in Dhaka and became a teacher, and I started to write a blog about religion, freedom and reason. In 2015, several bloggers who wrote like me were killed in Bangladesh. My name was on a list. I left the country in the same year and asked for asylum in Switzerland. I was recognised as a refugee, and I have a C permit now.

In Switzerland I started again from zero. I learned French, I worked in kitchens and in cleaning. In 2018 I founded, with other refugees, a discussion group in Neuchâtel called "Café des idées", where people of all origins meet every month to discuss a philosophical question. I still lead it. I also volunteer as a translator for Bengali-speaking asylum seekers at a legal counselling service.

In 2024 I started Philosophy at the university. It is my dream. Reading Kant or Spinoza in French is very hard for me, but I am learning every day. My thesis is about freedom of expression and the duty of states to protect those who speak.

After my studies, I want to teach philosophy and ethics, and to continue to write, now without fear.
