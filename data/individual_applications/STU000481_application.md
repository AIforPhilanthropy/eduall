## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

Nursing is the work I did for many years without the diploma, and now I study the Bachelor of Nursing at Bern University of Applied Sciences (BFH), in the part-time track.

My costs are CHF 1,816 each month. Rent is CHF 830, food CHF 383, health insurance CHF 210, fees CHF 125, and the rest is transport, phone and books (nursing books are expensive).

I work as a care assistant in a nursing home, about 20 hours a week, and earn CHF 2,221. My husband works as a kitchen helper and earns about CHF 3,500. He gives CHF 280 for my costs. I have CHF 1,311 saved.

From September 2027 I want to study full-time, so that I finish in January 2029 and not July 2029. The last clinical placements are full-time, with shifts, and I can keep only some weekend shifts, about CHF 1,130. Then CHF 406 is missing each month. My savings are enough for a few months.

I have looked for other scholarships, but most are for younger students or for Swiss citizens. I asked the university social fund, and they gave me one-time help last winter. With support from EduAll I can finish my last placements and my thesis without worry that I must stop before the end.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

I was born in a village near Mymensingh. My father was a rickshaw driver, my mother worked in the fields. I was the first girl in the village who finished secondary school.

In 2004 my mother died after giving birth, at home, without any nurse. I was eighteen. From that day I knew what I wanted to do. But there was no money for nursing school, so I worked as a helper in a small clinic in Mymensingh for many years. I learned everything I could, by watching.

In 2016 I came to Switzerland with my husband. I learned German and did a course as a care assistant with the Red Cross. I worked in a nursing home in Bern for seven years. The nurses there encouraged me to study.

In 2024 I started the Bachelor of Nursing. It is hard, the German of the textbooks, the exams, the placements. My grades are not high, GPA 2.74, but I passed 17 courses and I did not fail any placement. My patients like me, my supervisors write that I am calm.

I also volunteer every month with a group that visits Bengali-speaking women who have just had a baby, to explain the Swiss system and help with breastfeeding and check-ups.

I want to work in maternity care. For me, education means that no woman in my family dies like my mother did.
