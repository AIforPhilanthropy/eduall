## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

I will be transparent about what I have and what I lack.

I am in the second year of the Master of Medicine at the University of Geneva. My costs are CHF 2,221 a month. I earn CHF 935 as a night-shift care assistant in a clinic, and my brother, a school director in Thiès whose household earns about CHF 1,010, sends me CHF 200. I have CHF 3,144 in savings. The gap is CHF 1,086 a month.

This is a modest shortfall, and I say so plainly. My reason for applying is my health. I have chronic kidney disease, stage 3. My nephrologist has asked me to stop night work, because irregular sleep and long shifts are harming my kidney function. If I stop, the gap will more than double, and my savings will not last until my degree ends in July 2028.

I would rather protect my kidneys and finish medicine than risk both.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

I am thirty-two, from Thiès, and soon I will be a doctor.

My parents are farmers who never went to school. I studied biology in Dakar, trained as a nurse, and worked five years at the regional hospital of Thiès, the last two as deputy head nurse of internal medicine, leading a team of six nurses. On weekends I volunteered in mobile clinics in rural villages.

In 2017 I was diagnosed with kidney disease. I became a patient in the department I had run. That experience changed how I saw medicine: I understood how frightening it is to depend on others' decisions without understanding them.

I came to Geneva in 2021 to study medicine. My goal is to become a nephrologist and to help build dialysis and kidney care services in Senegal, where most patients with kidney failure still die without treatment.
