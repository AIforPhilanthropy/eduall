## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

Let me begin with the figures, and with a clear account of what I have and do not have.

My monthly expenses in Fribourg are CHF 1,688. My older sister, a pharmacist in Fès (household income around CHF 1,560), sends me CHF 420 each month, more than a quarter of what her family earns. I receive a small scholarship of CHF 1,576 a year from a Moroccan scientific association. I have no savings and no paid work.

I should explain why I have no employment. My doctoral project in the physics department is not funded by a salaried position. I came with a doctoral grant from a French research programme, which was cancelled when my first supervisor moved to Fribourg; I followed the project here, and my new supervisor has written to confirm that no funded position is available before 2028. I have never had a salaried job: my career so far has been entirely scholarship-funded study and research.

The monthly gap is CHF 1,268. My housing is also precarious. I live in a room rented on a monthly basis from a family, and they have told me they need the room back by the end of the year.

I am aware that asking my sister to carry this burden is not sustainable. An EduAll grant would bridge the period until my position is funded.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

I grew up in a working-class district of Fès. My father worked in a tannery and my mother never went to school. When I was sixteen, my father fell ill with a lung disease from the tannery chemicals. He was ill for many years.

I studied physics at Mohammed V University in Rabat on a merit scholarship, from 2011 to 2016, where I was elected president of the physics students' association and organised the first science fair for local secondary schools. After my master's, I went home to care for my father until he died in 2021, while continuing research with my professors from a distance. During those years I also volunteered every weekend with an association teaching mathematics to girls in rural villages near Fès.

I have published three articles on quantum optics, two of them as first author. My doctoral research focuses on single-photon sources for quantum communication.

I want to build a quantum optics laboratory in Morocco one day, and to show young people from districts like mine that physics is not reserved for the privileged.
