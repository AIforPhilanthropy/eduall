## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

I study Law at the University of Neuchâtel, part-time until now. From September 2027 I will study full-time, and I will graduate in July 2028 instead of July 2029.

My monthly costs are CHF 2,266, including CHF 1,050 in rent and CHF 107 in utilities. I work about 14 hours a week in a supermarket and at a legal counselling office and earn CHF 1,589. My parents, who live in La Chaux-de-Fonds and work as a factory worker and a cleaner, earn around CHF 7,000 together and give me CHF 380. Every month CHF 297 is missing.

In my final year I must complete an unpaid internship at a law firm, which takes three days a week, and write my thesis. I will keep only the counselling office, about CHF 680 a month, and the gap will be CHF 1,206. A grant from EduAll would allow me to complete my degree a year sooner, without my parents having to give more than they already do.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

The first time I entered the courthouse in Neuchâtel, as a fifteen-year-old interpreter for my aunt at a hearing about her work permit, I was terrified. I did not know half the words. The judge was kind, but I left knowing that without me, my aunt would have understood nothing.

We came to La Chaux-de-Fonds from a village in the Rif mountains when I was thirteen. At home we spoke Tarifit. I knew some Arabic, very little French. I spent two years in a welcome class. My father, who had studied agronomy in Morocco, could only find night shifts in a watch component factory here, and my mother cleaned offices. In 2019 my father lost two fingers in a machine accident. It took the family two years to recover.

I have worked since I was sixteen. For four years I have also volunteered at a legal information service for migrant women, and this year they began to pay me for a few hours a week.

Legal French is still harder for me than for my classmates, but I have passed every year.

I want to become a lawyer in labour law and migration.
