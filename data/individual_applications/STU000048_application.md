## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

Every month I am short CHF 1,883. My parents send me CHF 170, and I have a small scholarship of CHF 2,131 per year from my church in Nairobi, but my costs in Basel are CHF 2,053. I don't have a job and I don't have savings.

I want to tell you honestly why I don't work. When I came here I thought I would find a job easily, like in Nairobi. But every job asks for German, and my German was very weak. I applied at cafés, supermarkets, cleaning companies. Nobody called back. I have now decided to put all my energy into passing my exams, because my grades are also a problem.

My parents own one matatu, a minibus, in Nairobi. My father drives it and my mother does the accounts. In good months they earn about CHF 1,130, but they also pay school fees for my three younger siblings. When the fuel price went up this year, they had some months where they could send me almost nothing.

My rent is CHF 1,089 for a room in a shared flat. It is expensive, but I could not find anything cheaper that would accept a student without a Swiss guarantor.

I don't want to stop. I am the first one in my family who went to university, and my whole family is watching me. Please help me to stay.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

I will not pretend that my studies are going well. My GPA is 2.2 and I am "At Risk". I know that this is below what EduAll normally expects, and I want to explain it, not hide it.

I came to Basel in 2025, at twenty-one. I was so excited. I had studied German for one year in Nairobi at the Goethe-Institut, and I thought it was enough. It was not. In my first lecture, in epidemiology, I understood maybe one word out of ten. I cried in the toilet afterwards. For the whole first year I was translating every slide with my phone. I failed two exams and passed others only just.

But something changed this year. I found a study group, three Swiss students who explain things to me in simple German, and I help them with the statistics, which I understand well. My German is now B2. My last exams were my best ever. I believe my grades will rise.

Why public health? In my estate in Nairobi, when I was sixteen, there was a cholera outbreak. Our neighbour's baby died. It was because of the water, and everybody knew it, and nobody did anything. I want to be the person who does something.

I don't have a long CV. I did not have jobs or big activities before, I was in school and then preparing to come here. But I have a family who believes in me and a clear goal.

Education means to me that my siblings will see that it is possible. I really, really want to finish this.
