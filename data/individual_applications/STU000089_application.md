## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

I grew up in Maiduguri, in north-eastern Nigeria. My parents, a trader and a seamstress, never went to school. When the Boko Haram insurgency reached our city in 2009, I was twelve. Our neighbourhood was attacked twice. My father's shop was burned. For three years we lived in an internally displaced persons camp outside the city, twelve people in one tent.

In that camp, I organised a study group for teenagers who, like me, had lost their schools. We had one mathematics textbook, which we copied by hand. Nine of us eventually passed the national exams, and four went to university.

I studied materials engineering at the University of Lagos, where I was elected president of the Nigerian Society of Engineering Students, representing more than 3,000 members, and led a campaign for better laboratory equipment. Afterwards I completed a master's degree and spent two years as an unpaid visiting researcher, applying for doctoral positions abroad.

In 2026 I was admitted to Fribourg. My thesis addresses a problem I saw every day in the camp: corroded pipes that made water unsafe.

I want to work on affordable, durable materials for water infrastructure in regions like north-eastern Nigeria, where clean water is still a matter of life and death.
