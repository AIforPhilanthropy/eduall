## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

When I was nine, in Multan, I watched my mother bargain for an hour at the market for a bag of rice, and then give half of it to a neighbour who had nothing. I asked her why. She said: "Today it is her. Tomorrow it will be us." That sentence is, in a way, my research question: how do poor communities protect each other when the state does not?

My parents never went to school. My father was a night watchman, my mother cleaned houses. When my father died when I was fourteen, my brother left school to work, so that I could continue. I have never forgotten that.

I studied sociology in Lahore on a scholarship, and worked three years for an NGO on informal savings groups in poor neighbourhoods. My first co-authored paper came from that work.

My master's thesis looks at how Pakistani migrants in Switzerland support each other financially, through informal lending and remittances.

I want to become a researcher and work on social protection for informal workers.
