## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

I would like to be transparent about every aspect of my application, including the weakest one.

I began the Bachelor's in Physics at the University of Geneva in September 2025. My monthly costs are CHF 2,222, mostly my room at CHF 1,145. I work as a kitchen assistant six hours a week and earn CHF 687. My mother, a shea butter trader in Tamale, gives me CHF 310, a large share of the roughly CHF 1,090 the household has. The gap is CHF 1,225 a month.

My academic record in the first year is poor: my GPA is 2.08, and I am at risk. The move from Ghana to Geneva, the French language and the pace of the physics courses overwhelmed me, and I failed two courses. I am retaking them this autumn, and I have joined a tutoring programme.

I work because I must, but it costs me study time. A grant would allow me to reduce my kitchen hours in the second year, when I need to prove that my first year was not my limit.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

What changes a life? For me, it was a torch. When I was nine, in a village near Tamale, there was no electricity, and a teacher gave me a solar torch for winning a mathematics contest. For the first time, I could read at night. I took the torch apart to see how it worked. I have been curious about physics ever since.

My parents never went to school. I grew up helping my mother sell shea butter at the market. At sixteen I founded a study club in our town, where older students taught younger ones; it now has sixty members. I volunteered with a solar lamp project, and worked two years in a phone repair shop to save money.

I know my first year was weak. But I am the first person in my family to go to university, and I will not waste it.
