## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

I will tell you everything, because I have nothing to hide and I have very little left to protect.

My monthly costs in Fribourg are CHF 2,188: rent of CHF 1,002 for a small studio, CHF 139 of utilities, and health insurance of CHF 230, which is expensive because I am thirty and no longer eligible for student insurance. My older sister and her husband, a technician in Riyadh, send me CHF 700 every month from a household income of about CHF 2,440. That is almost a third of what they have.

I do not have a job now. I worked during my first year as a kitchen assistant, but I stopped in spring, when my research practicum began in schools. I still have CHF 6,740 of savings. I know that this is money many applicants do not have. But the gap of CHF 1,488 every month is eating it quickly.

And this summer, everything changed at home. My father, who has diabetes, lost most of the sight in one eye and needs laser surgery on the other, very soon, or he will become blind. In Egypt this is not covered for him. My sister now has to send money to our parents too. She told me, crying on the phone, that she cannot continue sending me the same amount.

My savings will now have to go to my father's eyes. I cannot let him go blind so that I can finish my thesis. But I also cannot bear to stop four months before the end. I am asking EduAll to help me finish in July 2028 without choosing between them.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

Education is the only thing that ever changed anything in my family. My grandmother married at thirteen. My mother at sixteen. Neither could read. I was the first person in my family to hold a pen with confidence, and the first to go to university. I know, in my body, what one good teacher can do.

Mine was Ms. Samia, in a crowded government school in Minya. Seventy children in the class. She noticed that I read everything I could find, and she gave me her own books. When my father wanted me to leave school at fourteen to work, she came to our house and argued with him for two hours. I stayed.

I became a teacher myself, and taught Arabic and English for five years in schools in Minya and Cairo. I saw classrooms of seventy, eighty, a hundred children, and teachers with no training for it. I wanted to learn how to change that.

In Fribourg I study how teachers can be supported in very large and very diverse classrooms. My thesis follows three teachers in Fribourg schools with many refugee children, and I compare their strategies with those I knew in Egypt. My GPA is 3.51. My German and French are still not strong enough, and I often feel like a child again in seminars. But I am learning, like my students used to learn.

When I finish, I want to train teachers, in Switzerland or in Egypt. I want to be Ms. Samia for many teachers, who will each be Ms. Samia for seventy children.
