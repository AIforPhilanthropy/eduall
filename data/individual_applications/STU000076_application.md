## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

I began the Master's in Sociology at the University of Bern in February 2026. My monthly costs are CHF 2,215, of which CHF 976 is rent for a room in Bümpliz and CHF 415 is health insurance.

I work as a night receptionist at a youth hostel two nights a week, earning CHF 467. My older brother, a civil servant in Ibadan whose household income is around CHF 1,050, sends me CHF 233. The gap is CHF 1,515 a month.

My eyesight limits the kind of work I can do. I have oculocutaneous albinism, with low vision, and I rely on magnification software and large-print materials. Many student jobs, such as delivery, cashier work or anything involving reading small print quickly, are not possible for me. The reception job is one of the few that works.

A grant from EduAll would allow me to focus on my coursework and research without taking on more night shifts, which already leave me exhausted in morning seminars.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

Let me introduce myself: a sociologist in training, from Ibadan, Nigeria, thirty-two years old, born with albinism.

In Nigeria, people with albinism are often seen as cursed or magical. As a child I was called names on the street; some people would not touch me. My parents, a market trader and a carpenter who had never gone to university, protected me as well as they could. My mother went to my school every term to explain to teachers why I needed to sit in the front row.

I studied sociology at the University of Ibadan, where I was the first person in my family to graduate. Then I worked for nine years for a disability rights organisation, first as a field officer and then as a programme officer, documenting discrimination against people with albinism and running awareness campaigns in schools.

My master's research will study how stigma against visible difference affects access to employment in West Africa and among African migrants in Europe.

I want to continue to a doctorate and then teach and research in Nigeria, where disability studies is still a young field. I want the next child with albinism in Ibadan to grow up hearing different words.
