## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

What does it take to become a doctor at twenty-four, in a third language, after a war? More patience than I thought I had.

I grew up in Raqqa. My parents were farmers who never went to school. In 2014, when I was thirteen, ISIS closed my school and my mother, a nurse's aide, could no longer work. The next year we fled to Turkey.

In Gaziantep I helped in a clinic for refugees after school, first cleaning, then translating for the doctors. That is where I decided to become a doctor. In 2019 we came to Switzerland. Our asylum application was rejected, but we have provisional admission, because a return to Syria is not possible. I finished school, learned German, and worked as an interpreter in hospitals.

In March, my mother, who went back to Syria to care for my grandmother, was injured in a building collapse and needs surgery.

German medical language is very hard for me, but I passed the entrance exam.

I want to be a doctor for people who, like me, arrive without the language.
