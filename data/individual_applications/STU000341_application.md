## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

My monthly costs are CHF 1,955, and my monthly income is CHF 710. The difference, CHF 1,245, is what I am asking EduAll to help with, and I want to explain it fully.

I am a doctoral student in Biology at Università della Svizzera Italiana (USI), in my second semester. My doctorate has no salaried position; teaching, about 5 hours a week, pays CHF 550 a month; my supervisor has confirmed in writing that the rest of my position depends on a grant to be decided in 2028. My brother, a civil engineer in Lahore, sends me CHF 160 out of a household income of about CHF 900. I have no savings.

I have tried to close the gap by taking additional teaching hours, but they cut into my laboratory time, and a doctorate in molecular biology does not forgive absences from the lab. I have also applied to two private foundations and am waiting for their answers.

I would be candid that my situation depends on a single grant decision next year. A grant from EduAll would bridge the months until then.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

I grew up in a village near Gujranwala. My father was a farm labourer and my mother never went to school. In our village, girls usually left school at twelve. I did not, because a teacher came to our house three times to convince my father.

I studied biotechnology in Lahore on scholarships, and worked for three years as a research assistant, publishing three papers on plant disease resistance. On weekends I volunteered at a free school for girls in my village, teaching science. I also helped organise a network of young women scientists in Punjab, which now has three hundred members.

When I was twenty-two, my father died, and I became responsible for my two younger sisters' education. Both are now at university.

My doctorate is on how wheat defends itself against fungal disease. I want to return to Pakistan and help farmers protect their harvests.
