I started my Master in History at the University of Neuchâtel in February 2026, part-time.

From September 2027 I want to study full-time, so that I finish in January 2029 instead of January 2030. I will keep only Saturdays at the bookshop, about CHF 1,010, and CHF 758 will be missing every month.

I want to be honest that I have savings of CHF 14,789. I saved this money over six years of full-time work before the master. But this year my mother in Peru was diagnosed with cancer, and I am the only one of my siblings who can help with the costs of her treatment, which are not covered by her insurance. I already sent part of my savings in the spring, and I expect to send more. A grant from EduAll would let me keep studying without having to choose between my education and my mother's treatment.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

I grew up in Villa El Salvador, a district on the sand south of Lima. It was built by families who came from the mountains in the 1970s, my grandparents among them. When I was a child there were still streets without water pipes, and the community organised everything itself: kitchens, schools, even a newspaper. I think this is why I love history. My neighbourhood made its own.

My parents sell shoes at a market stall. Nobody in my family went to university. In 2014, when I was fifteen, my mother came to work in Switzerland as a cleaner, and one year later I joined her in Neuchâtel. I did not speak French. School was very hard, and my mother had to go back to Peru after two years for health reasons. I stayed with a host family, finished my apprenticeship as a bookseller, and worked full-time in a bookshop for six years, the last three as deputy branch manager.

I did my bachelor in history part-time while working, and started the master this year. Academic French is still difficult for me. I write slowly and I need my texts checked. My first semester results were average, and I know I need to improve.

I volunteer at a homework club for Latin American children, and I co-founded a small association of Peruvian families in Neuchâtel that organises cultural events and helps newcomers.

For me education is a way to write down the history of people like my grandparents, who built a city from sand and are in no book.
