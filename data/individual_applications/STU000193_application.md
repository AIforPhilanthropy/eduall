I would like to be precise and candid about my situation.

I am in the first year of the Master's in Chemistry at the University of Basel, which I study part-time. My monthly expenses are CHF 2,270, most of which is rent: CHF 1,197 plus CHF 92 for utilities. I took this studio in a hurry after my previous shared flat was dissolved; it is more than I should pay, and I am looking for a cheaper place.

I earn CHF 2,039 a month as a laboratory helper, about 18 hours a week. My uncle in Sylhet, a schoolteacher whose household income is around CHF 680, sends me CHF 70. I also receive a scholarship of CHF 6,189 a year from a foundation supporting refugee students, which covers the CHF 161 monthly gap shown in the form, but it ends in August 2027. I have no savings.

From September 2027 my research project needs full laboratory days, and I will have to reduce my hours to about CHF 1,380 a month. The gap will then be CHF 820 a month.

My academic results are modest: my GPA is 2.66. My first semester coincided with the family crisis described below, and I did not perform as well as I can. In January, my father, who lives with my mother in a reception centre in Geneva while their asylum appeal is processed, was hospitalised with severe depression. I travel to Geneva every weekend to support my mother.

An EduAll grant would allow me to finish my master's by January 2030.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

When I was ten, in Dhaka, my father took me to his newspaper office on a Saturday. I remember the smell of ink, the noise of the machines, and my father's colleagues arguing about politics. He was an editor. He told me that words can be more dangerous than weapons. I did not understand then.

In 2019, after his newspaper published an investigation into political violence, our house was attacked. My father was beaten. We fled. I was fifteen. After months in transit, we arrived in Switzerland, and I was granted refugee status; my parents' case is still pending.

I learned German in a year, finished the gymnasium in Basel, and studied chemistry. I worked in a supermarket during my bachelor, and now in a laboratory.

My thesis is on catalysts for water purification. I chose chemistry because it is precise, and because, unlike politics, a reaction does not lie.

I want to do a PhD and work on clean water technologies.
