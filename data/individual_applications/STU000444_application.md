## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

For four years, before I started medicine, I worked as an ambulance technician in Iași. On a night shift you learn quickly what is urgent and what can wait. I will try to apply the same standard to my own application.

My parents run a small construction company in Iași. Their combined income is around CHF 5,030 a month in good years, and they send me CHF 1,170. I earn CHF 500 a month tutoring first-year students in anatomy. My monthly expenses are CHF 2,675: CHF 1,430 for a room in a shared flat in Plainpalais, CHF 330 for food, CHF 465 for health insurance, and the rest for fees, transport, phone and books.

So every month CHF 1,005 is missing, which I have covered with the last of what I saved as an ambulance technician. I am applying because I do not think this can hold for the next year. My parents' company depends on two or three large contracts at a time, and this spring one of them was cancelled after the client went bankrupt. My father has told me honestly that he cannot promise the same support next year. At the same time, my clinical clerkships start in autumn 2027. They are full-time, with early shifts and weekend duties, and I will not be able to keep tutoring at the same level.

I have not applied for any other scholarship yet, because most of the ones I found are for Swiss residents or for students with lower family income. I have no savings left; what I saved as an ambulance technician paid for my first year in Geneva and this gap.

I understand that other applicants may be in a more difficult position right now, and I accept that. If EduAll can support me for the final year, it would protect my clerkships from the risk that my family's business has a bad year.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

I come from Iași, in the north-east of Romania. My parents started their construction company after the revolution with one van and two workers. Neither of them went to university. They wanted me to take over the company one day, and I spent my school holidays on building sites.

At nineteen, I took a course as an ambulance technician instead, and for four years I worked on emergency crews covering the city and the villages around it. In my last two years I coordinated the shift schedule and the training of new crew members. I saw many patients who reached hospital too late, not because we were slow, but because the nearest hospital was an hour away.

That is why I decided to study medicine. I did my bachelor's in Geneva from 2022, in French, which I had only learned at school. The first semester was hard. I recorded every lecture and listened again in the evening. Now I have completed two courses in the master's programme and I feel at home in the hospital.

My goal is emergency medicine, and later helping to build better pre-hospital care in rural Romania. For my family, my studies were at first a strange choice. Now my father tells his workers about "the doctor" in the family. Education for me means being able to finally help the patient in the ambulance, and not only drive them.
