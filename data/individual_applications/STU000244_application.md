## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

For eleven years I worked as a lab technician at a water treatment plant in Zaporizhzhia, testing the water that half a million people drank. When the dam at Kakhovka was destroyed in June 2023, I was on shift. I will never forget the sound of the phones.

I must tell you honestly that today I do not need money in the usual sense. I study part-time and work about 19 hours a week in a water-testing laboratory, where I earn CHF 2,169. My partner, a software engineer in Zurich, earns around CHF 9,020 a month and pays CHF 380 of my costs. My costs are CHF 1,607. I have CHF 3,092 in savings. I have more than I need.

I am applying for something else. In summer 2028 I want to spend six weeks in southern Ukraine doing fieldwork on water contamination after the dam's destruction, which will become my bachelor thesis. For those weeks I cannot work, my university does not fund fieldwork in war zones, and my partner is frightened for me and does not want to pay for it. I want to pay for it myself.

If the committee decides that my application does not fit, I will understand. There are others who need this for food and rent, and they should come first.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

My mother cleaned floors at the plant where I later worked. My father drove a tram. Neither went to university. When I was twelve, my mother took me to the laboratory on a Saturday, and a chemist let me look at a drop of river water under the microscope. I saw it was alive. I never stopped looking.

I could not afford university after school, so I trained as a technician and worked. I was good at it. When the war started, I stayed and kept the water safe as long as I could. In 2023, after the dam, the plant was shelled. I left.

In Zurich I started environmental science at thirty-one. My grades are the best I have ever had, but that is not because it is easy. It is because I have waited so long.

I want to work on water safety in post-war reconstruction.
