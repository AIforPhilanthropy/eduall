## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

I am in the first year of the Master's in Philosophy at the University of Lausanne, which I study part-time, and will graduate in July 2029.

My monthly costs are CHF 2,009. I work as a night receptionist at a hostel, three nights a week, about 24 hours, and earn CHF 2,697. My brother, a school headmaster in Khulna whose household earns about CHF 940, still sends me CHF 190, although I have asked him to stop.

My income covers my costs. But my room, for CHF 868, is a sublet that ends in October. I have been looking for two months. Every room I find asks for a deposit of three months, and working nights, I cannot afford to spend the autumn moving from one temporary room to another.

A grant would allow me to secure a stable room and finish my degree.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

Philosophy matters because it asks what we owe each other, a question every refugee camp, every border and every city answers badly or well. I first asked it at twelve, in Khulna, when a cyclone destroyed our neighbours' homes and my father, a rickshaw driver who never went to school, gave half of our rice to a family we barely knew. "We are all neighbours when the water comes," he said.

I was the first in my family to study. I read Bengali literature and philosophy in Dhaka, and worked as a secondary school teacher for years. In 2013, after my writing on religious tolerance drew threats, I left Bangladesh and eventually settled in Switzerland, where I now hold a C permit.

I learned French at thirty. Philosophy in French is still a mountain I climb every day; I read every text twice.

My thesis is on the ethics of hospitality in Levinas and in Bengali Sufi poetry.

I want to teach philosophy and ethics.
