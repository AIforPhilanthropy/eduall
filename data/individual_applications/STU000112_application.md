## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

When I was eight, I sold oranges in the market of Takoradi after school, with my grandmother. She could not read, but she knew the price of everything, and she knew which customers would come back. Every evening she counted the coins twice, and put a few aside in a tin for my school fees. This tin paid my primary school.

My parents did not go to university; my mother died when I was ten and my father worked as a fisherman far away. My grandmother raised me. When she died, I was sixteen, and I lived with my aunt.

I studied economics at the University of Cape Coast with a scholarship, and worked five years at a rural bank, giving small loans to market women like my grandmother. I also volunteered with a savings group project for women in villages, teaching them to keep simple records. With my supervisor at the bank I wrote a paper on women's savings groups, which was published.

In Lucerne I help in the African students' group, organising welcome days and information about jobs.

My goal is to work on financial inclusion for women in West Africa. The tin of my grandmother is my first lesson in economics.
