## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

On a rainy evening last February, a week before my doctorate began, I stood outside a building in Kleinbasel with two suitcases, having just learned that the room I had rented online did not exist. I spent that night in a hostel and the following six weeks in three different temporary rooms. I begin with this because it explains why I am applying, even though my budget currently balances.

I should be transparent about my situation. My wife, who works as a nursing assistant in Basel and earns about CHF 4,900 a month, contributes CHF 1,978 towards my costs. My monthly expenses are CHF 2,001, so at the moment there is no gap. I also have savings of CHF 5,944, which remain from my previous job in Jakarta.

The difficulty is that my doctoral position is not yet funded. My supervisor has applied for a project grant that would employ me as a doctoral assistant; the decision is expected in spring 2028. Until then I am enrolled without a salary, and our household depends on a single income, with a fixed-term contract that ends in March 2027. If her contract is not renewed before the grant decision, we would have to live on our savings, which would last only a few months.

I am also still paying for the consequences of my housing problems: a rent deposit I lost, and short-term rooms at a higher price. We finally found a stable flat in July.

I would ask EduAll to consider my application as insurance against a bridging period, rather than a current shortfall. If the committee sees more urgent needs, I will fully understand.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

I grew up in Bandung, where my father repaired electrical equipment in a market stall and my mother sold food. I was the kind of child who took apart radios. At Bandung Institute of Technology I discovered chemistry, and I stayed for a master's in catalysis.

For five years I worked as a process chemist for a palm oil refinery near Jakarta. I was good at the work, but I became increasingly uncomfortable with its environmental cost. In 2021 I applied for a research position on biomass catalysts. That path eventually led me, after difficult personal circumstances that I prefer not to detail, to Switzerland in late 2025.

My doctoral project in Basel concerns catalysts that convert agricultural waste into chemicals. I have completed my first two doctoral courses.

My greatest difficulty is language. My research is in English, which I speak well, but everything else in daily life, from the housing office to my wife's employer, is in German. I am at level A2, and I attend evening classes twice a week. When I lost the room in February, I could not even read the complaint form.

I want to build a research career that connects Southeast Asia's agricultural economies with cleaner chemistry. Education, for me, is the long way to make up for the years I spent optimising a process I no longer believed in.
