## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

My financial need is small, and I want to say this first. I study the Master in History part-time. My expenses are CHF 1,757 per month. I earn CHF 1,947 as a research assistant for a professor at the History department and as a teacher at a Saturday Vietnamese school, about 17 hours a week together. My spouse, who owns a small Vietnamese restaurant in Zurich with a household income of about CHF 4,940, gives CHF 370 for my part of our costs. I have no savings.

So why do I apply? The restaurant is our only family income besides my work, and since the construction work on our street started this spring, we have lost about a third of our customers. The work will continue until summer 2027.

And in 2027/28 I must spend several weeks in archives in Paris and Aix-en-Provence, because my research is on colonial documents about Vietnam. During these weeks I cannot work, so my income will go down to about CHF 1,190 on average, and I will miss around CHF 197 per month, plus the travel.

A grant, even a small one, would allow me to do this archive work and would mean that my studies are not a burden on the restaurant in a difficult year.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

My goal is to write the history of ordinary Vietnamese people under French colonial rule, using documents that no Vietnamese researcher has read before. Most of these archives are in France, written in French, and until now mostly read by Western historians.

I am 44 years old. I grew up in Nam Dinh, in the north. My parents were rice farmers, and I was the first in the family to go to university. I studied journalism in Hanoi and worked for sixteen years as a journalist and later editor for a history magazine. I led a team of six writers. We published stories about villages, old crafts, and people forgotten in official history.

In 2019 I moved to Zurich, where my spouse had opened a restaurant. I learned German and French, and I volunteered at a Vietnamese language school for children every Saturday. In 2026 I started my master's at the University of Zurich.

My first semester went well, my GPA is 3.6, and I have already published two articles in history journals, based on work I began as a journalist.

After the master I want to do a PhD. I would like to make these archives accessible to Vietnamese readers, and to write books for the public, not only for academics.

For me education means to give back to my people their own stories.
