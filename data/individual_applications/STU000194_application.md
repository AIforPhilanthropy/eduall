I am a doctoral student in Psychology at the University of Neuchâtel, in my second year, researching the long-term psychological effects of family separation through migration. My doctoral position is not salaried: it is supported by a research scholarship of CHF 7,055 a year from a Swiss foundation, which covers only part of my costs. My supervisor has confirmed that no salaried position will open before 2028.

My monthly expenses are CHF 2,054. They include my share of rent, CHF 999 plus CHF 138 utilities, CHF 62 for research materials and participant compensation, and CHF 65 in other costs, a large part of which is travel to my mother's hospital appointments in Bern. My mother, who works part-time as a cleaner in Neuchâtel and earns about CHF 2,300, contributes CHF 690 each month, which is more than she can afford.

My housing is also insecure. The flat I share with my mother is in a building that has been sold, and all tenants must leave by March 2027.

An EduAll grant would allow me to continue my doctorate while supporting my mother through a difficult period.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

When I was five, in Ayacucho, my mother left for Switzerland to work. She sent money, letters and cassette tapes with her voice. I listened to them every night under my blanket. For five years I grew up with my grandmother, waiting. When I finally joined my mother in Neuchâtel at ten, I barely recognised her, and she barely recognised me. It took us years to find each other again.

That experience is at the centre of my research. There are millions of children in Latin America, Asia and Africa who grow up while a parent works abroad. We know very little about how these separations shape them, and their parents, over decades.

At school in Neuchâtel I struggled with French and with belonging. But I found my way, studied psychology, and completed my master's with distinction. I have published three articles on transnational families.

This year my mother was diagnosed with a serious autoimmune disease. She needs regular treatment in Bern, and I accompany her. The woman who crossed an ocean to give me a future now needs me close.

I want to become a researcher and a clinician, working with families separated by migration.
