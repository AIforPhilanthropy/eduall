## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

I want to be upfront: right now my income is higher than my expenses. I study part-time and work about 23 hours a week as a support worker in a residential home for people with intellectual disabilities, and I earn CHF 2,577 a month. My partner, who works as a logistics coordinator in Bern and earns about CHF 5,030 a month, contributes CHF 320 to my share of our costs. My own expenses are CHF 2,140 a month, most of it my part of the rent, CHF 1,141.

So why am I applying? Because this arrangement will not last through my degree. My partner's contract is fixed-term and ends in March 2027, and the company has already said it will not be renewed. If my partner does not find a new job quickly, we will both live on what I earn. In 2027/28 I also have a clinical internship that is part of the master, and I will have to reduce my care work to about 18 hours a week, so my income will go down to around CHF 2,060.

I have no savings. Before starting my master's I worked full-time, but most of that money went into language courses, the recognition of my Romanian degree and moving costs. I have not received any other scholarship.

My master's in Psychology, part-time, runs until January 2029. I would like EduAll to consider my application with that date in mind, and I will of course report any change in our situation, positive or negative.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

Do you remember the moment you understood what you wanted to do with your life? For me it was a Tuesday night shift in 2021, when one of the residents I cared for, a man in his fifties who does not speak, took my hand and led me to the window to show me the snow. Nobody had ever told me he could communicate like that. I realised how much we miss when we only listen to words.

I grew up in Iași, in north-eastern Romania. My mother works in a bakery and my father drove trucks until his back gave out. Nobody in my family studied at a university before me. I finished a bachelor's in psychology in Iași while working evenings in a call centre, and then came to Switzerland in 2019 to work in social care, because the pay in Romania did not allow me to live independently.

For five years I worked in care homes and residential services around Bern. I learned German on the job, first standard German and then, with a lot of patience from my colleagues, enough Bernese to understand the residents. In February 2026 I started the Master's in Psychology at the University of Bern. My first semester went well: GPA 3.43, average 83.8. Academic German is still hard for me, and I need much longer than my classmates for reading and writing, but I am getting there.

Outside of work and study I volunteer with a peer-support group for Romanian care workers in Switzerland, and I help run a monthly language café in my neighbourhood.

I want to work as a psychologist in disability services, focusing on non-verbal communication. For me education is a way to turn what I learned in care work into something that can help many more people than I can reach alone.
