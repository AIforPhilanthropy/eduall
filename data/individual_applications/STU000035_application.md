## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

In the interest of full transparency, I should begin with what I have, before explaining what I lack.

My father, who runs a car-parts shop in Sulaymaniyah, has supported me throughout my studies. Our family's income is around CHF 1,750 a month, and he sends me CHF 340. I also have savings of CHF 5,134, most of which he gave me when I first came to Switzerland, as a reserve for emergencies. My monthly expenses are CHF 2,148, which leaves a modest gap of CHF 1,808. I do not have a job; my chemistry programme, with its long laboratory days, has made it very difficult to hold one, and I have never worked before.

Two things have changed this year. First, in April my older brother was seriously injured in a road accident. He needed several operations, and my family has been paying for private treatment, which the public system could not provide in time. My father has told me that he may have to reduce what he sends me from the new year.

Second, I have lost my housing. The owner of my flat in St. Johann is selling it, and I must leave by the end of November. I will need to pay a deposit on a new room and, most likely, a higher rent than the CHF 1,055 I pay now.

My savings were meant for emergencies, and this year has produced two at once. I am applying to EduAll so that I can finish my degree in July 2028 without asking my family for money they now need for my brother.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

The first time I saw snow was on the Münsterplatz in Basel, in December 2023. I was nineteen, it was eleven at night, and I had just come out of the laboratory after my first successful synthesis. I remember standing there, looking at the snow falling on the cathedral, and thinking that I had come a very long way from my father's shop.

I grew up in Sulaymaniyah, in the Kurdistan Region of Iraq, in a family where nobody had studied beyond secondary school. My father left school at fourteen to support his siblings. My mother never went to school at all. They have always said that my education is their project too.

My childhood was marked by instability. In 2014, when I was ten, the war with ISIS brought thousands of displaced families to our city, and for a year our house was shared with three families of relatives from Mosul. I learned then what it means to lose everything, and how much people depend on each other.

In Basel, I have found a second community. I help organise the Kurdish Students' Society, where we run study groups and welcome events for new students, and I help newly arrived Kurdish families in our neighbourhood with official letters.

My studies have been demanding. My GPA is 2.93, and I have had semesters where I struggled, particularly in physical chemistry, where the German terminology still slows me down. But I have completed 24 courses, and in the laboratory I have found where I belong.

After my bachelor's, I want to do a master's in environmental chemistry and work on water and soil contamination in Kurdistan, where oil production has poisoned land that families have farmed for generations.
