## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

For three years I led the student association of African and Caribbean students at the University of Zurich. I organised welcome days, mentoring, a yearly conference. I was good at helping other people with their problems. I am less good at asking for help with mine. So I will just write the truth.

In March my mother, who raised me in Zurich, was diagnosed with ovarian cancer. She had to stop working. We had to give up our flat, because she could not pay the rent alone any more, and she moved in with a friend. Until then she had supported me, and I had never had a paid job. Since April I work about 15 hours a week at the visitor desk of a museum, and I changed my master's to part-time. I earn CHF 1,708.

My costs are CHF 2,084 a month. I am renting a room for CHF 1,068 until December, and I do not know what comes after. My father in Lagos, a secondary school teacher, sends me CHF 190 out of a household income of about CHF 900. I have CHF 2,855 left in savings, from a prize and from gifts. Every month CHF 186 is missing, and the savings pay it.

I am trying so hard to keep going. I am asking for help to finish, in July 2029, what she wanted so much for me.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

My mother came to Zurich from Lagos when I was nine. She cleaned hotel rooms for twelve years so that I could go to the gymnasium. Every night she asked me what I had learned. She never once asked what I would earn with it.

I studied history because of her stories: about my grandmother, who traded cloth across three colonial borders, about women who organised markets and strikes and whose names are in no archive. My master's research is about Nigerian market women's movements in the 1940s.

I have been active in the African diaspora community in Zurich since I was sixteen, organising history workshops for young people who, like me, grew up here without learning their own history at school.

I want to become a historian and a teacher. And I want my mother to be there when I graduate.
