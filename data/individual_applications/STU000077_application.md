## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

I began the Master's in Psychology at the University of Zurich in February 2026. My monthly costs are CHF 1,782, including CHF 799 for a room and CHF 203 for tuition.

I work three hours a week as a care assistant in a home for elderly people and earn CHF 378. My uncle in Hamburg, who runs a small shop and whose household income is around CHF 3,000, sends me CHF 406. The gap is CHF 998 a month.

My room is a temporary sublet that ends in November. I have been looking for another room since March but have not yet found one I can afford.

In May, my father was detained in Kabul for several weeks. He has now been released, but he lost his job, and my family depends on help from relatives. I will not be able to count on anything from home.

With a grant from EduAll, I could pay a deposit for a new room and continue my studies without taking more hours at work.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

Every Tuesday and Friday I spend my afternoon at a care home in Zürich-Wiedikon. Most residents are very old, some have dementia. There is one woman who was a teacher, and every time I come she asks me to read her a poem. I have learned many German poems this way. She corrects my pronunciation.

I was born in Kabul. My parents did not go to university; my father was a driver, my mother stayed at home. In 2019 I started psychology at Kabul University. In December 2022, women were banned from universities. I remember the day. We came to the gate and it was closed.

I continued with a scholarship programme for Afghan women at a university in Almaty, Kazakhstan, and finished my bachelor's there in 2025. It was far from home, but I was studying again.

I want to be a psychologist for women and girls who have experienced trauma and exclusion. In Afghanistan, millions of girls are at home now, without school. They will need support for many years.

My studies in Zurich have started well. Every week I learn more German, and more about how to help people.
