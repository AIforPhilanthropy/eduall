## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

The Mesopotamian marshes, where the Tigris and Euphrates meet, were once one of the largest wetlands in the world. In the 1990s they were drained almost completely, and a whole way of life disappeared with them. When I was a child in Basra, my grandmother still told stories of the reed houses and the water buffalo. When I finally saw the marshes myself, as an engineer in 2010, they were cracked mud. That image is the reason I study environmental science.

My parents ran a small grocery shop and never studied. I was the first in my family at university, where I studied civil engineering. For six years I worked on water projects in southern Iraq, including a restoration project in the marshes. Two articles came out of that work, on salinity and on water flow in restored areas.

I came to Switzerland in 2012 through marriage. Those years were not easy: my qualifications were not recognised, I worked in cleaning and in kitchens, and the marriage ended badly. I also spent those years volunteering as an Arabic interpreter for a women's shelter in Zurich, which taught me how many people are rebuilding their lives quietly, as I was.

In February 2026 I started my master's in Environmental Science at the University of Zurich, at forty. My first semester, GPA 2.98, was a return to academic work after eighteen years, and I expect my results to rise as I regain my footing.

My goal is to work on wetland restoration and water governance in the Middle East. The marshes are slowly coming back, and they need people who understand both the science and the region.
