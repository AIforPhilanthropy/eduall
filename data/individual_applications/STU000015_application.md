## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

Every month, my costs of studying in Zurich exceed the support available to me by CHF 2,411. That single figure has shaped almost every decision I have made since arriving at ETH, and it is the reason I am writing to EduAll.

My expenses total CHF 2,581 a month. Rent is CHF 1,363 for a studio in Oerlikon, close enough to the Hönggerberg campus that I can walk when I cannot afford the tram. Tuition and semester fees amount to CHF 243 a month, and health insurance to CHF 355. I have kept everything else to the minimum.

My younger brother, a pharmacist in Accra, sends me CHF 170 every month out of a household income of about CHF 1,100. That is a sixth of what his family lives on, and he does it because, for four years, I paid for his own university fees from my teacher's salary. It is a debt he insists on repaying, but it is not one I want to extend.

The Master's in Physics at ETH is a full-time programme in the most literal sense. Laboratory courses, problem sets and my semester project leave very little room for paid work, and the few student jobs I have found clash with compulsory lab sessions. For a semester I have tried to make the numbers work by cutting costs, but I have reached the limit of what cutting can do.

I have three semesters left before completing my degree in January 2028. A grant from EduAll would allow me to finish it with my full attention on physics, rather than on the gap at the end of each month.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

For five years, I taught physics to teenagers in Kumasi, in a school where the laboratory consisted of a single cupboard. We built pendulums from string and bottle caps, measured the speed of sound by clapping against the school wall, and assembled a working spectroscope from a broken DVD. My students called me "Mr Improvise". Some of my first students now study engineering and medicine.

My father was a primary school headmaster and my mother a nurse, and they raised us to believe that knowledge belongs to whoever is willing to work for it. I studied physics at the Kwame Nkrumah University of Science and Technology and always intended to continue, but family responsibilities came first: my salary paid for my younger brother's studies.

When he graduated, I decided that if I did not pursue advanced study then, I never would. I was admitted to the Master's in Physics at ETH Zurich and began in February 2026. The step from a Ghanaian classroom to one of the world's most demanding physics programmes has been enormous, and I have had to rebuild mathematics that I had not used since my bachelor's. I have met that challenge with the same approach I took in Kumasi: patience, repetition and a lot of improvisation.

My semester project, in the group for quantum optics, involves building a low-cost interferometer for teaching purposes, work that connects directly to my years in the classroom. After graduating, I want to return to Ghana and help establish teacher-training programmes in experimental physics, so that no school laboratory has to depend on a single cupboard.

In Zurich, I help run a weekly study group for African students in science and engineering, where newer students can ask the questions they are too embarrassed to ask in lectures.

Education gave me my career once. I want it to give the same to many more students.
