I am entering the second year of my Master's in Physics at EPFL, which I will complete in July 2028 with a thesis in experimental condensed matter physics.

My monthly expenses are CHF 1,806, the largest items being rent (CHF 763 for a room in a shared flat in Renens), food (CHF 303) and EPFL fees (CHF 243). I currently meet these costs through my work as a teaching assistant in first-year physics, which pays CHF 1,166 a month, and a contribution of CHF 643 from my elder brother, who works as an engineer in Germany and supports several members of our family from his income of about CHF 3,000.

I am applying because both sources are about to change. My teaching assistantship is tied to the autumn teaching schedule, and my thesis laboratory will need me full-time from October 2027, including for measurement campaigns at night and at weekends. At the same time, my brother has taken on the costs of our younger siblings' schooling.

An EduAll grant would allow me to give my thesis the full commitment it requires, and to relieve my brother of a responsibility he has carried generously for many years.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

Mazar-i-Sharif is a city of blue domes, dust and pigeons. In the courtyard of the Blue Mosque, thousands of white doves gather every morning, and as a child I used to count them with my father, a carpet trader who never had the chance to finish school. My mother, who learned to read as an adult, taught me the alphabet at the kitchen table.

In my city, few girls and boys from families like mine could hope to study science. My school had one physics teacher for six hundred pupils and no laboratory. What we had were questions, and a teacher who took them seriously.

At seventeen I won a scholarship to study physics at the Middle East Technical University in Ankara, where I completed my bachelor's degree in 2024. Studying in Turkish and English, far from my family, taught me that I can adapt to almost anything. In the autumn of 2025 I began my master's at EPFL.

Lausanne brought a new challenge: French. My courses are taught in English, but everything outside the lecture hall, from the housing office to my part-time job, is in French. I have progressed from beginner level to B1 in one year, and I now tutor first-year students in the language they speak best.

Alongside my studies, I have co-authored a publication on thin-film superconductors and helped found an informal network that supports Afghan students arriving in Switzerland with applications, housing searches and paperwork.

My goal is a doctorate in experimental physics, and ultimately to help build scientific education in Afghanistan when conditions allow. Education, for me, is the courtyard of doves: something that belongs to everyone who is patient enough to count.
