## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

I am a doctoral student in medicine at the University of Basel, in my second semester, researching trauma surgery outcomes in conflict settings. My doctorate is not salaried: it is supported by a scholarship of CHF 5,990 a year from a foundation for scholars displaced by war. My supervisor has confirmed that no paid research position is available for me before 2028.

My monthly expenses are CHF 2,083, including CHF 1,150 for a studio in Basel. My younger brother, an electrician in Gaziantep, sends me CHF 460 out of a household income of about CHF 1,930. He also supports our mother, who in March suffered a stroke and now requires daily care.

An EduAll grant would allow me to continue my research without placing an impossible burden on my brother at a time when our mother's care must come first.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

In twenty years, I hope to be leading a trauma research unit that trains surgeons for hospitals in conflict zones. I know what those hospitals need because I spent years in them without being able to practise as the doctor I had trained to be.

My parents were farmers near Idlib who never went to school. I was the first in our family to go to university. In 2011, when I was completing my final year of medicine in Damascus, the war began. I never received my licence. For the next five years I served without pay in field clinics and camp hospitals, as a volunteer, treating wounds with whatever we had.

In 2016 I reached Switzerland. My medical degree was partly recognised, and after years of language study and examinations I was admitted to a research doctorate in Basel.

My research analyses outcomes of improvised surgical care in Syrian field hospitals. I want the knowledge that was paid for with so much suffering to help future surgeons.
