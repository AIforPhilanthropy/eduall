## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

I started my Bachelor in Business at the University of Geneva in February 2026, so I have just finished my first semester.

My parents support me with CHF 1,560 a month. I want to be clear that my family is not poor by Romanian standards. My mother is a pharmacist and my father is an engineer in Brașov, and together they earn around CHF 5,260 a month. But they are still paying off the mortgage on our flat, and my younger sister started medical school in Cluj this year, which they are also paying for. What they send me is already almost a third of their income, and I don't think it is fair to ask for more.

I do not have a job at the moment. In my first semester I tried to find one, but my French was not good enough for most student jobs, and I decided to concentrate on passing my exams first. I have no savings.

My biggest problem is rent. When I arrived, the only place I could find was a furnished studio in Carouge for CHF 1,420 plus utilities. It is far too expensive for me, and the contract ends in December. Together with food, insurance and other costs I spend CHF 2,199 a month, so I am missing CHF 639 every month.

I am looking for a cheaper room in a shared flat or a student residence, and I am on the waiting list for university housing. If I get a place there, my costs will go down a lot. Until then, a grant from EduAll would cover the gap and let me start looking for a part-time job without the pressure of having to take the first thing I find.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

I chose business because I have seen what happens to a family when a small company fails. My uncle ran a furniture workshop in Brașov with twelve employees. It was a good business with good products, but nobody there understood finance. When a big client paid late, everything collapsed within a year. Twelve families lost their income. I was sixteen and I remember thinking that it did not have to happen.

I want to understand how companies, especially small ones, can be managed so that one bad month does not destroy them. Later I would like to work in advising or financing small and medium enterprises in Eastern Europe, where many businesses are family-run and have very little access to good advice.

My route to university was not direct. At nineteen I had a serious car accident. I spent almost a year in hospitals and rehabilitation and had to learn to walk properly again. I lost my place at university in Bucharest and it took me a long time to believe I could start again. When I finally did, I wanted to do it properly, somewhere that would push me, and I chose Geneva.

My first semester went better than I expected. I finished with a GPA of 3.49 and an average of 91.8, even though I was studying in French, which is my third language. It gave me confidence that I made the right choice.

For me, education is proof that a bad moment does not have to decide the rest of your life. That is true for people and, I believe, also for businesses.
