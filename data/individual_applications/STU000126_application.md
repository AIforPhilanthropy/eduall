## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

My monthly costs in Geneva total CHF 2,080. Today I cover them myself, and I would like to explain why that will stop in September 2027.

I study my Master's in Biology part-time and work about 20 hours a week as a laboratory helper in a biology department, earning CHF 2,241. My parents, who live in Geneva, contribute CHF 280. My father works as a kitchen assistant and my mother cleans offices; together they earn around CHF 5,500, and they have my three younger siblings at home. My rent is CHF 1,166 for a small room, since there is no space for me in my parents' two-room flat.

Since 2024 my parents have also been sending money to our relatives in Gaza, who lost their homes. This year, my grandmother and two cousins were killed. The money my family could spare for me is now going to those who survived.

From September 2027 my thesis in a plant genetics laboratory requires full days, so I will switch to full-time study and leave my job. This would allow me to complete my degree in January 2029 rather than a year later, but it leaves CHF 1,800 a month uncovered. A grant from EduAll would make that final year possible without adding to my parents' burden at the hardest time our family has known.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

I was sixteen when I left Gaza in 2019 to join my father, who had been working in Geneva for several years. I arrived speaking Arabic and school English. Nobody in my family had been to university: my father was a fisherman before the blockade ended his work, and my mother never finished school.

In Geneva I was placed in a welcome class. I learned French in eighteen months and finished the Collège. I studied biology at the University of Geneva, and I am now in my master's, working on drought-resistant wheat. In Gaza, I watched my grandmother grow vegetables in a tiny courtyard with water that was often salty. I want to develop crops that grow where water is scarce and poor.

I have worked since I was seventeen: in a supermarket, in a restaurant, and now in a laboratory. I have learned to study on buses, at night, and in hospital waiting rooms.

The past two years have been the hardest of my life. Every morning I read the news before I read anything else. But I have finished every course, and my GPA is 3.36. I carry my family's hope, and my grandmother's courtyard, with me.
