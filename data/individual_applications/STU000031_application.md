## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

I want to say it directly: at this moment I don't have a monthly deficit. I study Law part-time and I work about 19 hours a week as delivery driver for a pharmacy, where I earn CHF 2,165. My parents live in Lausanne and give me CHF 280 per month. I also have a scholarship of CHF 6,388 per year from a Bulgarian cultural foundation. My expenses are CHF 2,122; my rent is CHF 1,274, because I have a studio near the university. I moved out of my parents' flat because it is small and my younger brother and sister also live there.

So why I apply? In June my father's employer, a construction company in Crissier, went bankrupt. He worked there as foreman for twelve years. Now he receives unemployment money, which is less than his salary, and my parents' income has already dropped. Before, together they earned about CHF 6,960. My mother works as a cleaner in a hospital.

Also, I want to change to full-time study from September 2027, so I can finish in July 2028 and not in January 2029. For this I will drive only on Saturdays, about CHF 900 per month. I calculated it: with my parents' help and the scholarship I can almost manage, but only if my parents continue to send the same.

I am honest that my need is not urgent like for other students. If the committee thinks other students need it more, I accept it. But if my father does not find work, our family will be in a difficult situation, and the grant would protect my last year.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

In ten years I want to be a lawyer who defends workers from Eastern Europe in Switzerland. There are thousands of them: in construction, cleaning, care. Many of them don't know their rights, don't speak French, and accept everything because they are afraid.

I know them because I grew up with them. My father is a civil engineer and my mother a trained primary teacher, but their Bulgarian diplomas were never recognised here. They came to Lausanne from Pleven in 2010 to work, and I stayed with my grandmother in Bulgaria until I was twelve. When I joined them in 2013, I did not speak one word of French. I was put in a class for foreign students, and then in a normal class where I was the one who did not understand anything. I repeated one year. Many teachers thought I will do an apprenticeship at best.

I didn't accept this. I studied French every day, and later I passed the maturité. Since I was sixteen I also worked in summer on construction sites with my father, and later as a delivery driver for a pharmacy. I saw workers not paid for extra hours, working without protection, dismissed with one phone call.

At the university my results are average, I have a GPA of 2.85. Law in French is very difficult with so much text, and I still write slower than my classmates. But I am close to the end. My bachelor thesis is about the protection of posted workers in Swiss labour law.

After my bachelor I want to do the master in law and the lawyer's exam. For me education means that the son of a foreman and a cleaner can be the lawyer in the room.
