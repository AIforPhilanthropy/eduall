## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

For five years before coming to Switzerland, I worked as a technician in a solar power company in Lahore, installing panels on rooftops of schools and clinics in poor districts. I earned a good wage for Pakistan, and I supported my parents and two sisters. When I decided to study physics at EPFL, I promised my family that I would continue to help them.

I keep that promise. I work ten hours a week as a technician at a solar start-up in Renens, earning CHF 1,114 a month. My uncle, an electrical contractor in Lahore (household income around CHF 950), sends me CHF 250. My own costs, CHF 1,630, are covered.

But in May my father suffered a serious stroke. He cannot work, and my mother cares for him full-time. My uncle now also supports them, and part of my own income goes home each month. I am applying so that I can continue to support my family without reducing my study time any further.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

My father drove a rickshaw in Lahore for thirty years. My mother sewed clothes at home. Neither went to school. I was the first in my family to finish secondary school, and I did it while working in an electronics repair shop in the evenings.

At the solar company, I became team leader for a group of eight installers. We installed panels on more than a hundred schools in low-income neighbourhoods. On weekends, I volunteered to teach children in those schools how solar energy works, with small kits I built from spare parts.

In 2022, floods destroyed the homes of many of the families we had worked with. I spent two months helping with emergency power installations in camps.

At twenty-four, I began physics at EPFL. It is extremely demanding, and I am learning to study at a level I have never known. My goal is to become an engineer in renewable energy research and to bring solar power to communities that still live in darkness.
