## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

I am in my second year of the Bachelor's in Chemistry at the University of Geneva, which I study part-time. At forty-two, I am one of the oldest students in my programme, and my financial circumstances reflect a life that has already been long and full of responsibilities.

I work about 20 hours a week as a laboratory assistant for a water-testing company in Vernier and earn CHF 2,273 a month. My wife works as a nursing assistant at the Geneva University Hospitals, and our household income is around CHF 4,400 a month; she contributes CHF 240 towards my share of our costs. My own monthly expenses come to CHF 1,678, with a modest share of our rent of CHF 661 in a cooperative flat in Les Libellules.

The second half of my degree is considerably more laboratory-intensive, with practical courses that take entire afternoons, four days a week. From September 2027 I intend to study full-time, which would allow me to graduate in January 2029 rather than a year later. I will keep one day a week with my employer, which reduces my income to about CHF 1,010.

An EduAll grant would cover the resulting gap and allow me to keep studying at full pace through the laboratory years, without placing the weight on my wife's salary, which also supports her elderly mother in Morocco.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

When I was ten, I spent my summers in the tanneries of Fès, carrying skins between the dye pits for a few dirhams a day. The smell was terrible, and the colours were magical. I remember asking an old tanner why the water in one pit turned red and in the other yellow. He laughed and said "God's chemistry." I wanted to know the chemistry behind God's chemistry.

I left school at fourteen to work full-time; my father was a porter in the medina and my mother could not read. Nobody in my family had studied. For many years I worked in the tanneries and then in a textile factory in Casablanca, where I saw workers lose their health to chemicals that nobody explained to them.

In 2008 I came to Geneva with my wife. I worked in construction and cleaning, learned French and, at thirty-six, began evening classes to obtain my maturité. It took four years. In 2025 I entered the University of Geneva. My first three semesters have gone better than I dared hope: my GPA is 3.64, with an average of 95.1.

For ten years I have been active in the Moroccan association of Geneva, where I organise Arabic literacy classes for adults who, like my mother, never had the chance to learn to read.

My goal is to work in environmental and occupational chemistry, on reducing toxic exposure in industries like the tanneries of my childhood. I would also like to show the young people of my community that it is never too late to start.

Education, for me, is the answer to the question that old tanner could not answer.
