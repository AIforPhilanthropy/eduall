I have no savings, because until now I used everything I earned for my living costs, my laptop and to reduce what my parents must send. The transition period is where I will be in difficulty.

I understand if other students have a more urgent need than me now. But I want to explain that my situation is not as stable as the numbers look.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

How can one person be a good engineer if they never see the people who use their machines? I asked myself this question during my years as a technician in a hospital in Hue, when I saw a dialysis machine which was broken for three months because nobody could repair it and the spare part was too expensive.

I grew up in a small village near the Perfume River, in Thua Thien Hue. Every few years the floods came. In 2015 the water reached the second floor of our house, and we lost almost everything, including all my school books one month before my exams. I studied from photos of a friend's notes on my phone and passed the exam anyway. My parents are teachers, and for them education was always the first thing, even when we had to rebuild the house.

In Bern I study Engineering with focus on biomedical technology. My GPA is 3.77 and average 95.4. I am proud of this, because I study in German and English, both not my mother language. I am also the president of the Vietnamese Students Association in Bern, and I organised this year a workshop about medical device repair together with an NGO.

My dream is to design medical devices which are simple, robust and repairable in low-resource hospitals. In Vietnam and in many countries, expensive machines from Europe stay unused because of one small broken part.

For me education is not only personal success. It is a responsibility to bring back what I learn.
