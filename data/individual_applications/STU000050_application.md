My bachelor thesis on urban biodiversity needs field surveys through the spring, and I cannot do them with 23 hours at the reception. I will keep only the Sunday shifts, about CHF 560 a month, which leaves CHF 1,181 a month uncovered.

An EduAll grant would let me make this switch, find a stable room, and finish my degree a semester earlier.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

My mother has cared for other people's children in Geneva for sixteen years. She has taught three Swiss children to read, to ride a bicycle, to swim. She never went to university, and neither did my father. When I was a child, she used to take me with her to the parks where she worked, and she always said: "One day, you will have your own garden."

I was born in Batangas, in the Philippines, and came to Geneva at twelve to join my parents. I did not speak French. I spent my first year in a welcome class, and I remember the feeling of standing at the edge of every conversation. But I learned fast, and I finished the Collège with good grades.

At university I discovered environmental science, and especially coastal ecosystems. In Batangas, the coral reefs where my grandfather fished are dying. My bachelor's thesis is on the effect of rising water temperature on seagrass meadows, using data from the Philippines.

I am president of the Filipino Students' Association in Geneva, and every year we organise a cultural day for the Filipino community, which is large in Geneva but almost invisible. I also help younger Filipino teenagers with their homework in French.

After my bachelor I want to do a master's in marine ecology, and one day work on coastal protection in South-East Asia.

Education, for me, is the garden my mother talked about.
