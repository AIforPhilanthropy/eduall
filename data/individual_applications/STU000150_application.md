Many everyday tasks take me longer because I have only one hand, and this limits the jobs I can do. Most student jobs in cafés, shops or warehouses require two hands. The reception job is one of the few I can manage.

A grant from EduAll would give me the stability to finish my studies without worrying every month.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

My mother sold peanuts at the bus station in Thiès. I was born without a left hand, and in our neighbourhood some people said this was a punishment. My mother never believed that for one second. She sent me to school, she fought with the teachers who wanted me to stay at home, and she taught me to tie my shoes, carry water and write faster than anyone else in the class.

Nobody in my family had gone to university. My uncle, who is a pharmacist, was the only one who had studied at all. When I finished my baccalaureate early, at seventeen, with the best results in my school, he helped me apply abroad.

In Lausanne I discovered that the world is not made for people like me: doors, tools, laboratory equipment. But I also discovered that it can be changed. I joined a student group on accessibility, and I help organise information sessions for new international students about health insurance and doctors, which is always the first shock.

I want to work in public health on disability and inclusion, especially in West Africa, where children like me are still hidden at home.

My mother died last year. She did not see me finish. I will finish for her.
