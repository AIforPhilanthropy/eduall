## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

Physics matters because it explains the world without asking who you are. In my village near Tambacounda, where my parents farmed peanuts and millet, the world was explained by elders, by God, by tradition. When a teacher showed me, at eleven, why the moon changes shape, using a torch and a football, I felt that the universe had opened a door that nobody could close.

Nobody in my family had gone to school. I was sent to live with an uncle in Dakar for secondary school. I studied physics at university there, but had to stop at twenty-five to work. In 2010 I came to Switzerland, where I worked for many years in hotels and in a watch factory in the Jura, learning precision in a very literal sense.

I also founded a football club for African youth in Fribourg, which I led for eight years, and I volunteer as a tutor for mathematics at a community centre.

In 2025 I resumed my studies. My professors have been very supportive, and my thesis on thin superconducting films has already produced a co-authored publication.

After graduating, I want to do a PhD and later teach physics, ideally in Senegal, where universities need more trained staff. Every child should have the chance to see the moon explained with a torch and a football.
