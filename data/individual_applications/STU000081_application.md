## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

When I was eight, the power in Shatila camp went out for three days. My neighbour, an old man who had repaired radios all his life, sat me down with a broken radio, a battery and a torch, and showed me how to make the radio speak again. I felt like a magician.

I grew up in the Shatila refugee camp in Beirut, where my grandparents arrived in 1948. My parents never went to university. My father is an electrician, my mother cleans offices. I went to UNRWA schools, where classes had fifty students and computers were a luxury.

When I was fifteen, I joined a coding club run by an NGO, and I discovered programming. I started volunteering there as a mentor for younger children at sixteen, and I still do it online on Saturdays. I also worked in a phone repair shop after school to help my family.

I came to EPFL with an admission based on my baccalaureate results. It is the first time in my life that I have a desk of my own.

I want to specialise in computer networks and later build affordable internet infrastructure for refugee camps and poor neighbourhoods. Connection is not a luxury. It is how children like me find a way out.
