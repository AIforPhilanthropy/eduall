## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

I am in the third semester of my Bachelor's in Physics at the University of Zurich, and I expect to graduate in January 2031.

My monthly expenses total CHF 2,047, of which rent accounts for CHF 977, food for CHF 368 and university fees for CHF 203. The remaining costs are health insurance, public transport, phone and study materials.

Until this spring, I worked twenty hours a week in a warehouse and covered almost all of my costs myself. In April, my father suffered a serious stroke in Senegal. I travelled home, and after my return I moved to part-time study and reduced my warehouse work to about 16 hours a week, in order to catch up on my courses and to support my family remotely, by phone and administratively, in arranging his care. I now earn CHF 1,777. My sister in Dakar, whose household lives on around CHF 900, sends CHF 120. This leaves a monthly gap of CHF 150.

The gap is small, but it exists only because I work every hour I can. My remaining nine semesters include laboratory courses and a bachelor thesis that require time I cannot give while working long shifts. An EduAll grant would allow me to complete my degree on schedule while my family recovers from this crisis.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

I am twenty-nine, I grew up in Thiès, in Senegal, and I study physics because I have always wanted to understand how the sun could be made to work for people.

In Thiès, the electricity cut out almost every evening. I did my homework by the light of a kerosene lamp, and I remember wondering why a country with so much sunshine had so little light. That question still drives me.

I came to Switzerland in 2015, at eighteen, to join my mother, who worked in Zurich as a caregiver. My first years here were devoted to learning German and securing a vocational qualification as a logistics specialist, which allowed me to support myself. For several years I worked in warehouses in Zurich, often in early shifts, while preparing for the university entrance examination in the evenings.

In 2025 I began my physics studies at the University of Zurich. Returning to mathematics after years in logistics was demanding, but I have completed nine courses, and this year I was able to join a research seminar on photovoltaic materials.

The past months have tested me. My father's stroke brought the distance between Zurich and Senegal painfully close. Yet I kept up with my courses, and my determination to finish has only grown.

My aim is to pursue a master's degree in energy science and to work on solar technologies adapted to West African conditions. Education, for me, is the light I did not have in the evenings in Thiès.
