## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

I want to be fully transparent about my situation, including its uncertainties.

I began a doctorate in international law at the University of Zurich in September 2025. My doctoral project is funded by a scholarship for scholars at risk of CHF 5,051 a year, which covers only a small part of my costs. My supervisor has confirmed in writing that the chair has no salaried position available before 2028. I have no other employment at present.

My monthly expenses are CHF 2,552, higher than I would like. My studio costs CHF 1,407 plus CHF 140 in utilities; I took it at short notice when I arrived, and I am on waiting lists for university housing. My health insurance is CHF 357. My uncle in Amman, a shopkeeper (household income around CHF 880), sends me CHF 250.

The gap is CHF 2,302 a month before the scholarship. I have no savings. I recognise that this is a very large gap, and that my housing choice has made it larger.

An EduAll grant would allow me to continue my doctoral research while I secure cheaper housing and seek additional funding.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

In my first month in Switzerland, I visited the library of the International Committee of the Red Cross in Geneva and held, for the first time, the original printed volumes of the Geneva Conventions' commentaries. I had studied them for years from photocopies of photocopies in Gaza. I sat in the reading room and cried.

I grew up in Rafah. My father was a construction worker and my mother never went to school. I studied law at Al-Azhar University in Gaza and worked for eight years at a human rights organisation, documenting violations of international humanitarian law. I co-authored three reports and articles on the protection of civilians.

In 2024, after our office was destroyed and several colleagues were killed, I was evacuated with the help of an international scholars' network.

My doctoral research examines the legal protection of civilian infrastructure, water, electricity and hospitals, in armed conflict.

I want to teach international law and continue the work my colleagues can no longer do.
