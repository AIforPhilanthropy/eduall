## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

For three years I worked as a microbiologist at the Central Public Health Laboratory in Kabul. I tested water after floods, stool samples during cholera outbreaks, blood during measles epidemics. In August 2021, I went to the laboratory for the last time. Women were no longer allowed to work there.

I want to be transparent about my finances. My costs are CHF 2,312 a month. My studio costs CHF 1,351; it is expensive, but it was the only place I could find quickly when I arrived. I earn CHF 302 a month as a laboratory helper, a few hours a week. My brother, a doctor in Mashhad, sends me CHF 120 out of a household income of about CHF 760.

I have CHF 10,892 of savings. They are what I was able to bring out of Afghanistan, the value of my apartment in Kabul, which my brother sold for me. Every month I take CHF 1,890 from it. It will last about six months.

In May my sister, who is still in Kabul with her three daughters, lost her husband in an explosion. She has no income. I now send her money every month, from those same savings.

I am studying for a master's that lasts until January 2028. I do not see how I will get there without help.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

My father was a professor of chemistry at Kabul University, and my mother a teacher. They raised me to believe that knowledge belongs to women as much as to men. I studied biology in Kabul and did my master's in India on a scholarship. At the Central Public Health Laboratory, I trained young technicians, most of them women, and published two papers on waterborne diseases.

After 2021, I stayed at home for two years, teaching my nieces in secret. In 2023 I received a place in a programme for Afghan scientists and came to Switzerland.

French is my fourth language, and every lecture is exhausting. But I understand the science, and my supervisor trusts me.

I want to continue research on waterborne diseases, and to train the women scientists that Afghanistan will need again one day.
