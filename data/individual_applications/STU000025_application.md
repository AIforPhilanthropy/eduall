## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

I will describe my finances plainly, including the parts that may count against me.

I study Data Science part-time at the University of Lausanne and work about 12 hours a week as a data-entry assistant at a medical laboratory, where I earn CHF 1,315 a month. My monthly expenses are CHF 1,956, of which CHF 1,012 is rent for a studio in Chailly and CHF 245 is health insurance. My younger brother, an accountant in Karachi whose household lives on about CHF 1,040, sends me CHF 270. The monthly shortfall is CHF 371.

I do have savings of CHF 5,347, the remainder of what I earned in my first years in Switzerland, when I worked full-time in a hotel laundry. I have been drawing on them to cover the gap. I have not received any other scholarship; the university's social fund gave me a one-off payment last year, which I used for a laptop that can run the software my courses require.

At my current pace, my degree would take until January 2030. From September 2027 I would like to study full-time and stop working, because the final part of the programme includes a practical data project with a partner organisation that is incompatible with my job. Without my salary, my monthly gap would become CHF 1,686. An EduAll grant would allow me to make this switch, finish in January 2029, and keep a small margin for the months between graduation and my first job.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

When does a life actually begin? For me, I think it began at thirty-three, in a small office of a women's counselling centre in Lausanne, when a social worker asked me what I would like to do if nobody else's opinion mattered. Nobody had ever asked me that before.

I grew up in Hyderabad, in Sindh. I was a good student, especially in mathematics, but I was married at nineteen and my education stopped there. For many years my life was organised around my husband's family and their decisions. When we moved to Switzerland for his work in 2019, I spoke no French, had no friends, and depended completely on him. The marriage was not a safe place. In 2020 I left, with the help of that counselling centre.

The years after that were difficult: temporary housing, legal procedures, and work in a hotel laundry, where I folded sheets for eight hours a day and practised French with my colleagues. But I also started, slowly, to remember who I had been before, the girl who loved numbers.

In February 2025 I began a Bachelor's in Data Science at the University of Lausanne. French is still my fourth language and many courses are a struggle for me, but I have completed 15 courses with a GPA of 3.1. I am proudest of a project in which I analysed data on the use of domestic violence helplines in Vaud, which the counselling centre now uses in its reports.

After graduating, I want to work as a data analyst in the public or non-profit sector, helping social services understand who they reach and who they miss. I know from experience how many women never reach anyone.

Education, for me, is the life I was not allowed to have. I intend to live it fully.
