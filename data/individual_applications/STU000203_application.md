I know these numbers look weak. But every exam I have been able to sit, I have passed. My doctors are adjusting my treatment, and this year has been better.

My degree ends in July 2028. I am asking EduAll to help me get there.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

My mother sold pepper soup outside the University of Lagos gates for twenty years. Every day, she watched students walk in and out, and every night she told me: "One day you will walk in." She could not read. My father was a bus driver. Neither of them ever went through any gate like that.

I was born with sickle-cell disease. As a child I was in hospital so often that the nurses knew my name. Doctors told my mother I might not live past twenty. I am twenty-three now.

At school I fell behind every time I was sick, and caught up every time I was well. I became captain of our school football team, and later I coached younger children in our neighbourhood for free, because I knew what it felt like to be on the bench.

In Lagos I worked two years for an environmental NGO, organising plastic clean-ups on the beaches, and I led a youth group of fifty volunteers.

In Lausanne I study environmental science. I coach children's football at a community club, and I help organise a support group for young people with sickle-cell disease in Switzerland.

My mother died last year. She did not see me finish. But I walked in, Mama. I will walk out too.
