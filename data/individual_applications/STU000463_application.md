## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

For many years I worked as a technician in a nuclear power plant in Kozloduy, and later in a medical physics department in Bern. I start with this because it explains also my money situation, which is not bad.

I will be honest. My wife is a pharmacist in Bern and our household income is about CHF 9,500. She pays CHF 1,110 of my costs, which is mostly my part of the rent and the household. I study part-time and work also 30% as a technician in a radiotherapy department, and earn CHF 1,439. My costs are CHF 2,371. So, in the moment, there is no gap.

Why I apply: from September 2027 I want to study full-time, because the master thesis in a research group in Bern is full-time. Then I finish in July 2028 and not July 2029. I would reduce my job to one day in two weeks, about CHF 560. Then CHF 701 a month is missing. My wife can pay more, but we send also money to my parents in Bulgaria, both have small pensions and my father has heart problems. We have no savings, because the move to Switzerland and my first years of study took all.

I understand very well if other students have bigger needs. If EduAll can help with even a part, it would let me finish my thesis without worry.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

I was born in Vratsa, in the north-west of Bulgaria. My father worked in a cement factory, my mother was a cook in a school. Nobody in my family studied at university.

After technical school I worked at the Kozloduy nuclear plant, in radiation protection. I measured doses, calibrated instruments. I was good, but I always wanted to know why, not only how. In 2012 the plant reduced staff, and I lost my job. It was a difficult time, I had no work for one year and I was ashamed to tell my parents.

In 2014 my wife got a job in Bern, and I followed her. I did not speak German. I cleaned in a hospital for two years, and then, because of my experience, I got a position as technician in radiotherapy. There, the physicists explained me many things, and one of them said: "You should study physics. You already think like one."

At 37 I went back to school. I had only the Bulgarian technical diploma, so I did first the passerelle and bridging courses in physics, and in 2024 the university accepted me in the master, with some bachelor courses added. It was hard to study again after twenty years. I have now 5 courses. My grades are okay, not the best, GPA 3.18, but I am proud.

I want to become a medical physicist in radiotherapy. For me, education came late, but it came.
