## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

I will be honest with you about everything, the good and the difficult.

My costs are CHF 2,266 a month. I earn CHF 1,183 as a night receptionist in a hotel near Cornavin, four nights a week. My older sister, a seamstress in Kumasi, gives me CHF 180, a large share of the roughly CHF 1,080 the household has. I have CHF 1,078 in savings. So the gap is CHF 903 a month. I know this is smaller than for many others.

But I am tired. In 2023 I was diagnosed with multiple sclerosis. Some weeks my legs are weak and my eyes blur, and after a night shift I need a whole day to recover. My neurologist has told me that the night work is making my condition worse, and that I should stop. If I stop, the gap becomes much larger. If I don't stop, I may not finish.

And in May my younger brother's kidneys failed. He needs dialysis three times a week in Kumasi, and my sister and I are paying for it. My savings will go there.

My master's thesis is due in July 2028. I am asking EduAll to help me leave the night shifts for the last months, so that I can finish my degree before my body forces me to stop.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

When I was seven, I stammered so badly that the other children laughed whenever I opened my mouth. A teacher, Mr. Owusu, kept me after school every day for a year and made me read aloud to him, slowly, one sentence at a time. He never laughed. By the end of the year I could read a whole page. I have wanted to be that kind of person for someone else ever since.

My parents are a teacher and a nurse. I studied psychology in Accra and worked for seven years as a counsellor in a secondary school, and later as head of the student counselling unit, leading a team of five. I came to Geneva in 2026 for my master's, to train in clinical psychology.

French is my greatest difficulty. I understand lectures, but writing clinical reports in French takes me twice as long. My results are nevertheless very good, with a GPA of 3.7.

The diagnosis of MS changed my life. But it has also made me a better listener. I know now what it is like to fear your own body.

I want to work as a clinical psychologist with people living with chronic illness, and one day return to Ghana, where psychological support for them hardly exists.
