## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

Let me introduce myself with the truth about my money, because I think honesty is the only fair way to apply.

My parents, who own three bakeries in Plovdiv, earn around CHF 3,660 a month and send me CHF 650. I have a small scholarship of CHF 3,553 a year from a Bulgarian physics foundation and earn CHF 239 from occasional tutoring. My costs are CHF 2,070. Every month CHF 1,181 is missing, and my scholarship covers only a small part of it. Until now my parents covered the difference with extra transfers when the bakeries went well.

But in May my mother was diagnosed with breast cancer. She is having chemotherapy in Sofia, and my father is running the bakeries alone while driving her to treatment. One bakery has already closed. They tell me not to worry. I worry every night.

I started my doctorate in physics in February 2026. My position is not salaried yet; my supervisor expects a grant decision next year. I want to be able to tell my parents, truthfully, that they can stop sending me money, so that everything goes to my mother.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

I am twenty-six, a physicist, and the first person in my family to go to university. My parents left school at sixteen to bake bread. They built their business loaf by loaf, and they built my education the same way.

As a teenager I volunteered at the Plovdiv planetarium on weekends, guiding school groups, and I worked every summer in our bakeries, at four in the morning.

In Fribourg my research is on quantum sensors. French is still very difficult for me; I understand physics, but seminars and administration in French leave me exhausted.

My mother always said that bread and knowledge are the two things you must share. I want to finish what she helped me start.
