## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

I will tell you everything, even the savings.

I have CHF 7,398 in my account. I worked for six years as a laboratory scientist in a hospital in Lagos, and I saved half of my salary for this master. I thought it would be enough, with a small job.

My costs in Zurich are CHF 1,830 per month. My room in a student house is CHF 655, one of the cheapest in Zurich. I work in the ETH library four hours a week and earn CHF 491. My younger brother, a bank clerk in Ibadan, gives me CHF 170, a large share of the roughly CHF 930 the household has.

That leaves CHF 1,169 missing every month, which my savings cover for now. But in April, my mother had a stroke. She is paralysed on one side and needs a carer. My brother and I share the cost. My savings will go there. I cannot let her lie alone.

I began my master's in February 2026. It lasts until July 2028. I am asking EduAll to help me stay, so that my mother's care and my future do not have to fight each other.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

The first time I saw an electron microscope at ETH, in my first week, I stood in front of it like a child in front of a Christmas tree. In Lagos, our hospital laboratory had one old light microscope for three technicians. We diagnosed malaria, tuberculosis, typhoid by eye, and sometimes we were wrong. I always wondered how many lives we lost because of it.

My parents, a pharmacist and a teacher, always told me to learn everything I can. At the hospital I also started a volunteer programme teaching hand hygiene in primary schools near the hospital, and I was active in the women's group of my church, organising health talks.

My master's research is on antimicrobial resistance in hospital bacteria. After my master, I want to go back to Nigeria and build a modern diagnostic laboratory.

My mother always said I would do great things. Now I want her to see them.
