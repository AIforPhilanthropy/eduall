## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

My monthly costs in Zurich are CHF 2,168. My parents send me CHF 479; the gap is CHF 1,689. I have no savings and no job at the moment.

I want to explain why I don't work. I have rheumatoid arthritis. On bad days my hands and knees are swollen and painful, and I cannot write for long or stand. The lecture days at ETH already use most of my energy. I tried a job in a café in my first semester, but I had to stop after six weeks because of a flare. Office jobs with fixed hours are difficult because my good and bad days are not predictable.

My parents are both engineers in Hanoi, and they earn about CHF 1,720 together. What they send me is more than a quarter of their income. They also pay for my grandmother's care.

My health insurance is CHF 410 a month, and my medication has a high deductible. My rent is CHF 954 for a room close to the ETH, so I do not need to travel far on bad days.

I will be honest: I am not sure how long I can continue like this. My bachelor lasts until July 2028. The first year went well academically (GPA 3.51), but financially I am living month by month.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

My father taught me to count with a Chinese abacus when I was four. He was an engineer at a bridge construction company, and he loved numbers the way other people love music. On Sunday mornings he gave me problems to solve, and a small cake for each right answer. I became very good at mathematics, and a bit too fond of cake.

I studied electrical engineering in Hanoi and worked two years as an engineer in a power company. At twenty, I was diagnosed with rheumatoid arthritis. For a long time I tried to ignore it, working long hours on construction sites and control rooms. My health got worse. My doctor told me I needed work that was kinder to my body.

I decided to follow my first love, pure mathematics. At twenty-four, I started again as a first-year student at ETH. It is the most beautiful and the most difficult thing I have done.

I am interested in number theory and cryptography. I would like to become a researcher, perhaps in cryptography, where I can work from a desk, at my own rhythm, and still do something useful.
