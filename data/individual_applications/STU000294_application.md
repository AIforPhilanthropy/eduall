## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

For two years before coming to Switzerland, I worked in my parents' furniture export business in Jepara, handling the paperwork for shipments to Europe. That is where I first read contracts, and where I first saw how much depends on the fine print.

I am in the second year of my Bachelor of Law at the University of Bern. My costs are CHF 2,357 a month, including a studio for CHF 1,169 and CHF 140 utilities. My parents, who never went to university but built their business from a single workshop, earn around CHF 1,300 and give me CHF 230. I also work as a receptionist in a law firm on Saturdays and earn CHF 645.

My parents' business is under pressure this year, because new European rules on timber origin have stopped several of their contracts. A grant from EduAll would allow me to continue my studies without relying so heavily on them during this difficult year.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

When I was fourteen, a fire destroyed my parents' workshop in Jepara. Everything burned: the machines, the wood, the orders. My father sat in front of the ashes for a whole night. Then he began again, with borrowed tools. It took five years, but he rebuilt the business, bigger than before.

I learned from him that setbacks are temporary if you keep going.

I study law because I want to work in international trade law, helping small producers in countries like Indonesia to understand and meet the rules of foreign markets, instead of being pushed out by them.

German is my third language, and legal German is a challenge, but I have passed every exam so far.
