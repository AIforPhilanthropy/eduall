## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

I am in my second year of the Bachelor in Biology at the University of Basel, and I am also the mother of a one-year-old boy. Since he was born I have studied part-time.

I work about 17 hours a week as a laboratory helper and earn CHF 1,936. My parents, both engineers in Basel, earn about CHF 6,090 and give me CHF 390 a month; they also pay part of my son's crèche, which is not in my budget. My own costs are CHF 1,644.

From September 2027 I want to study full-time again, so that I can finish in July 2029 instead of a year later. The third year has laboratory courses until evening and a bachelor project, and I will keep only a few hours at the lab, about CHF 450 a month. Then CHF 804 a month are missing. My parents already take care of my son two afternoons a week, and my father will retire next year. I would like to be able to pay for more hours of childcare myself, so that my studies do not rest entirely on my parents.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

When I was seven, in Cluj, my grandfather took me to the botanical garden every Sunday. He knew the Latin name of every tree. He said that plants are the most patient teachers in the world. I believed him.

At eighteen, just before my first semester, I found out I was pregnant. Everybody told me to stop studying. My mother said: "No. You will do both, and we will help." My son was born in the summer, and I went back in September. I studied during his naps and in the laboratory while he was at the crèche.

I have also volunteered for two years at the Basel botanical garden, guiding school classes, and I work in a research laboratory.

My project is about how plants communicate stress through their roots. I want to become a plant biologist.

I think my grandfather would be proud, and my son already points at every tree we pass.
