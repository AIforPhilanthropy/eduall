## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

I will be transparent about my situation, including where I could have done things differently.

My monthly expenses in Zurich are CHF 2,317. My room costs CHF 1,164, which is more than I should pay; I took it in my first week because I was afraid of having nowhere to sleep, and I have been on waiting lists for student housing since. ETH tuition adds CHF 243 a month.

I earn CHF 477 a month as a tutor for first-year chemistry students. My father, a tailor in Patna (household income around CHF 430), sends me CHF 80, which is almost a fifth of what my family lives on. The gap is CHF 1,760 every month. I have no savings: I came to Switzerland with a loan from relatives, which I am still repaying from my tutoring income.

I could have chosen a cheaper university town. I chose ETH because it offered me admission and because its biochemistry programme is among the best in the world. I do not regret it, but I know it makes my need larger.

A grant would allow me to move into cheaper housing when a room becomes available, and to complete my degree by July 2028.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

On my first night in Zurich, in September 2025, I walked from the Hauptbahnhof to the ETH main building and stood in front of it at midnight. I had seen it only on a phone screen, in a cyber café in Patna. I remember thinking that my father had never seen a building like this in his life, and that I was standing in front of it for both of us.

I come from a Dalit family in Bihar. My father is a tailor, my mother makes bangles at home. Neither went to school beyond a few years. At school I was often made to sit apart from the other children. Some teachers refused to check my notebooks. I learned early that I would have to be twice as good to be treated as equal.

I was. I finished school as the top student in my district in chemistry and biology, and I founded a free study circle for Dalit children in our neighbourhood, which I led for three years. I worked in a pharmacy for two years after school to save money for my applications.

At ETH I study biochemistry. My GPA is 3.45. I am interested in enzymes that could make medicines cheaper to produce. In Bihar, many people cannot afford even basic drugs.

For me education is the only thing in India that caste cannot fully take away.
