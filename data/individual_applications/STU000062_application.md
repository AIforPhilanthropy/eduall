## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

What does it mean to start a scientific career at thirty-six? For many people it sounds too late. For me, it was the first moment it became possible.

I came to Geneva at nine with my parents, who emigrated from Vietnam to join my uncle. I grew up here, went to school here, and I have a C permit. My father was an engineer in Saigon; here he worked in a factory.

When I was nineteen, my mother developed early-onset dementia, and a few years later my father had a stroke. My brother had just started his business, so I stayed home and cared for both of them, for sixteen years. I never had a paid job until 2024, when my brother gave me work in his salon. At night, I read physics: first popular books, then textbooks, then online university courses.

After my parents died, in 2018 and 2020, I started a bachelor in physics at the University of Geneva, and now the master. I published my first co-authored article this year, on optical properties of thin films.

During the years of care, I was also a volunteer in an association that supports family carers in Geneva, first as a participant and then as a group facilitator. I still help there once a month.

I want to work in optics or photonics research. Physics gave me something to hold on to in years when my life was very small. Now I want to give it my full time.
