## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

One day I want to open a practice in Cairo where people can speak about their mental health without shame, and pay what they can. That is a long way from here. But this application is about the next year and a half, so let me be honest about where I am.

Right now my income covers my costs. I earn CHF 1,180 a month as a night receptionist in a hotel in Bern, and my older sister in Zurich sends me CHF 790 from her household income of about CHF 4,800. My costs are CHF 1,625. I know that on paper this looks comfortable.

It is not comfortable, because the money from my sister is not really for me. In June my father had a stroke in Alexandria. He cannot work any more and he needs care at home. My sister sends me the money, and I send most of it on to my mother, for the nurse and the medicines. So what is left for my own life is my salary, and some months not even that.

I have no savings and no scholarship. I do not want to leave my studies, and I do not want to stop helping my parents. Your support would let me do both without working more nights. I already work the fifteen hours a week my permit allows, mostly nights, and my body tells me it is enough.

I will understand if you decide that other students need it more.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

I was not a good student at school in Alexandria. I was a good listener. My friends came to me with everything, and I did not know then that this could be a profession.

At twenty-two I finished a degree in commerce because my father wanted it. I worked three years in a bank and I was very unhappy. When I came to Switzerland in 2021 to join my sister, I decided I would try, finally, what I wanted. I learned German, first in courses and then from the guests at the hotel, and I volunteered on the telephone of a helpline for Arabic-speaking people in crisis. There I understood: I want to do this with training, not only with heart.

My first three semesters in psychology have been the happiest time I ever had as a student. I love statistics, which surprises everyone.

This summer was the hardest time. When my father had his stroke, I flew to Alexandria and I sat by his bed for ten days. He could not speak. I held his hand and told him I am studying psychology, not commerce any more. I think he smiled. I came back for my exams, and I passed all of them.

Education for me means that I can one day give people what I could not give my father: someone who knows how to help.
