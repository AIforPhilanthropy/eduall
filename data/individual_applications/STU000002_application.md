## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

I began my Master's in Biology at the University of Lausanne in February 2026, after four years of working to make this possible. I have just completed my first semester, and my thesis is due in July 2028, and I am applying to EduAll so that the last months of this degree are not decided by money.

My monthly costs in Lausanne come to CHF 2,479. The largest item is rent: CHF 1,299 for a small studio in Renens, plus CHF 119 for utilities. I pay CHF 290 for health insurance, and the rest goes on food, transport and the basic materials my laboratory work requires. I have kept these costs as low as the Lausanne housing market allows; shared flats near the campus rarely accept tenants without a Swiss guarantor, and I have been turned away more than once for that reason.

Against these costs, I earn CHF 414 a month as a part-time technician in a teaching laboratory on campus, preparing cultures and reagents for bachelor practicals. My two sisters in Lahore, whose households together earn about CHF 1,160, send me CHF 266 each month. It is a generous gesture from families who have children of their own in school, and I do not want to ask them for more.

The difference between what I have and what I need is CHF 1,799 each month. What I brought with me, my own savings and the money my family raised by selling a plot of land, is being drawn down steadily, and it was always meant to carry me through the degree, not beyond it.

My thesis project, on antibiotic resistance genes in wastewater bacteria, requires long days in the lab during the final semester. I cannot extend my working hours without compromising it. An EduAll grant would allow me to finish the thesis with the focus it deserves and to complete my degree on time.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

When I was seven, my grandmother showed me a drop of pond water under a toy microscope that my uncle had brought back from Dubai. There were creatures in it, moving, feeding, dividing. I remember asking her how something so small could be alive, and her laughing and saying that most of life is too small to see. I have been trying to see it ever since.

I grew up in Faisalabad, the child of a textile worker and a mother who never learned to read. No one in my family had studied beyond secondary school. I earned a place at university through scholarships, completed a bachelor's degree in microbiology, and then worked for four years as a laboratory technician in a public hospital. I processed thousands of samples a year, and I watched infections that once responded to basic antibiotics stop responding to anything at all.

My path to Switzerland was not a straight one. As a non-binary person in Pakistan, I learned early to make myself small, at university, at work and in public. Many doors were closed to me without explanation. Coming to Switzerland to study was, in part, a decision to stop living that way. It took years of saving, two rejected applications and a great deal of stubbornness.

At UNIL I found an academic community that took me seriously. I have completed 2 courses, and I co-authored a paper on resistance genes in hospital effluent, drawing on methods I first learned in Faisalabad. My master's thesis extends this work to municipal wastewater, where resistant bacteria meet and exchange genes before reaching rivers and fields.

After graduation, I want to work in antimicrobial resistance surveillance, ideally in a programme that connects Swiss research laboratories with hospitals in South Asia. The problem does not respect borders, and the laboratories in countries like mine are where the earliest warnings appear, often unheard.

Education, for me, has been the one place where what I can do matters more than who people assume I am. I intend to make the most of the rest of this degree, and to use what I have learned where it is most needed.
