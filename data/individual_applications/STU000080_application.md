## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

My income is currently higher than my expenses, and my first semester results were poor. I want to state both things plainly.

My spouse, who works part-time as a school bus driver in Lausanne and earns around CHF 2,220 a month, contributes CHF 800 to my share of our costs. I earn CHF 1,199 a month as a pharmacy assistant, working about eleven hours a week. My expenses are CHF 1,605, with my share of our rent at CHF 490. I have no savings.

My GPA after the first semester is 2.0, with an average of 48.4. I failed two of four exams. The main reason is that I underestimated how much time EPFL requires and kept working almost as much as before. I have to retake both exams in the next session, and if I fail again I risk being excluded from the programme.

This is why I am applying. I want to reduce my working hours by half for the next two semesters, to give myself a real chance to pass. That would mean losing about half of my income each month, which our household cannot absorb easily, since my spouse's work is part-time. A grant would replace part of that lost income.

If the committee considers my need too small, or my academic risk too high, I understand.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

For six years I have worked in pharmacies, first in Alexandria and then in Lausanne. I know every medicine by name and by shelf. But I never understood how they worked inside the body. Last year, I decided it was time to learn.

I was born in Alexandria. My parents were a civil engineer and a French teacher, and they always wanted me to study at university. At eighteen, I had to work instead, when my father lost his job. Pharmacy assistant was supposed to be temporary. It became my life for years.

In 2022 I married and came to Switzerland with my spouse. Since then I volunteer with Caritas, accompanying elderly people to medical appointments, and I help at the Egyptian community association, organising the yearly Coptic and Muslim shared iftar and Christmas dinner, which brings our community together.

My first semester at EPFL was a shock. Mathematics I had not practised for six years, physics, chemistry, all at once. I failed. But I have not given up. I have joined a study group, I have a tutor, and I am revising every evening.

My goal is to work in pharmaceutical quality control or research, combining my practical experience with scientific knowledge.

Education, for me, is the thing I postponed for too long. I do not want to postpone it again.
