## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

I want to be truthful about our finances, because I know that on paper we do not look like a family in need.

I study my Master's in Environmental Science part-time, and I work about 21 hours a week as a freelance GIS analyst for an engineering office, mostly from home, and earn CHF 2,341 a month. My husband works as a risk analyst at a bank in Zurich and earns around CHF 9,500 a month; he pays CHF 220 towards my costs. I have savings of CHF 11,714. My own monthly expenses are CHF 2,392. By the usual measure, I have no monthly shortfall, and I will not pretend otherwise.

But in January our daughter, who is nine, was diagnosed with leukaemia. Since then our life has been the children's hospital. Health insurance covers the treatment itself, but not everything around it: the taxis for daily visits, the hours my husband has had to cut, the private help at home for our younger son while we are at the hospital. My savings are going into this, month after month, and so is the money that was meant to carry me through my master's. I work from home because it is the only way to be at the hospital every day.

I did not stop my studies. I thought about it. But my daughter asked me not to. She says she wants to see me graduate.

My master's runs until January 2030. I am applying for my own study costs only, so that my studies do not take money that her care needs. If the committee decides that others need it more, I will understand completely.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

Environmental science matters to me because I have seen what happens when a river dies. I grew up in Ruse, on the Danube. As a child, I swam in it every summer. In 2000, when I was seventeen, a cyanide spill from a mine upstream reached the river, and fish floated on the surface all the way to the Black Sea. My grandfather, a fisherman, cried. I had never seen him cry.

I studied chemistry in Sofia. After graduating, I went back to Ruse to care for my mother, who was ill for many years, and later my own children were born. I never had the chance to work in my field. In 2020 we moved to Zurich for my husband's work. I learned German, joined a parents' association at my children's school, and later helped organise the Bulgarian community's language classes for children. That community was our family in Switzerland.

In February 2026 I started a Master's in Environmental Science at the University of Zurich, focusing on river ecology. One month before, our daughter had been diagnosed. I study in hospital corridors, in waiting rooms, at night next to her bed. My first semester went better than I dared hope: my GPA is 3.33.

My thesis will be on micropollutants in Swiss rivers. I want to work in water protection, maybe for a cantonal office, maybe for the international Danube commission.

My daughter asks me every day what I learned. Explaining it to her is my favourite part of the day.
