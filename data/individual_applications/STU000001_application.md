## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

On paper my monthly budget balances, and I want to be honest about that. My parents send me CHF 220 a month and I earn CHF 816 from my job as a student assistant at the university library, where I work around seven hours a week. My expenses come to CHF 1,745, so in a normal month I have a small margin left.

The problem is that my situation is not stable, and I have no savings at all to absorb any shock. Since I arrived in Geneva I have moved four times. I have always lived in sublets because I could not get a regular lease without a Swiss guarantor or a deposit. My current room costs CHF 704 including utilities, but the sublet ends on 31 October and the main tenant is coming back. The rooms I have found so far ask for a deposit of two or three months' rent up front, which is money I simply do not have. If I do not find something in time, I will be sleeping on a friend's sofa during my final semester.

My parents run a small warung (a family restaurant) in Surabaya. Together they earn about CHF 1,230 a month, and they also support my grandmother and my younger brother, who is still in school. What they send me is almost a fifth of their income, and I know they have cut their own spending to do it. I cannot ask them for more. I also cannot increase my hours at the library, because my permit limits how much I can work during the semester and because I need the time for my bachelor thesis.

I have three semesters left. My bachelor will take ten semesters because I had to take a lighter course load in two semesters when I was working more, and I expect to finish in January 2028. I am applying to EduAll because a grant would let me pay a rental deposit and have a small safety net, so I can concentrate on finishing my degree instead of looking for a place to sleep. I have not received any other scholarship. I applied to the university's social fund last year but was told that the fund only gives one-time help in emergencies, not regular support.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

I grew up in Surabaya, in East Java. Nobody in my family went to university. My parents finished primary school and have run the same small warung for more than twenty years. When I told them I wanted to study public health in Switzerland, they did not really understand what it was, but they supported me anyway.

My interest in public health started in 2019, when there was a dengue outbreak in our neighbourhood. My younger cousin was hospitalised, and I saw how little information people had about prevention. Standing water, blocked drains, no one explaining anything. It made me realise that many health problems are not about hospitals but about organisation, information and trust.

At the University of Geneva I have completed 26 courses, and my average is 80.1 out of 100 (GPA 3.25). My favourite subjects were epidemiology and health promotion. My bachelor thesis looks at how migrant communities in Geneva use community health workers, and I hope to continue with a master's in global health.

Outside of class, I have been the president of the Indonesian Students' Association in Geneva for the last year. We organise welcome events for new students, help people find housing and translate documents. Because I have moved so often myself, I have become the person others ask for help with housing, which is a little ironic. Once a month I also organise a cooking evening in my neighbourhood in Pâquis, where Indonesian students and our Swiss and migrant neighbours cook together.

Studying here has not always been easy. The housing situation has been the hardest part, more than the courses or the language. But I have learned to plan, to ask for help and to help others.

For me, education means that my family's story can change. I want to go back to Indonesia and work on community health programmes, especially in poorer urban areas like the one I grew up in. EduAll's support would help me finish this last step properly.
