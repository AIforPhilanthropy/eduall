## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

I would like to describe my situation openly, including where it is my own responsibility.

I am in the first year of the Master's in Physics at the University of Geneva. My monthly expenses are CHF 2,317, most of which is rent: CHF 1,385 for a studio. My parents, who run a small furniture workshop in Jepara and earn about CHF 1,110, send me CHF 270. I work ten hours a week as a sales assistant in an electronics store and earn CHF 1,128. This leaves CHF 919 a month uncovered, which I have closed until now with a loan from a cousin in Jakarta.

I must also be candid about my first semester, which was weak: my GPA is 2.56. I was working too many hours while adjusting to French and to the level of the courses. I plan to reduce my hours in the second year, which will create a gap in my budget.

My parents' business has also suffered from falling export orders, and they have warned me they may need to reduce their support.

If the committee judges that other applicants need help more urgently, I will understand.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

My mother never went to school. She spent her life carving wooden panels in my father's workshop, and her hands are covered in small scars. When I was ten, she held my hand and said: "Your hands should hold books, not chisels." I have carried that sentence with me ever since.

In 2018, when I was fifteen, the workshop burned down. We lost everything. My father rebuilt it over three years, and I helped every afternoon after school.

I studied physics in Yogyakarta, where I was president of the physics students' association and volunteered teaching science to girls in rural schools. I also worked as a laboratory assistant.

French is my fourth language, and I am still learning it.

I want to study renewable energy materials, and one day bring solar power to villages like mine.
