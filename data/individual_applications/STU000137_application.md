## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

I study Law at the University of Bern, and I have four semesters left in the bachelor. I will finish in July 2028.

My costs are CHF 2,269 per month. My studio costs CHF 1,175 and CHF 140 for utilities. I chose it because I was seventeen when I arrived and my family wanted me to live alone and safely, not in a big shared flat. My uncle in Port Harcourt, a primary school teacher, contributes CHF 219, although the whole household lives on roughly CHF 760.

I also receive a scholarship of CHF 4,390 per year from a Nigerian women's lawyers' association, which helped me to come here.

I don't work, because the law programme in German takes all my time, and last year I started the preparation for the master's entrance.

Every month, CHF 2,050 is missing from what I need. The grant would allow me to finish my bachelor without stress and to start the master in law.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

Port Harcourt is the oil capital of Nigeria. The oil makes a few people very rich, and the rest of the city lives next to the gas flares and the black creeks. My family lived in a waterfront settlement. When I was eleven, the government demolished it to build a road. Thousands of families lost their homes in one morning. My mother, a fish seller, lost her stall and everything in it. Nobody gave us any compensation.

A lawyer from a human rights organisation came to our new neighbourhood to help the families. She was a young woman, and she spoke to the officials without fear. I decided that day that I would be like her.

Nobody in my family had gone to university. I studied very hard, and at sixteen I won a national essay competition on human rights. The prize, together with support from my community church, which collected money for me for two years, made it possible to come to Switzerland. I learned German in one intensive year.

In Bern I am part of the African students' association, where I organise legal information sessions for new migrants.

I want to become a human rights lawyer and work on land and housing rights in Nigeria.
