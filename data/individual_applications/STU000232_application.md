## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

I will start with the hardest part, because I think you need to know it first. At the moment my residence permit is not valid. I came to Basel in 2023 with a student B permit. When I applied to renew it this spring, the cantonal migration office asked me to prove that I have around CHF 2,600 every month. I cannot prove this. My renewal is still pending, and a free legal counselling service is helping me, but until there is a decision I live in a grey zone: I am still enrolled, but I am not allowed to work and I have no valid permit card. I understand this may make my application difficult, and I am writing to you anyway, because I have nobody else to ask.

Every month I need CHF 2,101 to live and study here. My older brother and his wife in Amman send me CHF 370. He is a driver and she is a nurse, and together they earn about CHF 1,480, so they give me a quarter of what they have, while raising three children. Apart from this I have nothing. No savings, no salary, no scholarship.

The biggest cost is my room: CHF 1,152 with utilities, a small studio in Kleinbasel. I know it is expensive. I moved there after the shared flat where I lived was sold, and with a pending permit almost no one would sign a lease with me. My health insurance is CHF 225. I pay it every month before anything else, because I am afraid to be without it.

Every month there is a gap of CHF 1,731. I have covered it with small loans from friends and by eating very little at the end of the month. I cannot continue like this. And there is a cruel circle: without money I cannot get my permit, and without my permit I cannot work to earn money. A grant from EduAll would count as proof of means for the migration office. It could break this circle and let me finish my degree in July 2028.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

What do you do when a child in front of you cannot read, and you do not know how to help? This question has followed me since I was a child.

I was born in Nablus, in the West Bank. My father drove a taxi and my mother cleaned houses. Neither of them finished school, but my mother always said the same sentence to me: "They can take your house, but not what you know." I did not understand it when I was a child. I understand it now.

All through secondary school, before I came to Switzerland, I helped as a volunteer in the primary school of our neighbourhood. I was not a teacher and I was not paid. I was the one who stayed after class with the children who could not read. Some of them were eleven years old and still could not write their name. I felt so helpless. I wanted to learn how to really teach them, not only to be kind to them. This is why I chose Education, and why I came to Basel, where there is a strong tradition in pedagogy and inclusive schooling.

I will be honest: my studies have been very difficult. I arrived with good English but almost no German, and most of my courses are in German. In the first years I understood maybe half of the lectures. I recorded them and listened again at night with a dictionary. My GPA is 2.34 and my average is 62.3, and I know these are not good numbers, and that they are below what you normally ask. But I have completed 24 courses, and every one of them I passed by fighting for it. My German is much better now, and my grades in the last two semesters were my best.

Since 2023 I volunteer every Saturday at a homework café for refugee children in Kleinbasel. Many of them are like the children I left in Nablus: clever, tired, and lost in a language that is not theirs. When one boy from Eritrea read a full page in German for the first time, he looked at me like he won something. I cried on the tram home. That is the moment I knew I was in the right field.

Education, for me, is not a career plan only. It is the thing my mother told me nobody can take away. Right now my permit, my security and my plans are all uncertain. What is left is four semesters. I am asking EduAll to help me finish it, so that I can become a real teacher for children who start life with less, like I did.
