## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

I would like to be transparent about my finances, because they are unusual for an EduAll applicant. At the moment my household can cover my costs, and I also have savings of CHF 10,320.

My husband works as a sous-chef in a hotel in Lausanne and earns around CHF 4,790 a month. He contributes CHF 1,280 to my part of our costs. I earn CHF 764 as a part-time drafting assistant for a small architecture office in Renens. My personal expenses come to CHF 1,649 a month. My share of the rent is only CHF 488, because we live in a subsidised family flat.

So why do I apply? Our savings are the money we put aside, over many years, for our two children. We have no family in Switzerland to help if anything happens, and my husband's contract is renewed every season. My last semester at EPFL is also the most expensive one: the diploma project requires models, large-format printing and site visits, and the studio hours make it impossible to keep working at the same level. From October I will reduce my job to a few hours a month.

I understand that other applicants may be in more urgent need. If the committee decides to support me, the grant would let me finish my diploma project in July 2028 without touching our children's savings.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

My first job in architecture was carrying cement bags. At nineteen, in Surabaya, I worked on a construction site because my family needed the money. My father was a fisherman, my mother sold vegetables at the market, and none of us had been to university. On that site I watched a young architect walk through the half-finished building, pointing at things, changing things. I remember thinking: I want to be the person who decides.

It took me a long time. I worked as a draftswoman in an architecture office in Jakarta for eight years, learning everything by watching and asking. I married, and we had our first child. In 2018 my husband found work in Switzerland, and a year later we followed him. I spent two years learning French, caring for our children, and preparing the admission exam for EPFL, which I passed on the second try, at thirty-four.

Studying architecture at EPFL with two small children has been the hardest thing I have ever done. There were nights in the studio when I was the oldest person in the room by fifteen years. But I have completed 23 courses, with a GPA of 3.18, and my work on low-cost housing was selected for the semester exhibition.

My diploma project is a design for flood-resilient housing for fishing communities on the north coast of Java, communities like the one I come from. Every year, the sea takes a little more of their land.

After graduating, I want to work in an office that designs social housing, and one day to open a small practice in Indonesia that builds for people who cannot afford architects.

I think of the young architect on that construction site often. I would like, one day, to be that person for someone else.
