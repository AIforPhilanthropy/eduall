## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

How do you study to become a doctor at forty-five while raising a child alone? One day at a time, and with a very careful budget.

I am in the first year of the Joint Medical Master in St. Gallen. My costs are CHF 1,996 a month, and my room share is CHF 616. I work as a nursing assistant in a care home on weekends and earn CHF 1,169. My cousin in Amman, a teacher whose household lives on about CHF 990, sends me CHF 170. I also have a scholarship of CHF 5,985 a year from a medical foundation.

What these numbers do not show is my daughter. She is twelve. Her costs are not in my budget, but every franc I earn is shared with her. From October, my final clinical rotations begin: full days, sometimes nights, in the hospital. I will have to give up most of my weekend shifts, and with them most of my income.

I want to finish. I am so close. A grant would let me finish without my daughter paying for it.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

What makes a nurse decide, at thirty-eight, to become a doctor? For me it was a night in Gaza in 2014, in the hospital where I had worked for eleven years. The electricity failed during an operation. The surgeon kept working by the light of our phones. We saved the patient. Afterwards the surgeon said to me: "You should have been a doctor." I laughed. But I never forgot.

In 2015 I came to Switzerland with my daughter, then a baby. My marriage ended soon after. For years I worked as a nursing assistant and learned German at night. My Palestinian nursing diploma was not enough, so I started again: bachelor of medicine in Zurich, and now the master in St. Gallen.

My daughter does her homework next to me every evening. She says she will be a doctor too. I tell her: first sleep.

After my studies I want to work in emergency medicine.
