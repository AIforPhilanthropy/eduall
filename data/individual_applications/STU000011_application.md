## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

I should say clearly at the outset that my monthly budget is almost balanced today, and that I have some resources that many applicants will not. I want the committee to see the full picture.

I study Business Administration part-time and work about 13 hours a week as a bookkeeping assistant for a small accounting office, earning CHF 1,452 a month. My spouse, who works as a hotel receptionist in Zurich and earns about CHF 3,700 a month, contributes CHF 310 towards my personal costs; the rest of the household income goes to our five-year-old child and the flat. I also receive a scholarship of CHF 4,444 a year from a Moroccan diaspora association. My own monthly expenses are CHF 2,249, which leaves a gap of CHF 487 before the scholarship. We also have savings of CHF 11,923, built up over several years before I returned to studying.

Why apply, then? Because at this pace my degree would take until July 2030. From September 2027 I want to study full-time and keep only about five hours a week of bookkeeping, which means my income would fall to around CHF 560 and my gap would grow to CHF 1,379 a month before the scholarship. The savings are our family's only reserve, set aside for our child, and my scholarship is renewed only year by year.

An EduAll grant would allow me to switch to full-time and finish in July 2029, a year earlier, without touching the money we have set aside. If the committee decides my need is less urgent than others', I will understand.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

In fifteen years, I want to run a small consultancy that helps immigrant-owned businesses in Switzerland grow beyond survival. Kebab shops, cleaning companies, hair salons, grocery stores: they are everywhere in Zurich, and most of them are one bad year away from closing, not because the owners are not capable, but because nobody has ever explained VAT, cash flow or credit to them in a language they understand.

I know this because my family lived it. I grew up in Fès, where my father ran a small leather workshop that was always one missing payment from ruin. As a teenager I kept the accounts in a school notebook. When I came to Switzerland in 2016 to join my spouse, I spent my first years cleaning offices at night, then working in a laundry, while learning German from free courses at the library.

In 2020 our child was born, very prematurely. We spent three months in the neonatal unit of the Triemli hospital. It was the most frightening period of my life, and it was also when I decided that I would not spend the rest of it in jobs that left no room for a future. When our child started kindergarten, I began preparing for the university entrance exam, and I passed on my first attempt.

I have now completed three semesters in Business Administration at the University of Zurich, with a GPA of 3.01, while working and raising a child. My bookkeeping job has become the practical side of my degree: I now handle the monthly accounts of fourteen small clients, most of them immigrants like me.

For me, education is how a family moves from surviving to planning. I want to earn this degree, and then help others make the same move.
