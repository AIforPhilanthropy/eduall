## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

I am a doctoral student in Chemistry at the University of Bern, in my second year. My doctorate is supposed to be funded by a Nigerian federal research scholarship. In practice, the scholarship has been paid only partly and irregularly: this year I received the equivalent of CHF 3,060 for the whole year. My supervisor has confirmed in writing that the group cannot employ me before a new project starts in 2028.

My student B permit expired in June. I applied for renewal on time, but the migration office asks me to prove around CHF 2,600 a month of resources, and I cannot. My renewal is therefore still pending. Because I applied on time, I may continue my teaching assistant work while it is processed, which brings CHF 587 a month. My older brother, a clerk in Ibadan with a household income of around CHF 560, sends me CHF 120.

My monthly costs are CHF 2,353. The largest item is my studio, CHF 1,147 plus CHF 104 for utilities. The monthly gap is CHF 1,646.

A grant from EduAll would count as proof of means for my permit and would allow me to continue my research until funding is secured.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

Chemistry matters because in the Niger Delta, where I grew up, the water and soil are poisoned by oil. I grew up in Warri, where gas flares burn day and night, and where my mother's fish from the creeks started to taste of petrol. I wanted to understand how to clean what had been destroyed.

My parents are both secondary school teachers. When I was fourteen, militants attacked our neighbourhood during the oil conflict, and we lived for almost a year with relatives in Benin City. I missed a full year of school and caught up by studying at night by candlelight.

I studied industrial chemistry in Benin City and worked four years as a laboratory analyst for an environmental testing company, measuring hydrocarbons in soil and water. I was elected president of the young chemists' association of our state, and organised community workshops about water safety in oil-affected villages.

My doctoral research is on materials that can absorb oil from water, and it has already produced a first publication. I want to return to the Niger Delta and apply this work where it is most needed.
