Every month, my expenses exceed my regular support by CHF 1,924. That figure is the heart of my application.

I am in the second year of the Master's in Biochemistry at the University of Zurich. My costs are CHF 2,389 a month, most of it rent: CHF 1,263 for a studio. My father, a pharmacist's assistant in Kumasi whose household earns around CHF 960, sends me CHF 140, and I earn CHF 325 as a tutor. I also receive a scholarship of CHF 7,463 a year from a foundation for African scientists, which covers part of the gap.

My thesis requires full days in the laboratory until July 2028. A grant would allow me to finish without reducing my laboratory time.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

I grew up in Kumasi, the son of a market woman and a pharmacist's assistant who never went to university. When I was twelve, my mother died of sickle-cell complications. I promised myself I would understand the disease that took her.

At KNUST I studied biochemistry, graduated at the top of my class, and led the student science society. I worked as a research assistant on sickle-cell disease, which led to my first publication. I also founded a mentoring programme for students from poor neighbourhoods of Kumasi.

At UZH I have a GPA of 4.0. My thesis explores gene therapy approaches for sickle-cell disease.

I want to do a PhD and later build a research programme on sickle-cell disease in West Africa, where most patients still die young.
