## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

For six years I worked in field teams for Egypt's water research centre, measuring salinity in the Nile Delta, often in villages where the fields had turned white with salt. I came to Basel to do the doctorate I had dreamed of all those years, on the Delta's future under rising seas.

My doctorate began in September 2024 with a mission scholarship from the Egyptian government. In spring 2026, because of the currency crisis, the government suspended payments to many scholars abroad, including me. My supervisor has written to confirm that there is no salaried position in the group until a new project starts in 2028.

My monthly costs are CHF 2,339. They include CHF 1,209 in rent plus utilities, CHF 345 for health insurance, and CHF 53 for sampling materials my fieldwork requires. My older brother, an engineer in Riyadh whose household earns around CHF 3,650, now sends me CHF 980. The gap is CHF 1,359.

And in May, our mother suffered a severe stroke in Mansoura. My brother is paying for her care too. I cannot keep asking him to carry both of us. A grant from EduAll would let me continue my research until my funding is restored, and let my brother give our mother what she needs.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

I grew up in Kafr El-Sheikh, in the northern Delta, where the land meets the sea. My father was an agricultural engineer who taught me to taste the soil. "Sweet soil grows rice," he said, "salt soil grows nothing." When I was a boy, the sweet soil reached almost to the coast. Today, in many villages, it does not.

I studied soil science in Alexandria and joined the national water research centre, where I became head of a field team of nine technicians, responsible for monitoring groundwater and soil salinity across three governorates. I trained dozens of young technicians, and I learned that data only matters when farmers trust the people collecting it.

My doctoral research in Basel models how sea-level rise and upstream dams will change salinity in the Delta by 2050. It is the question I have lived with all my life.

My mother used to pack food for me every time I went to the field, even when I was thirty. She still asks me on the phone, slowly now, whether I have eaten.

After my doctorate I want to return to Egypt and help the Delta's farmers prepare for what is coming. Forty million people depend on that land.
