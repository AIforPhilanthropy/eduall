## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

My parents send me CHF 540 every month, and I receive a scholarship of CHF 2,229 a year. My costs are CHF 2,219. Those are the numbers. But numbers are the easy part of my story.

My parents run a sunflower farm near Kherson. They never went to university; they built the farm from a few hectares after the Soviet collapse, and in good years it did well, with a household income of around CHF 3,070. Since 2022, half of our fields have been mined or occupied. My father still farms the part he can reach. Every harvest could be the last, and every month the money they send me comes from fields my father crosses with a metal detector first.

I do not know how long they can keep sending it. I have asked them to stop, many times. They say that if they cannot save the farm, they can at least save my future.

I am applying so that my future does not depend on those fields. My master's in Data Science ends in July 2028.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

I was eighteen when the occupation of our village began. For eight months we lived under it. I finished my first year of university online, from my bedroom, with the curtains closed, afraid of every car that stopped outside. When the village was liberated, we found the school burned and the library destroyed.

During those months, and after, I volunteered with a group of young people who delivered food and medicine to elderly neighbours who could not leave their homes. When the village was free, I was elected to coordinate the youth council's rebuilding of the school library, which we reopened with two thousand donated books.

I completed my bachelor's in computer science in Kyiv and came to Lugano in 2026. My master's is in data science, and I want to use it for something very concrete: mapping mined agricultural land in Ukraine from satellite data, so that farmers like my father know where it is safe to walk.
