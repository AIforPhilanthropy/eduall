## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

Every month, CHF 1,865 is missing. That is the short version of my situation.

My costs in Lausanne are CHF 2,215, most of it rent: CHF 1,301 for a studio in Renens. I have looked for a cheaper room for two years, but my placement hospitals change every few months, and most shared flats want tenants who stay put. My health insurance costs CHF 210.

My mother, a secondary school teacher in Lima whose household lives on about CHF 1,960, sends me CHF 350 every month. She has done this since my first semester. Until this summer I also worked as a care assistant in a nursing home, but my final clinical placement starts in August, full-time with shifts, so I have had to stop.

In March, a huaico, a mudslide, came down the hill in Chosica and buried half of my mother's house. Nobody was hurt, thank God, but everything on the ground floor was lost. My mother is paying for the repairs on credit. She has not reduced what she sends me yet, but she told me she does not know how long she can continue.

I have four semesters left. I finish in July 2028 and I already have an interview for a nursing position in Morges. I only need to get to the end.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

When I was fifteen, the bus I was travelling in with my cousin fell into a ravine near Huancayo. Nine people died, including my cousin. I spent five weeks in the hospital with a broken pelvis. The doctors were busy and rushed. The nurses were the ones who sat with me when I cried at night, who explained things, who remembered my name.

I decided then what I would become. It took a long time. My mother raised me and my brother alone after my father left, on her teacher's salary. After school I started nursing in Lima while working in a pharmacy, but I had to stop after one year when money ran out. For almost two years I worked as a nursing assistant in a private clinic, saving every sol I could.

In 2024, I came to Lausanne to study nursing again, from the beginning, in French. It was very hard, but it has also been the best time of my life. My GPA is 3.58. My supervisors say I am calm under pressure. I think it is because I remember what it is like to be the frightened one in the bed.

I want to work in trauma and emergency care. And someday I want to go back to the Andes, where a bus accident still means hours of waiting for help, and where a good nurse can be the difference between life and death.
