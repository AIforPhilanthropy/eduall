## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

I am applying to EduAll for support during the last four semesters of my Bachelor's in Medicine at the University of Zurich, which I will complete in July 2028 before continuing to the master's programme.

My monthly expenses total CHF 2,213. The largest item is rent, CHF 1,197 for a room in a shared flat in Oerlikon, followed by food at CHF 328 and tuition and semester fees at CHF 203. The remainder covers health insurance, transport, phone and study materials.

My only regular income is CHF 495 a month from my elder brother, who works as a delivery driver in Zurich and earns around CHF 3,900. He shares a small flat with two colleagues and sends me more than he can comfortably spare. This leaves a monthly shortfall of CHF 1,718.

Until last year I covered this gap by working evenings in a restaurant kitchen and on weekend cleaning shifts. From this semester onwards, however, my timetable includes clinical skills training, bedside teaching and preparation for the bachelor's examinations, which make regular paid work impossible without jeopardising my studies. I stopped working in the spring and have been covering my costs from the last of my earnings, which are now exhausted.

My housing is also insecure. My current sublet ends in December, and without a stable income I cannot sign a lease of my own. An EduAll grant would allow me to secure a room for the coming year and to concentrate fully on the examinations that will determine whether I can continue into the clinical master's programme.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

When I was nine, my grandmother fell ill in our village near Barisal. The nearest doctor was across the river, and the ferry did not run in the monsoon. My father carried her to the landing twice, and twice we turned back. I remember sitting beside her mat that night, fanning her, not understanding why no one could come. She recovered, slowly, but I never forgot the feeling of a problem that had a solution just out of reach.

My parents farm rice on a small plot of land, and neither of them completed primary school. I was the first in my family to finish secondary school, and the first to attend university. I came to Switzerland in 2019, at eighteen, and have lived here under provisional admission since then. My asylum application was rejected, but I cannot be sent back: my family belongs to the Urdu-speaking Bihari minority, and the Bangladeshi authorities have refused to issue me a passport. For my first years I learned German in intensive courses while working in restaurant kitchens, and in February 2024 I began my medical studies in Zurich.

The past two and a half years have tested me academically and practically. I have moved house five times, twice at very short notice, and for several weeks in 2024 I slept on friends' floors while searching for a room. Through all of this I have completed twenty-two courses and have not interrupted my studies once.

Since 2023 I have been part of a health information project for newly arrived Bengali-speaking families, helping them understand how to register with a doctor, use the emergency services and apply for health insurance subsidies. Many of them are where I was a few years ago.

I intend to specialise in general internal medicine and, in the long term, to work in rural primary care. For me, education is what stands between a problem and its solution, and I hope to become the doctor who crosses the river.
