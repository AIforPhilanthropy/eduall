## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

I will be honest from the first line: I do not need money for rent or food.

I came to Neuchâtel in 2024 on a student permit, for a year of French and bridging courses in mathematics before the master. During that year the insurance company where I tutored staff in statistics offered me a 60% contract as a data assistant, about 24 hours a week. I accepted, my permit was changed to a work permit, and in 2025 I began the master part-time. I earn CHF 2,650. My partner, a doctor at the hospital in Neuchâtel, earns around CHF 9,500 a month and pays CHF 300 for my share. My costs are CHF 1,852.

So why am I writing? Because in April my younger brother, who lives in Timișoara, was diagnosed with schizophrenia after a severe crisis. He needs private treatment and a supported living place, which the Romanian system does not provide in time. My parents are pensioners. My partner and I are paying. I want to use the money I earn for him. I cannot reduce my contract for my thesis year, because my permit depends on it, so the thesis will have to fit into evenings and weekends until January 2029. A grant would let me buy some of that time back, for example by taking unpaid days for my thesis.

I understand if you decide that my case does not fit. Many applicants need help for food and rent. They should come first.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

The first time I sat in the mathematics library in Neuchâtel, with the lake behind the window, I thought of my brother. When we were children in Timișoara, he was the one who loved numbers. I was the one who followed him. He taught me prime numbers with buttons.

I left school at seventeen, after our father's accident at the factory, to work. For many years I worked in shops and warehouses, and I volunteered with an organisation supporting people with mental illness, perhaps because I already saw something in my brother that nobody wanted to name. I also helped organise a community garden in our neighbourhood.

At thirty-three I finally went to university in Timișoara, and at thirty-eight I came to Neuchâtel, with the master as my goal. My first paper, on graph theory, was published this year.

I want to teach mathematics, and to work on mental health support for students.
