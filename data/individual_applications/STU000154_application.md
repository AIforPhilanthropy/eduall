## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

Every morning I take the tram past the Basel Zoo to the Biozentrum, and every morning I think of how improbable it is that I am here.

I study the Master's in Microbiology at the University of Basel, and I will finish in January 2028. My costs are CHF 2,229 a month. My rent is CHF 1,217, and health insurance CHF 260. I work as a laboratory technician twelve hours a week, earning CHF 1,149, and my older brother, an electrician in Bielefeld whose household earns about CHF 5,000, sends me CHF 1,260. He has done this since the day I arrived.

But my brother has just learned that our youngest sister, who was taken by ISIS in 2014 and has been missing ever since, may be alive in a camp in north-eastern Syria. He is spending everything on lawyers, intermediaries and travel to bring her home. I told him to stop sending me money. He refused. I cannot let him choose between us.

A grant from EduAll would let me release my brother from carrying me, so that all his strength can go to finding our sister.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

In August 2014, when I was eighteen, ISIS came to Sinjar. I am Yazidi. My family fled up the mountain with thousands of others. We had no water. I saw children die of thirst. My youngest sister was taken on the road. We never saw her again.

After two years in a camp in Kurdistan, I came to Switzerland in 2016 as a refugee. I had just finished secondary school and wanted to study biology in Mosul. I started from zero: German, bridging courses, a bachelor's in biology in Basel. I worked in laboratories, cleaning glassware at first, then as a technician. At the Biozentrum I now train new technicians, and I led the organisation of our laboratory's move to the new building last year.

In the camp, I saw cholera spread through the tents. Microbiology became my way to fight something I could finally understand. My master's thesis is on waterborne pathogens in refugee camps.

I want to work in outbreak response for humanitarian organisations. I have seen what happens when there is no clean water. I want to be the one who brings it.
