## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

For eleven years I worked as a mountain guide assistant in the Atlas, carrying bags, cooking for trekking groups, and later leading groups of tourists up Toubkal. I learned every rock on the path. I did not know their names.

I want to be honest: today I do not have a monthly deficit. I study part-time and work as a cleaner in a hospital, about 18 hours a week in the evenings, and earn CHF 2,028. My husband, a mechanic in Bern with an income of about CHF 5,000, contributes CHF 370 to my costs. I also received a scholarship of CHF 7,181 a year from a foundation for adult students. My costs are CHF 2,134.

And I must be honest about my studies too: my first semester was very bad. My GPA is 2.06. I had not studied mathematics or chemistry for twenty years. I failed two exams.

I apply because I want to reduce my evening work, to have the time to catch up and pass my retakes. My husband's salary also supports his parents in Marrakech, and we cannot afford for me to fail and lose a year. My bachelor ends in January 2032.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

I grew up in a Berber village in the High Atlas. My parents were shepherds; my mother could not read. But my father had studied at a teachers' college in Marrakech before he returned to the land, and he taught me to read with old newspapers. At twelve I left school to work.

The mountains were my school. As a guide assistant, I saw floods destroy villages and landslides close roads. After the earthquake of 2023, I went back to my village and spent three months helping rebuild with the association of our valley, which I have been part of for many years. I saw that houses built on the wrong ground fell, and others stood.

I came to Switzerland in 2014 after my marriage. At thirty-seven, I began to study geology, to learn the names of the rocks I walked on all my life, and why the ground moves.

I want to work on landslide and earthquake risk in mountain villages, in Morocco and in the Alps.
