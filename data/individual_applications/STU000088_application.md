## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

I am in the first year of my Master's in Physics at the University of Fribourg, and I will finish in July 2028.

My monthly costs are CHF 1,882. My room in a shared flat near Pérolles costs CHF 885, and my health insurance CHF 285. My only support is CHF 190 each month from my older sister in Dakar, who sells fabrics at the Sandaga market and whose household lives on about CHF 1,120. She gives me about a sixth of everything she earns. Every month when the money arrives, I feel both grateful and ashamed.

I do not have a job. The master's courses and laboratory work fill my days, and my French, although good, is not enough for the few student jobs that are available in Fribourg, where most employers want German as well.

This leaves CHF 1,692 missing every month. I have nothing saved. Until now I have survived with small loans from friends in our community, which I will repay the day I earn a salary.

With a grant from EduAll, I could stop counting coins before buying bread, and give all my strength to my thesis on solar cells. I have come a very long way to reach this classroom. I would like to be allowed to finish.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

In ten years, I want to lead a small laboratory in Dakar that designs cheap solar panels for villages. I want the children of those villages to do their homework under electric light, and not under a streetlamp, as I did.

When I was seven, my father, a poor farmer in the region of Kaolack, sent me to a Quranic school in Dakar. I was a talibé. Like thousands of children in Senegal, I spent my days begging in the streets for my marabout, barefoot, with a tomato can for coins. I did not go to a real school. At night, I sat under a streetlamp near the Place de l'Indépendance and copied letters from old newspapers.

At twelve, a social worker from an association found me. She took me to a shelter and enrolled me in school. I was the oldest in my class by five years. I will never forget her. Nobody in my family had ever gone to school. I finished my baccalaureate at twenty-one and studied physics at Cheikh Anta Diop University. The university was often closed by strikes, and it took me many years. But I finished.

Since then, I have given every free day to the same association that saved me. With other former talibés, we created a group that walks the streets of Dakar on Friday evenings, talking to children, bringing them food and telling them there is another way.

In Fribourg, I discovered the physics of solar cells, and I discovered that I am good at it.

For me, education is not a privilege. It is the streetlamp I read under, finally turned into real light.
