## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

For eleven years I have worked in care homes, and I still do: about 19 hours a week, three evenings and one weekend day, as a care assistant in a home for elderly people in Köniz. I earn CHF 2,151. This is why I study nursing part-time. My partner, who works as a bus driver in Bern and earns about CHF 5,300, gives CHF 330 for my share of our life. Together this covers my costs of CHF 1,690.

So why do I write to you? Because my body is telling me I cannot continue like this. I have a chronic back condition, two herniated discs, from years of lifting residents. The care home work that pays my share is exactly the work that damages me. My doctor has told me I must reduce the physical work, and from September 2027 I will drop one evening, so I will earn about CHF 1,510. And in June my mother in Tangier was diagnosed with cancer; my partner and I now send money for her treatment.

I began my bachelor in nursing in February 2026, at thirty-nine. It is a long road until January 2032. I want to become a nurse so that I can care for people in a way that does not destroy me, and with knowledge that I did not have for eleven years.

A grant would let me reduce my hours in the care home before my back decides for me.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

There is an old man in the care home, Herr B., who was a carpenter. He does not speak much anymore. But every time I come into his room, he takes my hand and pats it three times. Nobody knows why. I have decided it means "thank you, see you tomorrow."

I grew up in Tangier. My father was a teacher, my mother a seamstress. I came to Switzerland at twenty-eight, and I started in care homes because it was the work I could find. I discovered that I love it. I love the old people, their stories, their stubbornness, their fear and their jokes.

But after eleven years I also know the limits of a care assistant. I see the nurses make decisions I do not understand, and I want to understand. I want to recognise when a resident's confusion is an infection and not "just age".

In Bern I am also active in the Moroccan association, where I organise information evenings about care for elderly immigrants, who often end their lives very alone.

My first semester was hard. After twenty years away from school, I had to learn how to study again, in German. But I will learn, as I learned everything else.
