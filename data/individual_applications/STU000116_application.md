## Section 4 — Financial Need Statement

*Instructions: Describe your financial situation and explain why you need EduAll support to pursue your studies in Switzerland. Address why paying for your education is difficult, which costs are most challenging for you, what would happen to your studies without support, and whether you have exhausted other funding options (300–400 words).*

I am in the first year of my Master's in Physics at the University of Geneva, and I am applying to EduAll because my housing and my finances have both become fragile at the same time.

My monthly expenses are CHF 1,788, including CHF 846 for a room and CHF 300 for health insurance. I earn CHF 223 a month tutoring secondary school students in mathematics. My younger brother, a school teacher in Hyderabad (household income around CHF 510), sends me CHF 100. It is a fifth of his income, and he does it without complaint.

My room is a sublet in Carouge. The main tenant has told me that she is returning in November, and I have not yet found another place I can afford. Most listings ask for a Swiss guarantor or three months' deposit.

The monthly gap is CHF 1,465. My master's programme includes an experimental project that requires long laboratory hours, which limits how much I can work. A grant from EduAll would secure a stable room and allow me to complete my degree by January 2028.

## Section 5 — Personal Statement

*Instructions: Tell us who you are and where you come from, describe your educational goals and aspirations, share any personal challenges or circumstances you wish to (optional), and explain what education means to you (max. 500 words).*

My mother taught physics in a girls' school in Hyderabad, Sindh, for thirty years, with a single set of lab equipment she had bought with her own savings. She would carry a box of batteries, wires and lenses from classroom to classroom. When I was a child, I carried it with her.

She died when I was twenty-two. I took over her classes at the same school, and I became head of the science department at twenty-seven, leading a team of nine teachers. Over those years I trained young teachers, rebuilt the laboratory after it was damaged in the 2022 floods, and ran a small physics club that sent students to national competitions.

The floods of 2022 were the hardest period of my life. Our house and the school were under water for weeks. We lost almost everything. I spent months with colleagues cleaning, rebuilding and teaching in tents. It was then that I decided to study again, to deepen my own understanding of physics and come back able to teach it at a higher level.

In Geneva, I study condensed matter physics. My goal is to return to Sindh and help develop physics teaching at the university level, where there is a severe lack of trained staff.

My mother's box of equipment is still in my brother's house. One day I would like to replace it with a real laboratory.
